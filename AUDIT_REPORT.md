# AUDIT_REPORT.md — Trading-Academy-Pro Pre-Publication Review

**Reviewer role:** Lead Reviewer (independent of authorship)
**Audit date:** 2026-08-05
**Scope:** Full repository — 40 modules, 373 files under `docs/`, plus root governance documents (`MASTER_SKILL.md`, `CURRICULUM.md`, `ROADMAP.md`, `CONTRIBUTING.md`, `README.md`, `TODO.md`, `CHANGELOG.md`)
**Files modified by this audit:** None. This report is the only file created, as instructed.

## Methodology

This audit combined two independent passes, cross-validated against each other:

1. **Automated mechanical audit** (lead reviewer, via script): template-section compliance across all 270 core lessons, cross-reference (`[XX.YY]`) validity, module-level and lesson-level prerequisite-ordering and cycle detection, numeric historical-fact consistency across every citation of the same event, duplicate-file/duplicate-lesson-number detection, near-duplicate-sentence detection (shingling), file-size compliance (~2,000-line cap), hype/absolute-language scan, and index-title-vs-H1-title consistency.
2. **Seven parallel deep-read content reviews** (independent sub-agents, no shared context with each other or with lesson authorship), each assigned a module cluster, instructed to read every lesson in full, recompute every formula, verify every checkable historical/biographical claim, check terminology against `docs/33_Glossary/`, and assess pedagogical quality against `MASTER_SKILL.md`'s explicit standards.

Where a sub-agent flagged a factual claim as wrong, the lead reviewer independently corroborated the two highest-stakes claims (Citadel Securities' founding year; the 31.06 title/content mismatch) against grep-verified source text before inclusion below. All findings are anchored to exact file paths and section names.

## Executive Summary

Trading-Academy-Pro is a substantially strong, close-to-publication-ready curriculum. The mechanical audit found **zero broken cross-references** (270 valid lesson IDs checked against every `[XX.YY]` citation across 373 files), **zero broken index links, zero duplicate lesson files, zero circular dependencies at the module level** (verified by graph cycle detection across all 31 core modules), and **only two incidental sentence-level duplications** in the entire ~80,000-line corpus. Every formula independently recomputed by the seven reviewers — options payoffs, all five Greeks' sign conventions, futures leverage/hedge ratios, expectancy, drawdown recovery, position sizing, Sharpe/Sortino, CAPM, DCF/DDM, PEG, ROE/ROA/ROCE — checked out correct, with exactly **one exception** (a Kelly Criterion worked example, detailed below). Historical facts were overwhelmingly accurate: of dozens of checkable dates, figures, and named individuals across the two most fact-dense clusters alone (Modules 20–23 and 29–31, 62 lessons), only **two clear factual errors** and **one overstated causal claim** were found.

The issues that do exist cluster into a small number of repeatable, addressable patterns:

1. **A systemic Glossary (Module 33) coverage gap** — independently flagged by *four of the seven* reviewer teams without prompting each other — concentrated exactly where students need it most (unfamiliar jargon in Modules 10–19) and extending into Modules 01–09's foundational vocabulary. This is the single most consequential, most-corroborated finding in this audit.
2. **One real content/title mismatch** (lesson 31.06) tracing to a curriculum-planning-stage error, which the lesson author correctly avoided fabricating around but never resolved at the source.
3. **A handful of specific, correctable factual/numerical errors** (Volmageddon's VIX magnitude, a Kelly Criterion worked example, Citadel Securities' founding year, a SEBI Act causal-timeline overstatement).
4. **Three genuine missing topics** at the curriculum-planning level (taxation, practical account-opening, dedicated brokerage-cost treatment) that no amount of lesson-level editing can fix — these require new content.
5. **One repository-wide cosmetic issue**: 269 of 270 lessons' "Next Lesson" footers still read "(to be generated)" for lessons that have long since been generated.

**No Critical-severity issues were found.** Several High-severity items should be resolved before publication, primarily the Glossary gap and the 31.06 mismatch, both of which are corrective rather than exploratory work.

## Part 1 — Cross-Cutting Findings (Repository-Wide)

These findings span multiple modules or the repository structure itself, rather than a single lesson.

### AUDIT-001 — [High] Systemic Glossary (Module 33) coverage gap, independently identified across four separate module-cluster reviews
- **Location:** `docs/33_Glossary/Glossary_A-D.md`, `Glossary_E-L.md`, `Glossary_M-R.md`, `Glossary_S-Z.md`
- **Description:** `MASTER_SKILL.md` Rule 12 requires every lesson to "define every term the first time it is used... link to the `docs/33_Glossary/` entry," and Rule 15 requires terminology consistency anchored to the glossary. In practice, **zero lessons across the entire 270-lesson corpus contain an actual hyperlink into the glossary**, and the glossary itself covers only a small fraction of the terminology the lessons actually teach and quiz on. This was independently flagged, without cross-agent prompting, by reviewers covering Modules 01–04, 05–09, 10–13, and 14–19. Missing terms by cluster:
  - **Modules 01–04:** CHOCH/Change of Character, Fractal, Swing Point, Support, Resistance, DII, Free-Float Market Cap, GIFT Nifty/SGX Nifty, T+1.
  - **Modules 05–09:** Inflation, Repo Rate/Federal Funds Rate, CPI, Balance Sheet, Income Statement, Cash Flow Statement, Free Cash Flow, P/E Ratio, ROE/ROA/ROCE, EBITDA, DCF, WACC, Dividend Discount Model, EPS, Enterprise Value, Quantitative Easing, Current Ratio, Debt-to-Equity, Revenue Recognition, Competitive Moat, Balance of Payments.
  - **Modules 10–13:** Every named candlestick pattern (Doji, Hammer, Shooting Star, Marubozu, Spinning Top, Engulfing, Harami, Piercing Line, Dark Cloud Cover, Morning/Evening Star, Three White Soldiers/Black Crows), every named chart pattern (Head and Shoulders, Double/Triple Top/Bottom, Triangle, Flag, Pennant, Cup and Handle, Wedge, Rounding Top/Bottom, Broadening Formation), Fibonacci, MACD, Divergence, Trendline/Channel, Supply/Demand Zone, Inside/Outside Bar, Fakeout.
  - **Modules 14–19:** Point of Control, Value Area (VAH/VAL), Auction Market Theory, TPO, Initial Balance, High/Low Volume Node, Composite Man, Spring, Upthrust, the Three Laws, Smart Money Concepts (as its own entry), Liquidity Pool, Mitigation, Inducement, Premium/Discount Zone, Kill Zone, Market Maker Model, Optimal Trade Entry, Power of Three, Judas Swing, Volume Spread Analysis, Open Interest, Climactic Volume.

  Additionally, **two existing glossary entries actively contradict what their source lessons teach**: the combined "Circuit Breaker / Circuit Filter" entry (`Glossary_A-D.md`) gives one generic definition, while `[03.05]` explicitly and repeatedly teaches these as two distinct mechanisms at different scales (a dedicated Learning Objective and Misconceptions entry both hinge on the distinction) — the glossary as written contradicts the lesson's core teaching point. Similarly, "Liquidity" (`Glossary_E-L.md`) only captures the everyday, volume-based sense, while `[02.06]` teaches a second structural sense (resting-order clusters) and explicitly flags the everyday-only definition as a misconception to correct.
- **Recommendation:** This is the single highest-priority remediation item in this audit. Add the ~70+ missing terms across the four glossary files, in the existing one-line-definition-plus-`[XX.YY]`-backlink format. Split the Circuit Breaker/Filter entry into two. Expand the Liquidity entry to cover both senses. Then retrofit inline glossary links into lesson text at first use (a separate, larger follow-up pass — consider tracking it explicitly in `TODO.md` rather than doing it silently, so it isn't mistaken for having already been done).
- **Suggested fix (examples):**
  ```
  **Point of Control (POC)** — The price level with the highest traded volume within a profile period. [16.02]
  **Circuit Filter** — A per-stock price band (e.g., ±5%/10%/20%) beyond which an individual security cannot trade on a given day. [03.05]
  **Circuit Breaker (Market-Wide)** — An index-level mechanism halting trading across the entire market when Nifty/Sensex moves a defined percentage from the previous close. [03.05]
  ```

### AUDIT-004 — [Medium] Three lessons cite forward (not-yet-taught) prerequisites
- **Location:** `docs/12_Chart_Patterns/12.09_Broadening_Formations.md` (cites `[23.01]`), `docs/14_Volume/14.04_Open_Interest_vs_Volume_in_Derivatives.md` (cites `[20.01]`), `docs/24_Trading_Strategies/24.11_Combining_Strategies_into_a_Portfolio_of_Systems.md` (cites `[27.04]`)
- **Description:** Each lesson lists a prerequisite from a module that comes numerically *later* in the curriculum sequence. Each is self-aware of this (annotated "cross-reference; may be generated later" / "generated later" / "forward reference" inline), so this was a deliberate, acknowledged authoring choice, not an oversight — but it still means a student reading in order encounters a citation to unlearned material, a direct tension with `MASTER_SKILL.md` Rule 2 ("Never skip prerequisites"). The `[14.04]` case is structurally the most significant: Open Interest is fundamentally a derivatives-native concept, taught in the Volume module three modules before Futures (20) introduces derivatives contracts at all. Independently, the 10–13 cluster reviewer also flagged `[12.09]`'s citation as miscategorized (listed under "Prerequisites" when it functions as a forward-pointing related-link, not a true dependency).
- **Recommendation:** For `[12.09]` and `[24.11]`, move the forward citation out of "Prerequisites" into a "See also" note in the lesson body. For `[14.04]`, either add 2–3 self-contained sentences defining Open Interest without relying on `[20.01]`, or accept the forward reference but reclassify it the same way.
- **Suggested fix:** Change the Prerequisites-section bullet to a body-text note: *"This concept connects forward to `[20.01]` (Module 20: Futures), covered later in the curriculum — a full treatment of derivatives contracts isn't required to understand Open Interest at this introductory level, but revisit this lesson after Module 20 for the complete picture."*

### AUDIT-005 — [High] Lesson 31.06's title, filename, and generated content all disagree, and describe an event that does not appear to have occurred as titled
- **Location:** `docs/31_Case_Studies/31.06_May_2022_IndusInd_Bank_Adani_Group_Episodes.md`; `docs/31_Case_Studies/_Index.md` (row 31.06); `CURRICULUM.md` (Module 31, item 6)
- **Description:** `CURRICULUM.md` and `_Index.md` both title this lesson "May 2022 IndusInd Bank / Adani Group Episodes" (confirmed by direct grep of both files). The lesson's own H1 heading drops both "May 2022" and "IndusInd Bank," reading only "IndusInd Bank and Adani Group Episodes," and the entire lesson body covers only the Adani Group–Hindenburg Research episode, correctly dated to **January 24, 2023** — not May 2022. IndusInd Bank is never substantively discussed; the lesson includes only a hedge asking readers to independently verify "related banking-sector developments." There does not appear to have been a major IndusInd Bank market event in May 2022 — the real, well-documented IndusInd Bank derivatives-accounting disclosure occurred in **March 2025**, well outside "the same period" as the Adani report and after this content was authored. The original curriculum title appears to have been mistaken at the planning stage for both halves of its title. The lesson author's decision not to fabricate IndusInd Bank content was the correct call given the constraint, but the source-level title/filename error was never corrected, meaning the module currently delivers 9 genuinely distinct case studies plus one redundant restatement, not the 10 distinct events implied by `CURRICULUM.md`.
- **Recommendation:** Fix at the source. Either (a) retitle the lesson to "Adani Group–Hindenburg Episode (January 2023)" across `CURRICULUM.md`, `_Index.md`, and the file's own H1 and filename, dropping the IndusInd Bank framing entirely, or (b) keep the dual-topic structure but add a properly dated, appropriately hedged section on the actual IndusInd Bank episode (2025) as a clearly-labeled later addition outside the module's originally planned scope.
- **Suggested fix:** Rename file to `31.06_Adani_Group_Hindenburg_Episode_2023.md`; update H1 to `# [31.06] The Adani Group–Hindenburg Research Episode (January 2023)`; update `_Index.md`'s table row and `CURRICULUM.md`'s item 6 to match.

### AUDIT-006 — [Low] Cross-cutting stylistic uniformity across most lessons

### AUDIT-007 — [Medium] Missing topic: Taxation (STCG/LTCG, STT, F&O tax treatment)
- **Location:** Curriculum-wide — confirmed via `grep -in "tax" CURRICULUM.md` returning zero results
- **Description:** No module or lesson anywhere in the 40-module curriculum provides dedicated coverage of capital gains taxation (short-term/long-term), Securities Transaction Tax (STT), or F&O-specific tax treatment (e.g., business income classification for derivatives trading) — despite this being one of the most practically important topics for any Indian retail trader the academy is training, and despite `MASTER_SKILL.md`'s stated goal of professional-trader-level readiness. "Tax" appears only in passing within 39 files (e.g., a single STT mention in `[03.08]`), never as a dedicated topic.
- **Recommendation:** Add a dedicated lesson (or short module) covering Indian capital gains tax treatment for equity/F&O, STT, and how taxation should factor into strategy selection and position sizing (e.g., after-tax expectancy). The most natural home is either a new lesson in Module 03 (Indian Market) or Module 25 (Risk Management), given taxation directly affects net returns and thus expectancy calculations.
- **Suggested fix:** New lesson, e.g., `[03.11] Taxation of Indian Market Participants — STCG, LTCG, STT, and F&O Tax Treatment`, cross-referenced from `[24.01]`'s expectancy formula (since taxes reduce realized expectancy) and `[39]`'s capstone projects.

### AUDIT-008 — [Low] Missing topic: practical account-opening / KYC / demat account setup
- **Location:** Curriculum-wide — confirmed via `grep -in "demat account|KYC|account opening" CURRICULUM.md docs/01_Foundation/_Index.md docs/03_Indian_Market/_Index.md` returning zero results
- **Description:** The academy explains what a demat account, broker, and depository *are* (`[01.09]`) but never walks through the practical process of actually opening a trading/demat account, completing KYC, or the basic mechanics of funding an account — a genuinely useful, low-complexity topic for a "zero-knowledge" beginner curriculum that currently has no home anywhere in the 40 modules.
- **Recommendation:** Add a short practical lesson, most naturally in Module 01 (Foundation) immediately after `[01.09]`, or as a Module 38 (Practice) drill.
- **Suggested fix:** New lesson or drill, e.g., `docs/38_Practice/Drill_09_Opening_a_Trading_Account.md`, walking through KYC documents, demat/trading account linkage, and first-funding steps at a generic, broker-agnostic level.

### AUDIT-009 — [Low] Missing topic: dedicated brokerage/total-cost-of-trading treatment
- **Location:** Curriculum-wide — confirmed via `grep -in "brokerage" CURRICULUM.md` returning zero results
- **Description:** Brokerage costs are mentioned in passing in scalping (`[24.07]`) and backtesting (`[29.03]`) lessons but never given dedicated treatment as "total cost of trading" (brokerage + STT + stamp duty + slippage combined), despite this directly affecting the breakeven win rate (`[25.04]`) and expectancy (`[24.01]`) formulas the academy teaches extensively.
- **Recommendation:** Fold into the same new lesson recommended in AUDIT-007 (taxation), since both are "cost of trading" topics that affect the same downstream formulas — a single "True Cost of Trading" lesson covering taxes, brokerage, STT, and slippage together would be more efficient than two separate additions.

### AUDIT-010 — [Medium] Missing macro cross-references in two locations discussing macro-driven mechanisms
- **Location:** `docs/23_Volatility/23.01–23.06` (all six lessons); `docs/31_Case_Studies/31.01_2008_Global_Financial_Crisis.md`, `31.02_2020_COVID_Crash_and_V_Shaped_Recovery.md`, `31.08_Long_Term_Capital_Management_Collapse_1998.md`
- **Description:** `MASTER_SKILL.md` Rule 10 requires no instrument to be taught in isolation from its macro regime. The Volatility module never explicitly connects IV/VIX regime behavior to Module 06's macro framework (rate cycles, QE/QT, global liquidity cycles) despite `[06.06]` Global Liquidity Cycles and Risk-On/Risk-Off being a near-direct match for `[23.05]`'s regime discussion. Separately, three Module 31 case studies discuss significant monetary-policy mechanics (GFC's "coordinated policy response," COVID's Fed rate cuts and QE, LTCM's "flight to quality") without ever cross-referencing `[06.01]`, `[06.03]`, or `[06.06]`, despite Rule 13's "cross-reference relentlessly" standard.
- **Recommendation:** Add explicit cross-references from `[23.05]` to `[06.06]`/`[06.02]`/`[06.03]`, and from `[31.01]`, `[31.02]`, `[31.08]` to the relevant Module 06 lessons tying their monetary-policy narrative back to where those mechanisms were formally taught.
