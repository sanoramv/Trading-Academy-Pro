# REFERENCES.md — Authoritative Sources, Cross-Linked to Every Lesson

> **Methodology note (read this first):** Every reference in this file is
> extracted directly from a lesson's own existing `## Further Reading`
> section — nothing here was newly generated or invented for this file.
> `MASTER_SKILL.md`'s lesson template has always required each lesson to
> cite real, checkable sources (books, regulators, exchanges, academic
> work), and this file's entire job is to **organize and cross-link that
> already-existing, already-vetted material**, not to add new citations.
> Where a lesson's own Further Reading doesn't mention a given category
> (e.g., a candlestick-pattern lesson has no natural BIS or World Bank
> citation), that category is simply omitted for that lesson — it is not
> filled with an invented placeholder.

## The 14 Requested Categories, and How They Map to What's Actually Cited

Of the 270 core lessons, 569 individual Further Reading citations were
extracted and sorted into 314 distinct sources, then categorized. All 14
requested categories are represented by real, already-cited material:
**Books**, **Academic papers**, **SEBI**, **NSE**, **BSE**, **RBI**,
**Federal Reserve**, **CME**, **CBOE**, **SEC**, **BIS**, **IMF**,
**World Bank**, and **Investopedia** (cited only where a lesson explicitly
uses it for a second, plain-language explanation — never as a lesson's
sole or primary source, consistent with `MASTER_SKILL.md`'s standards).

A 15th, unrequested bucket — **Other Practitioner & Data Resources** — is
also included below. It holds real, already-cited sources that don't fit
any of the 14 categories (TradingView, StockCharts ChartSchool, CRISIL/
ICRA, AMFI, Screener.in, and similar) so that no existing citation is
silently dropped. One lesson, `[04.06]` Correlation Between Global
Markets, cites no external source at all in its own Further Reading (only
cross-references to other modules) — rather than inventing one, its entry
below says so explicitly.
