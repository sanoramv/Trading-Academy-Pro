## Core Concepts

### Definition
**Stock screening** is the process of systematically filtering a large
universe of stocks down to a manageable shortlist using quantitative
criteria (ratios and metrics from this module), before applying deeper
qualitative analysis (moats, management, industry context) to the
shortlisted candidates. It is a **starting point for research**, not a
final investment decision-making tool — screening identifies candidates
worth deeper investigation, not conclusions ready to act on.

### Intuition
With thousands of publicly listed companies across Indian and US markets,
systematic screening is essential simply to narrow the universe to a
workable number of candidates for deeper analysis — but because screening
relies purely on quantitative criteria, it necessarily misses the
qualitative dimensions (`[08.07]`-`[08.09]`) that ultimately determine
genuine investment quality, making it the *first* step of a
comprehensive process, never the *only* step.

### Mental Model
```
FUNDAMENTAL SCREENING PROCESS (this module's synthesis)

STEP 1: QUANTITATIVE SCREEN (fast, mechanical, narrows universe)
  - Profitability: ROE, ROCE above a threshold ([08.03])
  - Liquidity/Solvency: reasonable Current Ratio, manageable
    Debt-to-Equity ([08.04])
  - Efficiency: reasonable Asset Turnover, CCC ([08.05])
  - Valuation: P/E, PEG within a reasonable range for the
    sector ([08.06])

STEP 2: QUALITATIVE DEEP-DIVE (slower, judgment-based, applied
         to the SHORTLIST from Step 1)
  - Moat assessment ([08.07])
  - Management/governance assessment ([08.08])
  - Industry structural assessment ([08.09])

STEP 3: VALUATION (Module 09) -- estimate intrinsic value and
         compare to current market price
```

## Real World Analogy
Stock screening is like using an online property search tool's filters
(price range, number of bedrooms, location) to narrow thousands of
listings down to a manageable shortlist worth actually visiting in
person — the filters do valuable, efficient work narrowing the search, but
no serious buyer would purchase a house based purely on the filtered
listing data without an in-person inspection (the qualitative deep-dive)
and a genuine assessment of whether the asking price reflects fair value
(valuation, Module 09).

## Indian Market Example
**Screener.in** is a widely-used free Indian stock screening tool allowing
investors to filter companies by exactly the ratio categories developed in
this module (ROE, ROCE, Debt-to-Equity, P/E, and many others), and even
supports custom, saved screening formulas — a practical, freely accessible
tool directly applying this lesson's framework to real Indian market data.

## US Market Example
**Finviz** and similar free US stock screening tools offer comparable
filtering capabilities for US-listed companies, allowing investors to
apply this module's quantitative criteria systematically across the much
larger US market universe, before proceeding to the qualitative deep-dive
this lesson emphasizes as an essential, non-optional second step.

## Historical Example
**Benjamin Graham's** original quantitative screening criteria (from
*Security Analysis*, `[08.01]`) — including specific thresholds for P/E,
price-to-book value, and debt levels — represent one of the earliest
systematic, rules-based stock screening approaches in fundamental
analysis history, an approach modernized and automated by today's free
screening tools but conceptually rooted directly in this nearly
century-old methodology.

## Institutional Perspective
Institutional quantitative research teams build considerably more
sophisticated, multi-factor screening models than the simplified checklist
in this lesson — incorporating dozens of quantitative factors
simultaneously, often back-tested against historical performance
(previewing Module 30's Quantitative Trading content) — but even these
sophisticated institutional screens are typically followed by fundamental
analyst deep-dives on flagged candidates, preserving this lesson's
two-step quantitative-then-qualitative structure at a more advanced scale.

## Retail Perspective
Retail investors sometimes stop at the screening step, treating a stock
that "passes the screen" as an investable conclusion rather than a
research starting point — this lesson's core message, that screening
narrows the universe but never substitutes for qualitative deep-dive
analysis, is precisely the gap between amateur and more disciplined
retail investment practice.

## Professional Insights
Professional analysts caution against **over-optimizing screening
criteria** to match historically successful investments in hindsight (a
form of overfitting, previewed for Module 29's algorithmic trading
content) — a screen with too many narrow, specifically-tuned criteria may
simply reflect what worked in the past without genuinely predicting future
success, favoring simpler, more robust, conceptually-grounded screening
criteria over excessively complex, backward-fitted formulas.

## Common Mistakes
- Treating a stock that passes quantitative screening criteria as a
  ready-to-act investment decision, skipping qualitative deep-dive
  analysis entirely.
- Using screening criteria without understanding why each specific
  threshold matters (this module's earlier lessons provide that
  understanding).
- Over-optimizing screening formulas to match past successful picks,
  risking overfitting rather than genuine predictive value.

## Misconceptions
- **"A stock passing all quantitative screening criteria is guaranteed to
  be a good investment."** Screening is explicitly the first step,
  narrowing the universe — qualitative deep-dive (`[08.07]`-`[08.09]`) and
  valuation (Module 09) remain essential before any investment decision.
- **"More screening criteria always produce better results."** As the
  Professional Insights section notes, excessively complex, narrowly-
  tuned screens risk overfitting to historical patterns rather than
  reflecting genuinely robust, forward-looking investment logic.
- **"Screening tools are only useful for professional analysts."** Free
  tools like Screener.in (India) and Finviz (US) make this entire
  module's framework directly, practically accessible to any retail
  investor.

## Key Takeaways
- Stock screening systematically narrows a large market universe using
  quantitative criteria — a starting point for research, never a final
  decision.
- A complete process combines quantitative screening (Step 1), qualitative
  deep-dive (Step 2), and valuation (Step 3, Module 09).
- Free tools (Screener.in for India, Finviz for US) make this module's
  entire framework practically accessible to any investor.
- Overfitting screening criteria to past successes risks poor forward-
  looking predictive value.

## Summary

toolkit (profitability, liquidity/solvency, efficiency, valuation metrics)
to systematically narrow a large market universe — provides an essential
starting point for fundamental research, but must always be followed by
the qualitative deep-dive (moat, management, industry analysis) this
