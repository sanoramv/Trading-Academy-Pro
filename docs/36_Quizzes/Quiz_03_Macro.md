## Section A — Recall
1. What are the four business cycle stages?

## Section B — Application
6. The yield curve inverts, but Nifty continues rising for six more months. Does this invalidate the yield curve's usefulness as a signal? Explain.
7. US markets fall sharply overnight on weak jobs data. Using `[04.03]`'s framework, what would you check before the Indian market opens?
8. A central bank cuts rates unexpectedly. Name two sectors that historically tend to benefit early in a rate-cutting cycle, and explain why.
9. Explain, using `[06.06]`'s framework, why a "risk-off" episode in the US can affect Indian equities even without any India-specific negative news.
10. Compare the market reactions typically expected from a scheduled Union Budget versus a surprise, unscheduled RBI rate move.

## Section C — Judgment
11. A trader claims macro analysis is "too slow and academic" to matter for short-term trading. Using this cluster's content, explain one way macro context still matters even for a trader with a multi-day holding period.
12. You see India VIX and US VIX both spiking simultaneously. What does this combination suggest that either one alone might not?

## Answer Key
1. Expansion, peak, contraction (recession), trough. `[05.08]`
2. Fiscal = government spending/taxation; monetary = central bank rates/money supply. `[05.07]`
3. A widely watched, though imperfect, recession indicator. `[06.07]`
4. Large-scale central bank asset purchases to inject liquidity and lower long-term rates. `[06.03]`
5. Gauging likely Nifty opening direction from overnight global cues. `[04.03]`
6-12. Open-ended: leading indicators can lead by variable, uncertain lengths of time (`[06.07]`); check GIFT Nifty and broader risk sentiment before assuming a mechanical reaction (`[04.03]`); Financials/Consumer Discretionary historically benefit early in rate cuts (`[27.06]`); global risk-off spreads via correlated capital flows regardless of local fundamentals (`[04.06]`, `[25.06]`); scheduled events are more likely to be partially priced in than surprises (`[24.10]`); macro context still shapes sector rotation and risk regime even for shorter holding periods (`[27.06]`, `[23.05]`); simultaneous VIX spikes suggest a genuinely global, correlated risk-off episode rather than a market-specific issue (`[04.06]`, `[25.06]`).
