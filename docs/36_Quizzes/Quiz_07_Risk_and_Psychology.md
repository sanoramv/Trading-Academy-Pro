## Answer Key
1. (Win Rate × Avg Win) − (Loss Rate × Avg Loss). `[24.01]`
2. Risking no more than 1% of capital on any single trade's stop-loss distance. `[25.02]`
3. Kelly % = W − [(1−W)/R]. `[26.04]`
4. The largest peak-to-trough equity decline observed in a track record. `[25.05]`
5. The failure to consistently execute a known rule under real, live pressure. `[28.01]`
6. An emotional disruption state, typically triggered by a loss, that impairs decision-making. `[28.04]`
7. Expectancy = (0.4×3) - (0.6×1) = 1.2 - 0.6 = +0.6 (positive, valid per `[24.01]`).
8. 35 / (1-0.35) ≈ 53.8%. `[25.01]`
9-18. Open-ended: correlated cluster risk exceeds naive independent-sum assumption (`[25.06]`); this is revenge trading, a symptom of tilt, typically compounding losses (`[28.04]`); a winning trade from a bad process is still a process failure ("lucky win," poor process cell) (`[28.07]`); expectancy requires SURVIVING enough trades for the average to materialize — oversized single losses can end that (`[25.01]`); a 3-loss streak is often normal variance, not evidence of strategy failure (`[24.12]`); professionals experience the same psychology but with structural support and practiced discipline (`[28.08]`); consistent sizing determines whether tested expectancy actually plays out (`[26.01]`); vague rules aren't checkable — a rulebook needs specific numbers (`[25.08]`); scaling in adds on CONFIRMATION; averaging down adds to a LOSER without confirmation (`[26.06]`); this violates stop-loss discipline (`[25.03]`), risk-per-trade consistency (`[25.02]`), and reflects loss aversion (`[28.03]`).
