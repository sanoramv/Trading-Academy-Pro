## Section A — Recall
1. What is the difference between SMA and EMA?
2. What does RSI measure?
3. What confirms a genuine breakout versus a likely false one?
4. What is Point of Control (POC)?
5. What is the difference between Market Profile and Volume Profile?

## Section B — Application
6. A stock breaks above resistance on volume 40% below its 20-day average. Should you treat this as a high-confidence breakout? Explain.
7. Price makes a new high, but RSI makes a lower high than its prior peak. What is this called, and what does it suggest?
8. You see a bullish engulfing candle form at a random point mid-range, with no nearby support level. Should you treat it the same as one forming exactly at a well-tested support zone?
9. A Head and Shoulders pattern completes, but volume on the "break of neckline" is unremarkable. What should you check before trusting the pattern?
10. Explain how POC and Value Area from Volume Profile could inform where you'd place a stop-loss on a mean-reversion trade.

## Section C — Judgment
11. A trader uses seven different indicators simultaneously and only trades when all seven agree. What risk does `[10.11]`'s multi-indicator confluence lesson identify with this approach?
12. Explain why "location matters more than shape" for candlestick pattern reliability, using a specific example.
13. You identify what looks like a clean cup-and-handle pattern, but it formed during an earnings-driven single-day spike rather than organic price action. Should your confidence level differ from a pattern that formed gradually? Why?

## Answer Key
1. SMA weights all periods equally; EMA weights recent prices more heavily. `[10.06]`
2. The speed and magnitude of recent price changes (overbought/oversold). `[10.07]`
3. Above-average volume alongside a genuine close beyond the boundary. `[14.03]`, `[24.04]`
4. The price level with the highest traded volume within a profile period. `[16.02]`
5. Market Profile uses time-at-price (TPO); Volume Profile uses actual traded volume-at-price. `[16.04]`
6-13. Open-ended: low volume undermines breakout confidence, elevated false-breakout risk (`[13.04]`, `[14.03]`); this is bearish divergence, suggesting weakening momentum (`[10.10]`); context/location matters more than shape alone (`[11.08]`); unconfirmed volume on a pattern break warrants caution (`[12.10]`); POC/Value Area boundaries provide structurally meaningful stop levels (`[16.02]`); excessive indicator stacking risks paralysis/over-filtering and doesn't guarantee higher win rate (`[10.11]`); location context (support/resistance) gives a pattern actual structural meaning (`[11.08]`); event-driven pattern formation may not reflect the same underlying supply/demand dynamics as organic formation (`[12.10]`).
