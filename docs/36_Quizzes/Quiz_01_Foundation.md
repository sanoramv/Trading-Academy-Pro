## Section A — Recall
1. What is the difference between a market and an exchange?
2. What are the three main financial statements?
3. What defines an uptrend using classical structure terms?
4. What is the difference between BOS and CHOCH?
5. What does the P/E ratio measure?

## Section B — Application
6. A stock trades at a P/E of 45 while its sector average is 18. List two questions you would investigate before concluding it is "overvalued."
7. Given a chart showing three consecutive higher highs and higher lows, followed by a break below the most recent higher low — what does this most likely signal, and what should you check next before acting?
8. A company's Income Statement shows rising profit for three straight quarters, but its Cash Flow Statement shows consistently negative operating cash flow over the same period. What does this divergence suggest, and which lesson addresses it directly?
9. You are evaluating a company using a top-down approach. List the order in which you would move through your analysis.
10. A DCF model produces an intrinsic value 40% above the current market price. Name two assumptions in the model you would stress-test before trusting this conclusion.

## Section C — Judgment
11. A friend recommends buying a stock purely because "the chart looks like it's about to break out," without checking the company's financial statements. What is missing from this decision process, and why does it matter regardless of whether the trade happens to work this time?
12. You've identified a genuine CHOCH signal on the daily chart. Should you enter immediately at market, or does sound practice suggest anything else first? Explain your reasoning.
13. A company reports strong revenue growth, but its accounts receivable are growing even faster than revenue. Is this a reason for concern? Why?
14. Explain why "the stock went up after I bought it" is not sufficient evidence that your fundamental analysis process was sound.
15. A trader treats every valuation metric (P/E, P/B, EV/EBITDA) as equally reliable across every industry. What risk does this create?

## Answer Key
1. A market is the broader system of buyers/sellers; an exchange is the organized venue facilitating trade. `[01.01]`
2. Income Statement, Balance Sheet, Cash Flow Statement. `[07.01]`
3. A sequence of higher highs and higher lows. `[02.03]`
4. BOS confirms trend continuation; CHOCH warns of a potential reversal. `[02.05]`
5. Price relative to earnings per share. `[08.06]`, `[09.02]`
6-15. Open-ended application/judgment — grade against this lesson's specific reasoning: relative valuation requires peer/growth context (`[09.02]`), structure breaks require confirmation not blind reaction (`[02.05]`), cash flow divergence signals possible earnings quality issues (`[07.06]`), top-down moves macro→sector→company (`[08.02]`), DCF is highly assumption-sensitive (`[09.03]`), technical signals without fundamental context are incomplete (`[08.01]`), structure signals still need risk management (`[25.03]`), rising receivables vs. revenue can signal aggressive revenue recognition (`[07.06]`), outcome ≠ process validation (`[24.12]`), valuation multiples are industry-context-dependent (`[09.02]`).
