# [02.03] Higher Highs, Higher Lows, Lower Highs, Lower Lows

> Module: Market Structure · Lesson 3 of 8 · Estimated time: 30 minutes

## Learning Objectives
- Precisely define HH, HL, LH, LL and identify them on a real chart.
- Understand why both the high AND the low matter, not just one.
- Learn the specific structural event that signals early trend weakening.
- Practice labeling a real chart's swing sequence correctly.

## Core Concepts

### Definition
- **Higher High (HH):** a swing high that is priced above the previous
  swing high.
- **Higher Low (HL):** a swing low that is priced above the previous swing
  low.
- **Lower High (LH):** a swing high that is priced below the previous swing
  high.
- **Lower Low (LL):** a swing low that is priced below the previous swing
  low.

A confirmed uptrend requires **both** HH and HL occurring in sequence — a
confirmed downtrend requires **both** LH and LL. One without the other is an
early warning sign, not full confirmation, of trend continuation.

### Intuition
Beginners often only watch the highs (or only the lows), but a healthy
trend needs confirmation from *both*. Imagine a trend as a two-legged
walk: the "high leg" and the "low leg" need to move in the same direction
together for the walk to be considered a confident, ongoing stride. If one
leg moves forward while the other stays still or moves backward, that's an
early sign of a stumble — not yet a fall, but worth watching closely.

### Mental Model
Track each swing point as it forms and label it relative to the *previous*
swing point of the same type (not the previous candle):

```
Sequence:  Low1 -> High1 -> Low2 -> High2 -> Low3 -> High3

Compare High2 to High1: higher? --> HH.  lower? --> LH.
Compare Low2 to Low1:  higher? --> HL.  lower? --> LL.
Compare High3 to High2: higher? --> HH.  lower? --> LH.
Compare Low3 to Low2:  higher? --> HL.  lower? --> LL.
```

### Visual Explanation
Labeling every swing point in sequence, rather than just glancing at the
overall chart shape, is what turns a vague visual impression into a
rigorous, repeatable structural read.

### ASCII Diagram

## Real World Analogy
Think of HH/HL/LH/LL like tracking a company's quarterly results on two
separate metrics: revenue (the "highs") and profit margin (the "lows"). A
company reporting record revenue every quarter (HH) but with shrinking
profit margins each quarter (LL-equivalent on the "low" metric) is showing
mixed signals — impressive on one dimension, worrying on another. A truly
healthy growth story needs both metrics improving together, just as a
healthy uptrend needs both HH and HL confirming together.

## Indian Market Example
During Nifty 50's strong 2020–2021 recovery uptrend (post-COVID low of
7,511 in March 2020), the index consistently posted both higher highs and
higher lows through most of that period — each pullback found support
above the prior pullback's low, confirming trend health at every stage,
until the pattern eventually began showing its first Lower High around
October 2021 near the 18,600 level, an early structural warning sign that
preceded the broader 2022 correction phase.

## US Market Example
The **Nasdaq Composite's 2021–2022 transition** is a clean example: through
most of 2021, the index posted higher highs and higher lows. Its final
all-time high near 16,212 in November 2021 was followed by a failure to
make a new higher high on the next attempt (forming a Lower High), and
shortly after, price broke below the prior Higher Low — together
constituting the two-step structural confirmation (LH followed by LL) that
the multi-year uptrend had genuinely ended and a downtrend had begun.

## Historical Example
The **2008 pre-crisis Sensex top** (January 2008, near 21,000) followed a
similar pattern: after years of consistent HH/HL structure during the
2003–2007 bull run, the index formed a Lower High in early 2008 attempting
to retest its January high, followed shortly after by a decisive break
below the prior Higher Low — the same two-step early-warning-then-
confirmation sequence traders can learn to recognize in real time using
nothing more than the HH/HL/LH/LL framework taught in this lesson.

## Institutional Perspective
Institutional trend-following systems are often literally coded around
formal HH/HL/LH/LL rules (sometimes with additional filters like minimum
percentage move thresholds to filter out noise) — turning what looks like
a discretionary, "eyeballing the chart" skill into a systematic, rules-based
process. This is one of the cleanest bridges in this academy between
discretionary chart reading (this module) and systematic/algorithmic
trading (Module 29).

## Retail Perspective
Retail traders often notice a Lower High forming but dismiss it as "just a
small pullback," waiting for very obvious, large-scale confirmation before
acting — by which point much of a move has often already happened.
Learning to treat a single LH (in a prior uptrend) or HL (in a prior
downtrend) as an *early warning worth monitoring closely*, without
over-reacting to it as definitive proof of reversal, is the balanced
approach this lesson aims to build.

## Professional Insights
Professional traders often specifically watch for the **two-step
sequence**: first an anomalous swing point (an LH within an uptrend, or an
HL within a downtrend) as an early warning, then a confirmed break of the
most recent swing low/high as the actual structural confirmation (fully
covered as Break of Structure in `[02.05]`). Trading purely off the first
warning sign alone, without the second confirmation, is a common source of
premature, low-probability reversal trades.

## Common Mistakes
- Only tracking highs (or only lows), missing that a genuine trend needs
  confirmation from both.
- Treating a single Lower High (in an uptrend) as automatic proof of a full
  reversal, without waiting for the corresponding low to also break.
- Mislabeling minor, low-significance wiggles as swing points, cluttering
  the structural read with noise (a skill refined further in `[02.07]`,
  Swing Points and Fractals).

## Misconceptions
- **"Any lower high means the trend is definitely over."** A single LH is
  an early warning worth monitoring, not automatic confirmation — genuine
  reversal requires the corresponding structural break too (`[02.05]`).
- **"HH/HL analysis only works on daily charts."** This framework applies
  identically across any timeframe — from 1-minute charts to monthly charts
  — which is exactly why multi-timeframe analysis (`[02.08]`) matters.
- **"You need an indicator to identify HH/HL/LH/LL."** This is a purely
  price-based, visual skill requiring no indicator, though some charting
  tools offer automated swing-labeling features as a convenience.

## Key Takeaways
- HH/HL define uptrend confirmation; LH/LL define downtrend confirmation —
  both the high and the low must confirm together.
- A single anomalous swing point (LH in an uptrend, HL in a downtrend) is an
  early warning sign, not full confirmation of reversal.
- Full structural reversal requires a two-step sequence: the anomalous
  swing point, followed by a confirmed break of the corresponding prior
  swing extreme (`[02.05]`).
- This framework is timeframe-agnostic and requires no indicators.

## Summary
Higher Highs, Higher Lows, Lower Highs, and Lower Lows are the precise,
buildable vocabulary for everything discussed more loosely in `[02.01]` and
`[02.02]`. A genuine trend needs both the high and low sequence confirming
together; a single anomalous swing point is an early warning worth
monitoring, while full structural reversal requires the two-step sequence
completed in the next lesson's topic — Break of Structure and Change of
Character.

## Revision Notes
- HH = swing high above the previous swing high. HL = swing low above the
  previous swing low. LH/LL = the mirror image for downtrends.
- Uptrend confirmation needs BOTH HH and HL; downtrend needs BOTH LH and LL.
- A single anomalous swing point = early warning, not confirmation.
- Full reversal = anomalous swing point + confirmed break of the prior
  swing extreme (covered in `[02.05]`).
- Timeframe-agnostic, indicator-free skill.

## Quiz
1. What confirms a genuine uptrend?
   a) A single higher high alone
   b) Both higher highs and higher lows occurring together
   c) High trading volume alone
   d) A green candle

2. What is a Lower High (LH) within a prior uptrend considered?
   a) Definitive proof of reversal
   b) An early warning sign worth monitoring, not full confirmation
   c) Meaningless noise to ignore
   d) A buy signal

3. What two-step sequence confirms a full structural reversal?
   a) Two green candles in a row
   b) An anomalous swing point followed by a confirmed break of the prior swing extreme
   c) A single indicator crossing a line
   d) High volume alone

4. Does the HH/HL/LH/LL framework require a specific indicator?
   a) Yes, RSI is required
   b) No — it's a purely price-based, visual skill
   c) Yes, MACD is required
   d) Yes, only volume indicators work

5. True or False: This framework only works on daily timeframe charts.
   a) True
   b) False

## Answers
1. **b)** — Genuine uptrend confirmation requires both HH and HL together.
2. **b)** — A single LH is an early warning, not full reversal confirmation.
3. **b)** — The two-step sequence is an anomalous swing point followed by a confirmed structural break.
4. **b)** — No indicator is required; it's purely price/swing-based.
5. **b) False** — The framework applies across any timeframe.
