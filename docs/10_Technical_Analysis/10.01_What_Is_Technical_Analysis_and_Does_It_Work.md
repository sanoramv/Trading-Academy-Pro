## Core Concepts

### Definition
**Technical analysis (TA)** is the study of historical price and volume
data to identify patterns and structure (`[02.01]`) that may inform future
price behavior, resting on three core assumptions: (1) **"Price discounts
everything"** — all known information (fundamentals, sentiment, macro
factors) is already reflected in the current price; (2) **"Price moves in
trends"** — prices tend to continue in an established direction until a
genuine reversal occurs, connecting directly to `[02.02]`'s "structure
until proven otherwise" principle; and (3) **"History tends to repeat
itself"** — recurring patterns in price behavior reflect persistent
patterns in human psychology and market participant behavior.

### Intuition
Where fundamental analysis (Modules 07-09) asks "what is this business
worth," technical analysis asks "what does the current balance of buyers
and sellers, and the historical pattern of their behavior, suggest about
likely near-term price behavior" — a fundamentally different question,
using price and volume data itself as the primary information source
rather than financial statements or macro data.

### Mental Model
```
TECHNICAL ANALYSIS'S THREE CORE ASSUMPTIONS:

1. "PRICE DISCOUNTS EVERYTHING"
   All known information is already reflected in current price
   --> focus on price itself, not re-deriving fundamentals

2. "PRICE MOVES IN TRENDS"
   Directly extends [02.01]-[02.02]'s structure framework --
   trends persist until genuine structural evidence of reversal

3. "HISTORY TENDS TO REPEAT ITSELF"
   Recurring patterns reflect persistent, recurring human
   psychology (fear, greed, herding) -- the foundation for
   candlestick (Module 11) and chart pattern (Module 12) analysis
```

### Visual Explanation
The efficient market hypothesis (EMH) debate frames the central academic
controversy around TA's validity — understanding both sides equips you to
use TA with appropriate, evidence-based confidence rather than either
blind faith or dismissive skepticism.

## Real World Analogy
Technical analysis is like reading crowd behavior at a large public event
to anticipate what might happen next — you can't know with certainty what
any individual will do, but recurring patterns in how crowds move, react
to stimuli, and follow momentum (a crowd starting to move toward an exit
tends to keep moving that direction; a sudden loud noise tends to produce
a somewhat predictable startle-and-regroup pattern) provide genuinely
useful, if imperfect, probabilistic information — exactly the kind of
insight TA aims to extract from price and volume "crowd behavior."

## Indian Market Example
Indian technical analysts routinely combine chart-based structure analysis
(Module 02) with fundamental context (Modules 07-09) when analyzing stocks
like Reliance Industries or Nifty 50 itself — using TA specifically to
time entries/exits around fundamentally-supported theses, rather than as
a standalone predictive tool disconnected from business or macro
understanding, reflecting the balanced, combined approach this lesson
advocates.

## US Market Example
Academic finance research on US markets has produced genuinely mixed
findings on TA's predictive validity — some studies find weak, sometimes
statistically significant edges from certain price patterns (particularly
around momentum effects over medium-term horizons), while others find no
reliable edge once transaction costs and market efficiency are properly
accounted for — a nuanced, still-debated academic literature rather than a
clear-cut verdict in either direction.

## Historical Example
**Charles Dow's** late 19th-century writings on market averages and trend
behavior (developed fully in `[10.02]`) represent technical analysis's
foundational historical starting point — predating modern academic
finance and the efficient market hypothesis debate by many decades,
illustrating that TA's practitioner tradition developed independently,
through direct market observation, before later being subjected to formal
academic scrutiny.

## Institutional Perspective
Institutional trading desks use technical analysis substantially for
**execution timing and risk management** (identifying favorable entry/
exit structure, setting stop-losses based on structural levels per
`[02.04]`) rather than as a standalone source of investment theses —
reflecting a pragmatic, integrated use of TA alongside fundamental
analysis (Modules 07-09) and quantitative methods (Module 30), not TA in
isolation.

## Retail Perspective
Retail traders often encounter technical analysis presented as a
near-magical predictive system by less rigorous educational sources —
building a balanced, evidence-based understanding of TA's genuine
strengths (structure reading, risk management, `[02.01]`-`[02.08]`) and
genuine limitations (imperfect prediction, no certainty) from the outset
prevents both excessive skepticism and excessive overconfidence.

## Professional Insights
Professional technical analysts increasingly emphasize that TA's greatest
practical value may lie less in precise price prediction and more in
**probabilistic risk framing** — using structure (Module 02), patterns
(Modules 11-12), and indicators (this module) to define clear, asymmetric
risk/reward setups (defined stop-loss, defined target) rather than
claiming certain knowledge of future price direction.

## Common Mistakes
- Treating technical analysis as a guaranteed, mechanical prediction
  system rather than a probabilistic framework.
- Dismissing TA entirely based on strong-form EMH arguments without
  engaging with its genuine, more nuanced practical uses (risk management,
  structure reading).
- Using TA in complete isolation from fundamental and macro context,
  missing valuable complementary information.

## Misconceptions
- **"Technical analysis can predict exact future prices with
  certainty."** TA is explicitly probabilistic — even its most skilled
  practitioners operate with defined risk management (Module 25) precisely
  because outcomes are never certain.
- **"Technical analysis has been definitively proven either completely
  valid or completely invalid by academic research."** The academic
  evidence is genuinely mixed and nuanced, varying by specific pattern,
  timeframe, and market efficiency conditions — neither extreme position
  is well-supported by the actual research literature.
- **"You must choose either technical or fundamental analysis exclusively,
  never both."** As `[08.01]` established and this lesson reinforces, many
  skilled practitioners combine both approaches, using fundamentals to
  decide what to consider and technicals to help time entries/exits and
  manage risk.

## Key Takeaways
- Technical analysis rests on three assumptions: price discounts
  everything, price moves in trends, and history tends to repeat.
- The efficient market hypothesis debate around TA's validity remains
  genuinely nuanced and academically mixed, not definitively resolved.
- TA's most defensible practical value lies in structure reading and risk
  management, building directly on Module 02's foundation.
- Institutional and skilled retail practitioners typically combine TA with
  fundamental analysis rather than using either in isolation.

## Summary
Technical analysis — studying price and volume patterns based on the
assumptions that price discounts everything, moves in trends, and tends to
repeat historical patterns — offers a probabilistic, structure-focused
complement to the fundamental analysis toolkit built across Modules 07-09,
with its most defensible practical value lying in risk management and
structure reading rather than certain price prediction. This balanced,
evidence-based framing sets up the next lesson's historical foundation:
Charles Dow's original writings establishing the discipline's core
principles.

   complete certainty.
   a) True
   b) False

## Answers
1. **b)** — Price discounts everything, price moves in trends, and history tends to repeat are TA's three core assumptions.
2. **b)** — The academic evidence on TA's validity is genuinely mixed and nuanced.
3. **b)** — TA's most defensible value lies in structure reading and risk management, not certain prediction.
4. **b)** — Institutions typically combine TA with fundamentals for execution timing and risk management.
5. **b) False** — TA is explicitly probabilistic, never offering certain prediction.
