## Core Concepts

### Definition
**Position adjustment** means modifying an existing options position —
by rolling a leg to a different strike or expiry, adding a new leg, or
closing part of the position — in direct response to how its Greeks
have evolved since entry. This lesson works through one continuous,
**Hypothetical Example** case study, applying every Greek from `[22.02]`
through `[22.06]` in sequence to a single Nifty iron condor
(`[21.10]`) as market conditions shift over several sessions.

### Intuition
Module 21's strategies were analyzed as static positions held to
expiry. In practice, professional traders rarely hold a multi-leg
position passively — they monitor how its Greeks evolve and adjust when
the position's risk has meaningfully changed from what was originally
accepted at entry. This case study makes that process concrete, step by
step.

### Mental Model — Worked Case Study (Hypothetical)
```
DAY 0 — ENTRY
  Nifty at 24,000. Trader sells an iron condor (`[21.10]`):
  Short 24,200 Call / Long 24,400 Call
  Short 23,800 Put  / Long 23,600 Put
  Net Credit = 90. Net Greeks: Delta ~0, Gamma -0.02, Theta +12, Vega -18

DAY 3 — NIFTY RALLIES TO 24,150
  Net Delta has drifted to +0.15 (call side now closer to being tested)
  Net Gamma has grown more negative (approaching the short call strike)
  READ: Directional risk emerging on the call side; Gamma risk rising

DAY 3 — ADJUSTMENT DECISION
  Option A: ROLL the call spread up and out (higher strikes, later expiry)
            -> resets Delta closer to neutral, but requires paying to roll
  Option B: CLOSE the call spread only, leave the put spread running
            -> removes the threatened side entirely, banks remaining Theta
             from the put side
  Option C: DO NOTHING, accept the now-directional risk
            -> only appropriate if within originally planned risk tolerance

DAY 7 — IV RISES SHARPLY (unexpected event)
  Net Vega (-18) now working AGAINST the position as IV increases
  READ: Even with Delta managed, rising IV independently hurts the position
  -> reinforces closing/reducing the position rather than "waiting it out"
```

## Real World Analogy
Adjusting a position by its Greeks is like a ship's captain course-
correcting gradually as conditions change, rather than only reacting
once the ship has already run aground — noticing Delta drift (a
gradually shifting current) early allows a smaller, cheaper correction
(rolling a strike) than waiting until the position (the ship) is
already in serious trouble.

## Indian Market Example
Continuing this lesson's case study: the trader chooses Option B on Day
3 — closing only the threatened call spread while leaving the put
spread open — banking the call side's realized loss but preserving the
put side's remaining Theta income, then closes the put spread entirely
on Day 7 once rising IV (falling Vega) further erodes the position's
edge, ending the trade with a smaller, defined loss rather than holding
unmanaged to expiry. **Hypothetical Example** for illustration only.

## US Market Example
A trader holding a similarly structured SPY iron condor ahead of an
unscheduled Federal Reserve statement (`[06.02]`) notices Vega turning
sharply negative-impactful as IV begins rising in the hours before the
statement, and closes the position proactively rather than waiting to
see the statement's content — an example of acting on Vega risk alone,
independent of any Delta/Gamma drift. **Hypothetical Example** for
illustration only.

## Historical Example
Professional volatility-selling desks' practice of actively adjusting
or closing short-premium positions ahead of known volatility-inflating
events — rather than holding mechanically to expiry — became especially
visible industry-wide following episodes like the "Volmageddon" event of
February 2018 in US markets, when several short-volatility products
suffered severe, rapid losses partly attributed to inadequate active
risk management of exactly the Gamma and Vega exposures this case study
walks through.

## Institutional Perspective
Institutional desks formalize this case study's decision process into
explicit risk limits — predefined Delta, Gamma, and Vega thresholds that
trigger mandatory review or automatic hedging — removing discretion
about *whether* to act once a threshold is breached, only *how*.

## Retail Perspective
Retail traders rarely have institutional risk-limit systems, but can
apply the same discipline manually: deciding, before entry, at what
Delta/Gamma/Vega drift level they will review or adjust a position,
rather than deciding reactively (and often emotionally, Module 28
previewed) only after a loss has already grown large.

## Professional Insights
Professional traders emphasize that adjustment is not the same as
"avoiding a loss" — Option B in this case study still banked a real
loss on the call spread; the goal of Greeks-based adjustment is
managing the *size and character* of risk going forward, not eliminating
risk that has already materialized.

## Common Mistakes
- Waiting to adjust until a position is deep in loss rather than acting
  on early Delta/Gamma drift signals.
- Adjusting based on Delta/Gamma alone while ignoring an independent
  Vega-driven risk, as this case study's Day 7 event illustrates.
- Rolling a threatened leg repeatedly without a predefined limit on how
  many times or how far the position will be defended.

## Misconceptions
- **"Adjusting a position always prevents a loss."** It manages ongoing
  risk exposure — it does not undo a loss already realized on a
  previously threatened leg.
- **"If Delta looks fine, the position is fine."** As this case study's
  Day 7 event shows, Vega can independently threaten a position even
  when Delta and Gamma appear stable.
- **"Only professionals need an adjustment plan."** Any trader running
  multi-leg strategies benefits from deciding adjustment triggers before
  entry, not improvising them under pressure.

## Key Takeaways
- Position adjustment responds to how net Greeks evolve after entry, not
  just conditions at entry.
- Rolling, partial closing, and full closing are the three main
  adjustment techniques, each suited to different Greek-drift situations.
- Delta/Gamma drift and Vega-driven risk can each independently justify
  adjustment — always check both, not just one.
- Deciding adjustment triggers before entry (not reactively) is a
  discipline any trader, not just institutions, can apply.

## Quiz
1. In this lesson's case study, what triggered the Day 3 adjustment decision?
   a) Falling Theta  b) Delta and Gamma drifting as Nifty rallied toward the short call strike  c) A dividend announcement  d) A change in lot size

2. What triggered further action on Day 7?
   a) A stock split  b) IV rising sharply, hurting the position via negative Vega  c) Theta turning negative  d) Nothing, the position was closed already

3. Name one of the three main adjustment techniques discussed.
   a) Ignoring the position  b) Rolling the threatened leg  c) Doubling the position size  d) Switching underlyings

4. Why should adjustment triggers be decided before entry?
   a) It's a regulatory requirement  b) To avoid reactive, pressured decisions under stress  c) It has no real benefit  d) Only institutions need to do this

5. True or False: A position with stable Delta and Gamma cannot be threatened by any other Greek.
   a) True
   b) False

## Answers
1. **b)** — Delta and Gamma drifting as Nifty rallied toward the short call strike.
2. **b)** — IV rising sharply, hurting the position via negative Vega.
3. **b)** — Rolling the threatened leg (partial closing and full closing are the other two).
4. **b)** — To avoid reactive, pressured decisions once a position is already under stress.
5. **b) False** — Vega-driven IV risk can threaten a position independently of Delta/Gamma.
