## Core Concepts

### Definition
This capstone lesson synthesizes a complete, honest evaluation of ICT
methodology, distinguishing concepts on a **spectrum of evidentiary
support**: **relatively well-grounded** (Kill Zones' session-based
liquidity logic, `[19.03]`, supported by documented market
microstructure research; Market Maker Models' structural parallel to
long-established Wyckoff mechanics, `[19.02]`); **moderately
speculative** (Optimal Trade Entry's specific 62%-79% numerical
boundary, `[19.04]`, an ICT-specific refinement without independent
rigorous validation); and **most speculative/criticism-prone** (Power of
Three and Judas Swing's precise daily narrative structure, `[19.05]`-
`[19.06]`, particularly vulnerable to the retrospective-bias criticism
already raised in `[18.08]`).

### Intuition
Just as `[18.08]` taught a nuanced, spectrum-based evaluation of SMC
rather than a single blanket verdict, ICT similarly deserves
differentiated treatment — some of its concepts (session-timing logic)
rest on genuinely stronger empirical footing than others (precise daily
narrative sequencing), and a mature practitioner should calibrate
confidence accordingly rather than treating the entire framework as
uniformly valid or uniformly invalid.

claims within a single alternative health regimen — some specific
components might have genuine, well-documented physiological grounding,
while others rest primarily on anecdote and appealing narrative
structure — a thoughtful consumer applies differentiated scrutiny to
each specific claim rather than either accepting or rejecting the entire
regimen as a single, undifferentiated package.

## Common Mistakes
- Treating ICT as either entirely valid or entirely invalid, rather than
  applying differentiated, concept-by-concept evaluation.
- Relying heavily on ICT's more speculative, narrative-heavy concepts
  (precise PO3 sequencing) without personal, prospective verification.
- Failing to integrate ICT's genuinely useful concepts (Kill Zones,
  Market Maker Models) simply due to skepticism about the framework's
  more speculative elements.

## Misconceptions
- **"ICT should be either fully adopted or fully rejected as a single,
  undifferentiated package."** A differentiated, spectrum-based
  evaluation, concept by concept, is the appropriate approach, as this
  lesson demonstrates.
- **"ICT's more speculative concepts have zero value at all."** Even
  speculative concepts like Power of Three can provide useful heuristic
  structure for organizing analysis, provided they're applied with
  appropriate skepticism and prospective verification rather than blind
  confidence.
- **"This module's critical treatment of ICT means the framework is
  worthless."** The synthesis specifically identifies genuinely
  well-grounded elements (Kill Zones, Market Maker Models) worth
  incorporating alongside appropriate skepticism toward more speculative
  claims.

## Key Takeaways
- ICT concepts exist on an evidentiary spectrum: Kill Zones and Market
  Maker Models are relatively well-grounded; OTE's specific boundary and
  Judas Swing are moderately speculative; Power of Three's precise daily
  narrative is most speculative and retrospective-bias-prone.
- Apply `[18.08]`'s four-criteria checklist (prospective vs.
  retrospective, academic validation, commercial incentive, subjective
  judgment) to any specific ICT claim.
- A mature practitioner calibrates confidence differentially across
  specific concepts, rather than wholesale accepting or rejecting the
  entire framework.
- This evidentiary-spectrum approach mirrors this academy's consistent
  epistemic standard applied throughout Modules 10, 17, and 18.
