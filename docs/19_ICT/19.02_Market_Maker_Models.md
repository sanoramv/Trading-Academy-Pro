## Core Concepts

### Definition
ICT's **Market Maker Models** describe a specific, idealized sequence of
price behavior institutional "market makers" are theorized to follow
when establishing directional positioning. The **Market Maker Buy
Model** sequences: an initial decline sweeping sell-side liquidity
(`[18.02]`) below a recent low, followed by a sharp reversal
("displacement") leaving behind an order block and often an FVG
(`[18.04]`), then a subsequent markup phase. The **Market Maker Sell
Model** mirrors this exactly in reverse: an initial rally sweeping
buy-side liquidity above a recent high, followed by sharp reversal
displacement, then markdown.

### Intuition
These models are ICT's specific, sequenced synthesis of concepts already
covered separately across Modules 17-18 — the Buy Model's opening
liquidity sweep directly parallels Wyckoff's Spring (`[17.05]`), the
displacement and resulting order block parallel `[18.03]`'s formation
mechanics, and the overall sequence mirrors the Accumulation Schematic's
Phase C-to-E progression (`[17.03]`), simply packaged as a single, named,
repeatable pattern.

### Visual Explanation
The "displacement" candle(s) immediately following the liquidity sweep is
considered the Market Maker Model's most critical confirming signal —
ICT traders specifically look for a sharp, high-momentum move (often
itself creating an FVG, `[18.04]`) as confirmation that genuine
institutional positioning, not just a random liquidity sweep, has
occurred.

## Real World Analogy
A Market Maker Model is like a specific, choreographed sequence a savvy
negotiator follows when making a large purchase: first appearing to
walk away or express disinterest (drawing out the seller's best,
lowest offer — the liquidity sweep), then decisively moving to close
the deal once the best terms are revealed (the displacement), before
proceeding with the full transaction (the markup/markdown phase) —
a deliberate, sequenced negotiation tactic rather than a single,
simple action.

## Indian Market Example
Indian ICT-influenced Bank Nifty futures traders specifically watch for
this three-stage sequence on intraday charts — a sweep below a recent
swing low, followed by sharp displacement and order block formation, is
treated as a higher-confidence long setup than a simple support bounce
identified without this specific sequential confirmation.

## US Market Example
US ICT traders analyzing ES futures frequently combine Market Maker
Model identification with `[19.03]`'s Kill Zone timing — specifically
favoring Market Maker Model setups that complete their liquidity sweep
and displacement stages within a recognized high-liquidity session
window, rather than treating the pattern as equally significant
regardless of when it occurs.

## Historical Example
Market Maker Models represent ICT's specific, named synthesis
(popularized 2010s, per `[19.01]`) of the underlying Wyckoff Spring/
Upthrust and Accumulation/Distribution Schematic mechanics (Module 17)
established decades earlier — another clear instance of ICT packaging
established concepts into a memorable, sequenced framework.

## Institutional Perspective
Institutional analysts note that the underlying sequential logic (initial
liquidity-clearing move, followed by decisive positioning, followed by
directional continuation) has genuine plausibility grounded in how large
orders are typically built — though institutions would not describe
their own execution sequence using ICT's specific branded "Market Maker
Model" terminology.

## Retail Perspective
Retail traders should specifically practice identifying all three
sequential stages (sweep, displacement, order block formation) together,
rather than acting on any single stage in isolation — the model's
value lies specifically in its sequenced, multi-stage confirmation
structure.

## Professional Insights
Professional analysts caution that Market Maker Models, like other ICT
and SMC concepts, require genuine displacement confirmation (a
sufficiently sharp, high-momentum reversal) rather than any minor
reversal being labeled as displacement — weak, ambiguous reversals
following a liquidity sweep should not be treated as confirmed Market
Maker Model setups.

## Common Mistakes
- Acting on a liquidity sweep alone, without waiting for genuine
  displacement confirmation.
- Labeling any minor reversal as "displacement," diluting the model's
  intended selectivity and confirmation value.
- Not recognizing the model's direct structural parallel to Wyckoff's
  Accumulation/Distribution Schematics (Module 17).

## Misconceptions
- **"Market Maker Models are an entirely novel ICT invention."** They
  directly parallel Wyckoff's Spring/Upthrust and Accumulation/
  Distribution Schematic mechanics (Module 17), packaged as a named,
  sequenced framework.
- **"Any reversal following a liquidity sweep counts as a valid Market
  Maker Model."** Genuine, sufficiently sharp displacement confirmation
  is required — weak reversals should not be treated as confirmed
  setups.
- **"The Buy Model and Sell Model are unrelated patterns."** They are
  direct mirror images of each other, exactly paralleling Module 17's
  Accumulation/Distribution mirror relationship.

## Key Takeaways
- Market Maker Buy Model: sell-side liquidity sweep → displacement
  (order block + often FVG) → markup.
- Market Maker Sell Model: buy-side liquidity sweep → displacement
  (order block + often FVG) → markdown.
- These models directly parallel Wyckoff's Spring/Upthrust and
  Accumulation/Distribution Schematic mechanics (Module 17).
- Genuine displacement confirmation (sufficiently sharp reversal) is
  essential — weak reversals should not be treated as valid setups.

## Quiz
1. What are the three stages of the Market Maker Buy Model?
   a) Random events  b) Sell-side liquidity sweep, displacement, markup  c) Only markup  d) Only a liquidity sweep

2. What are the three stages of the Market Maker Sell Model?
   a) Buy-side liquidity sweep, displacement, markdown  b) Identical to the Buy Model  c) Only markdown  d) Random events

3. What Wyckoff concept do Market Maker Models directly parallel?
   a) Dow Theory alone  b) The Accumulation/Distribution Schematics and Spring/Upthrust mechanics  c) DCF valuation  d) Nothing, it's unrelated

4. What confirmation is essential before trusting a Market Maker Model setup?
   a) None needed  b) Genuine, sufficiently sharp displacement  c) Only volume  d) Only time of day

5. True or False: The Buy Model and Sell Model are unrelated, independent patterns.
   a) True
   b) False

## Answers
1. **b)** — Sell-side liquidity sweep, displacement, markup.
2. **a)** — Buy-side liquidity sweep, displacement, markdown.
3. **b)** — The Accumulation/Distribution Schematics and Spring/Upthrust mechanics.
4. **b)** — Genuine, sufficiently sharp displacement.
5. **b) False** — They are direct mirror images of each other.
