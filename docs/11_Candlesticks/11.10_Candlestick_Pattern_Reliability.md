## Core Concepts

### Definition
**Backtesting** systematically tests a trading pattern or rule against
historical data to measure its actual, empirical performance, rather than
relying on anecdotal impression or folklore. Applied to candlestick
patterns, backtesting research has produced genuinely **mixed, nuanced
findings** — some studies find modest, statistically detectable edges for
certain patterns under certain conditions (particularly when combined with
the contextual factors from `[11.08]`), while other studies find little to
no reliable edge for patterns applied in isolation, echoing `[10.01]`'s
broader, unresolved EMH-versus-TA debate.

### Intuition
This lesson directly extends `[10.01]`'s foundational caution — technical
analysis broadly, and candlestick patterns specifically, should be
evaluated with the same evidence-based skepticism this academy has
applied throughout, rather than accepted on the strength of tradition,
popularity, or compelling narrative (like `[11.09]`'s Homma folklore)
alone. Honest engagement with backtesting research's genuinely mixed
findings represents the mark of analytical maturity this module has built
toward across all ten lessons.

### Mental Model
```
WHY BACKTESTING CANDLESTICK PATTERNS IS METHODOLOGICALLY HARD:

1. CONTEXT-DEPENDENCE ([11.08]): patterns' significance
   depends heavily on structural/trend/volume context --
   testing pattern shape ALONE (without this context)
   likely understates genuine real-world performance

2. SUBJECTIVITY IN DEFINITION: exact qualifying criteria
   (e.g., HOW SMALL must a "small body" be for a Doji?) vary
   across sources, making replication and comparison across
   studies genuinely difficult

3. SURVIVORSHIP/PUBLICATION BIAS: studies/sources finding
   POSITIVE results may be more likely to be published/
   promoted than null-result studies, potentially skewing
   the visible research base

4. MARKET REGIME CHANGES: a pattern's historical performance
   in one market era/regime (per Module 06's liquidity
   cycles) may not transfer to different future conditions
```

## Real World Analogy
Approaching candlestick pattern reliability claims is like evaluating
traditional folk remedies passed down for generations — some turn out,
upon rigorous modern scientific testing, to have genuine, measurable
efficacy (often when specific conditions are properly controlled for),
while others turn out to have little to no effect beyond placebo, despite
long-standing tradition and widespread belief. The appropriate response
isn't blanket dismissal of all traditional knowledge, nor uncritical
acceptance — it's applying rigorous testing and calibrating confidence to
match what that testing actually shows.

## Indian Market Example
Indian quantitative research desks and academic finance departments
studying Nifty 50 and individual Indian stock price data have, similar to
global research, found genuinely mixed results when backtesting classical
candlestick patterns — with some studies suggesting modest edges for
specific patterns under specific contextual conditions (particularly when
combined with volume and structural filters, per `[11.08]`), while
patterns tested in naive isolation frequently show weaker or
statistically insignificant standalone edges.

## US Market Example
Academic finance literature examining US equity and futures markets has
produced a substantial, genuinely mixed body of research on candlestick
pattern efficacy — some peer-reviewed studies report modest positive
findings for certain patterns (particularly Engulfing and Morning/Evening
Star variants) under certain conditions, while others report
insignificant or even reversed results, a pattern of genuinely mixed
academic findings directly paralleling `[10.01]`'s broader EMH-versus-TA
discussion.

## Historical Example
The broader historical arc from Homma-era folklore-adjacent tradition
(`[11.09]`) through Nison's 1991 systematic Western introduction to
today's quantitative backtesting era represents a genuine methodological
maturation — modern practitioners increasingly demand the kind of
rigorous, evidence-based validation this lesson advocates, rather than
accepting centuries-old tradition purely on the strength of its longevity
and popularity, mirroring the broader historical maturation of financial
analysis from pure folklore toward increasingly rigorous empirical
practice traced throughout this entire academy.

## Institutional Perspective
Institutional quantitative research desks (previewing Module 29-30) treat
candlestick patterns as testable hypotheses requiring rigorous statistical
validation before any systematic capital deployment — building formal
backtests with clearly defined pattern criteria, appropriate contextual
filters, and out-of-sample testing (validating a strategy on data it
wasn't developed using) before considering a pattern-based signal
worthy of live trading capital.

## Retail Perspective
Retail traders benefit enormously from adopting this same evidence-based
skepticism — rather than accepting candlestick pattern claims purely
because they're widely taught or intuitively appealing, developing the
habit of asking "what evidence actually supports this specific claim,
under what conditions" represents genuine analytical maturity, directly
extending `[10.01]`'s foundational caution to this module's specific
subject matter.

## Professional Insights
Professional discretionary traders who have successfully used candlestick
patterns for years typically emphasize that their genuine edge comes
substantially from the **contextual, confluence-based application**
(`[11.08]`, `[10.11]`) developed through extensive practice and pattern
recognition experience — not from the patterns' shapes alone in
isolation, directly consistent with backtesting research's finding that
context-dependent application shows more consistent value than naive,
isolated pattern application.

## Common Mistakes
- Accepting candlestick pattern reliability claims uncritically, based
  purely on tradition or popularity, without considering the genuinely
  mixed backtesting evidence.
- Dismissing candlestick patterns entirely as worthless folklore, an
  equally unbalanced extreme not well-supported by the actual research.
- Not distinguishing between patterns tested in naive isolation (weaker
  evidence) versus patterns tested with appropriate contextual filters
  (generally stronger evidence).

## Misconceptions
- **"Candlestick patterns have been definitively, scientifically proven
  to work reliably."** The backtesting evidence is genuinely mixed and
  context-dependent, not a definitive, unambiguous proof of reliable
  standalone efficacy.
- **"Candlestick patterns have been definitively, scientifically proven
  to be worthless folklore with zero value."** This equally overstates the
  evidence — some studies do find modest, context-dependent value,
  particularly when combined with `[11.08]`'s contextual framework.
- **"Backtesting candlestick patterns is straightforward and produces
  clear, unambiguous answers."** As this lesson's methodological
  challenges section demonstrates, definitional subjectivity, context-
  dependence, and potential publication bias make rigorous backtesting
  genuinely difficult, contributing to the mixed findings across
  different studies.

## Key Takeaways
- Backtesting research on candlestick patterns shows genuinely mixed
  findings, not definitive proof of either strong reliability or
  worthlessness.
- Methodological challenges (definitional subjectivity, context-
  dependence, publication bias) make rigorous backtesting genuinely
  difficult.
- Patterns tested with appropriate contextual filters (`[11.08]`)
  generally show more consistent evidence than patterns tested in naive
  isolation.
- The evidence-based, calibrated position: modest, context-dependent
  value as one confluence input, never a standalone guaranteed signal.
