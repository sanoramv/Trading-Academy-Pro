# Glossary — S to Z

> Part of Module 33. See `_Index.md` for how to use this reference.

**SEBI (Securities and Exchange Board of India)** — India's securities market regulator. `[03.02]`

**Sector Rotation** — Shifting portfolio weight toward sectors historically favored at the current business cycle stage. `[27.06]`

**Sharpe Ratio** — Excess return over the risk-free rate divided by a portfolio's total volatility; a risk-adjusted return measure. `[27.08]`

**Short Squeeze** — A heavily shorted stock's rising price forcing short covering, which itself adds further upward pressure. `[31.07]`

**Skewness** — A statistic measuring the asymmetry of a return distribution. `[30.02]`

**Sortino Ratio** — Like Sharpe, but using only downside volatility rather than total volatility. `[27.08]`

**Standard Deviation** — The square root of variance; a measure of spread around the mean, equivalent to "volatility." `[23.01]`, `[30.02]`

**Statistical Arbitrage** — Systematic strategies (including pairs trading) exploiting statistical relationships between instruments. `[30.03]`

**Stop-Loss** — A predetermined price level at which a losing position is exited to limit further loss. `[25.03]`

**Straddle** — A non-directional options strategy buying a call and put at the same strike, betting on large movement. `[21.09]`

**Strangle** — Like a straddle, but using different (OTM) strikes for the call and put, at lower cost. `[21.09]`

**Strike Price** — The fixed price at which an option's holder may buy (call) or sell (put) the underlying. `[21.03]`

**Survivorship Bias** — A backtesting pitfall of testing only currently-existing instruments, excluding delisted/failed ones. `[29.03]`

**Systematic Risk** — Broad, market-wide risk affecting nearly all assets simultaneously; cannot be diversified away. `[27.01]`

**Technical Analysis** — Studying price and volume charts to forecast future price behavior. `[10.01]`

**Theta** — The Greek measuring an option's premium sensitivity to time passing (decay). `[22.04]`

**Tilt** — An emotional disruption state, typically triggered by a loss, that impairs decision-making quality. `[28.04]`

**Trend-Following** — A strategy entering positions in the direction of an established trend, using a trailing stop. `[24.02]`

**Undefined Risk (Options)** — A position (e.g., naked short options) with no mathematically fixed maximum loss. `[26.05]`

**Unsystematic Risk** — Asset-specific risk (e.g., a single company's earnings miss); reducible through diversification. `[27.01]`

**Value-at-Risk (VaR)** — A risk metric estimating the maximum expected loss over a period at a given confidence level. `[30.07]`

**Value Investing** — Buying stocks trading cheap relative to fundamentals, one of the classic factor-investing categories. `[09.01]`, `[30.05]`

**Vega** — The Greek measuring an option's premium sensitivity to implied volatility. `[22.05]`

**VIX** — The CBOE's index of expected S&P 500 volatility over the next 30 days; the original "fear gauge." `[23.03]`

**Volatility** — A statistical measure of how much an asset's price fluctuates over a given period. `[23.01]`

**Volatility Clustering** — The tendency for high-volatility periods to be followed by more high-volatility, and vice versa. `[23.05]`

**Volume** — The number of shares/contracts traded over a given period. `[14.01]`

**VWAP (Volume-Weighted Average Price)** — An execution algorithm splitting an order to match the market's typical volume pattern. `[29.05]`

**Wyckoff Method** — Richard Wyckoff's framework of accumulation/distribution phases and the "Composite Man" concept. `[17.01]`

**Yield Curve** — A plot of bond yields across different maturities; inversion is a widely watched recession signal. `[06.07]`
