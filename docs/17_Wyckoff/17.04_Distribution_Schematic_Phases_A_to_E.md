## Core Concepts

### Definition
The **Wyckoff Distribution Schematic** is the direct mirror image of the
Accumulation Schematic (`[17.03]`), describing the typical sequence of
events as the Composite Man (`[17.06]`, previewed) distributes (sells) a
large position near market tops before eventually marking price down.
Its five phases parallel Accumulation's structure: **Phase A** (stopping
the prior uptrend — Preliminary Supply, Buying Climax, Automatic
Reaction, Secondary Test); **Phase B** (building the cause — extended
range-bound consolidation at elevated prices); **Phase C** (the test — an
Upthrust or Upthrust After Distribution, probing above resistance for
remaining demand); **Phase D** (evidence of weakness — Sign of Weakness
decline, Last Point of Supply); and **Phase E** (the markdown — price
leaves the range in a sustained downtrend).

### Intuition
Every Accumulation Schematic concept has a direct, mirrored Distribution
equivalent: where Accumulation's Selling Climax marks capitulation
selling being absorbed, Distribution's Buying Climax marks euphoric
buying being absorbed; where Accumulation's Spring shakes out weak
sellers below support, Distribution's Upthrust traps eager buyers above
resistance; where Accumulation's Sign of Strength confirms markup is
beginning, Distribution's Sign of Weakness confirms markdown is
beginning.

### Mental Model
```
WYCKOFF DISTRIBUTION SCHEMATIC (mirror image of Accumulation)

PHASE A: STOPPING THE UPTREND
  PSY (Preliminary Supply) -> BC (Buying Climax) ->
  AR (Automatic Reaction) -> ST (Secondary Test)

PHASE B: BUILDING THE CAUSE
  Extended range-bound consolidation at ELEVATED prices

PHASE C: THE TEST
  UT (Upthrust) or UTAD (Upthrust After Distribution) --
  deliberate probe ABOVE resistance, trapping eager buyers

PHASE D: EVIDENCE OF WEAKNESS
  SOW (Sign of Weakness -- strong decline through range
  support) -> LPSY (Last Point of Supply -- final rally
  failing at a LOWER level)

PHASE E: THE MARKDOWN
  Price LEAVES the range decisively, sustained downtrend
  begins
```

### Visual Explanation
The Upthrust in Phase C mirrors the Spring's psychology exactly but in
reverse — it appears, in isolation, to be a bullish breakout above
resistance, but within the full schematic context it represents a
deliberate trap designed to lure in late buyers and trigger short-cover
buying, providing exactly the additional demand the Composite Man needs
to sell into before the eventual markdown.

## Real World Analogy
The Distribution Schematic is like a savvy early investor in a
hyped-up, overvalued company quietly selling shares to enthusiastic late
buyers: first absorbing the initial euphoric buying wave (Phase A),
then patiently continuing to distribute shares over an extended period
while public enthusiasm remains high (Phase B), deliberately allowing one
final rally to lure in remaining eager buyers at inflated prices (Phase
C's Upthrust), then beginning to visibly weaken as informed selling
overwhelms remaining demand (Phase D's SOW), before the stock's price
finally declines substantially (Phase E's markdown).

## Indian Market Example
Indian Wyckoff practitioners analyzing a Nifty 50 constituent stock after
a prolonged rally specifically watch for this five-phase mirror sequence
— identifying a Buying Climax on unusually high volume (`[14.05]`),
followed by extended range-bound consolidation at elevated prices, an
Upthrust probing above range resistance, and eventually a Sign of
Weakness decline confirming distribution is likely complete before
considering caution or a short entry near the Last Point of Supply.

## US Market Example
US Wyckoff analysts studying historical US equity bull-market tops
frequently identify textbook Distribution Schematics playing out over
several months before major declines — the Upthrust After Distribution
(UTAD) event in these historical examples is often specifically cited as
the single most decisive, high-confidence signal within the entire
schematic, since it directly confirms the Composite Man's exhaustion of
new demand.

## Historical Example
Wyckoff's original correspondence course materials from the 1930s
(`[17.01]`) formalized this Distribution Schematic alongside its
Accumulation mirror image, explicitly teaching both as complementary,
symmetric frameworks — a structural elegance that has made the paired
schematics particularly memorable and widely taught across nearly a
century of subsequent market education.

## Institutional Perspective
Institutional risk managers specifically monitor for Distribution
Schematic formation in portfolio holdings that have experienced
substantial prior gains — recognizing early-stage Distribution (Phase A's
Buying Climax and Automatic Reaction) can prompt earlier, more measured
profit-taking than waiting for the full schematic's later, more obvious
Phase D confirmation.

## Retail Perspective
Retail traders frequently struggle more with Distribution recognition
than Accumulation recognition, since Distribution occurs during periods
of prevailing bullish sentiment and euphoria — recognizing the Upthrust
(Phase C) as a trap rather than a genuine breakout requires deliberately
overriding the crowd psychology`[13.04]`'s fakeout discussion warned
against, now applied specifically at market tops.

## Professional Insights
Professional Wyckoff practitioners specifically note that Distribution
Schematics, because they typically form during periods of strong positive
sentiment, often generate considerably more false-breakout trading losses
among retail participants than Accumulation Schematics do — making
disciplined Upthrust recognition (`[17.05]`) a particularly valuable,
differentiated skill.

## Common Mistakes
- Treating an Upthrust (Phase C) as a genuine bullish breakout rather
  than recognizing it as a potential distribution-confirming trap.
- Underestimating Distribution risk during periods of strong bullish
  sentiment, when skepticism is psychologically most difficult to
  maintain.
- Waiting for Phase E's obvious markdown before recognizing Distribution,
  missing the more actionable earlier-phase signals.

## Misconceptions
- **"Distribution and Accumulation are unrelated, independent
  concepts."** They are direct mirror images of the same underlying
  Composite Man behavior, applied at opposite market extremes.
- **"An Upthrust is simply a bullish breakout, like any other."** Within
  full schematic context, it frequently represents a deliberate trap
  designed to attract late buyers before markdown — full treatment in
  `[17.05]`.
- **"Distribution is easier to identify than Accumulation."** Many
  practitioners find Distribution genuinely harder to identify in
  real-time, precisely because it forms during periods of strong,
  psychologically compelling bullish sentiment.

## Key Takeaways
- The Distribution Schematic mirrors Accumulation's five phases exactly,
  but at market tops: A (stopping the uptrend), B (building the cause),
  C (the test/Upthrust), D (evidence of weakness), E (the markdown).
- Key Phase A/D events: Preliminary Supply, Buying Climax, Automatic
  Reaction, Secondary Test, Sign of Weakness, Last Point of Supply.
- The Upthrust in Phase C is a deliberate trap for late buyers, not
  simply a bullish breakout — full treatment in `[17.05]`.
- Distribution is often psychologically harder to recognize than
  Accumulation, since it forms during periods of strong bullish sentiment.

## Summary
The Wyckoff Distribution Schematic's five phases — stopping the uptrend,
building the cause at elevated levels, testing for remaining demand,
confirming weakness, and finally marking down — mirror the Accumulation
Schematic (`[17.03]`) exactly, describing the Composite Man's typical
distribution process at market tops. This sets up the next lesson's
detailed, combined treatment of Springs and Upthrusts — the pivotal
testing events at the heart of both schematics — along with the related
concept of Tests more broadly.
