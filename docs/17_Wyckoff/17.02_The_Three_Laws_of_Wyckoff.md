## Core Concepts

### Definition
Wyckoff's entire method rests on **Three Laws**: **(1) The Law of Supply
and Demand** — price rises when demand exceeds supply and falls when
supply exceeds demand, the same underlying logic already introduced via
`[02.01]`'s market structure discussion, now formalized as Wyckoff's
foundational axiom. **(2) The Law of Cause and Effect** — a period of
sustained accumulation or distribution (the "cause," built during a
trading range) produces a proportional subsequent price move (the
"effect") once that range resolves, providing Wyckoff's theoretical basis
for projecting price targets. **(3) The Law of Effort vs. Result** —
comparing volume (effort) against the resulting price change (result)
reveals whether a move is genuine or suspect, directly extending
`[14.01]`'s volume-confirmation philosophy into Wyckoff's specific
analytical framework.

### Intuition
These three laws work together sequentially: the Law of Supply and
Demand explains *why* price moves at all; the Law of Cause and Effect
explains *how far* a move is likely to travel once a range resolves; and
the Law of Effort vs. Result provides an ongoing *diagnostic check* for
whether current price action genuinely reflects the supply/demand
balance the first two laws would predict, or whether something suspect
(low effort producing outsized result, or vice versa) is occurring.

### Mental Model
```
WYCKOFF'S THREE LAWS (working together):

LAW 1: SUPPLY AND DEMAND
  Demand > Supply -> price RISES
  Supply > Demand -> price FALLS
  (explains WHY price moves)

LAW 2: CAUSE AND EFFECT
  Trading range duration/size (CAUSE) ->
  proportional subsequent price move (EFFECT)
  (explains HOW FAR price is likely to move)

LAW 3: EFFORT VS. RESULT
  Compare VOLUME (effort) to PRICE CHANGE (result)
  Aligned effort/result = genuine move
  Misaligned effort/result = SUSPECT move, watch closely
  (explains WHETHER to trust the current move)
```

### Visual Explanation
The Law of Cause and Effect is often visualized using a horizontal
trading range's width (the "cause" built during accumulation or
distribution) as a rough proportional predictor of the subsequent
vertical price move's extent (the "effect") — a wider, longer-duration
range tends to produce a larger subsequent move than a narrow, brief one.

## Real World Analogy
The Three Laws are like evaluating a company's product launch: Law 1
(Supply/Demand) is like observing whether customer demand for the new
product exceeds available supply (driving price up) or vice versa; Law 2
(Cause/Effect) is like recognizing that a longer, more extensive
pre-launch marketing campaign (cause) tends to produce a larger, more
sustained sales surge (effect) than a brief, minimal campaign; Law 3
(Effort/Result) is like noticing when a huge marketing spend (effort)
produces disappointingly weak sales (result) — a red flag suggesting the
campaign's apparent momentum may not be genuine.

## Indian Market Example
Indian Nifty 50 traders applying Wyckoff's Three Laws might observe a
multi-week sideways consolidation (the "cause"), then use its width to
roughly estimate a proportional subsequent breakout target (the
"effect") — while simultaneously watching for Effort vs. Result
divergence (e.g., a breakout day showing high volume but disappointingly
small price progress) as a warning sign of a potentially suspect,
unsustainable move.

## US Market Example
US Wyckoff practitioners analyzing individual stocks specifically use
point-and-figure charting (a technique closely associated with Wyckoff's
original methodology) to quantify the Law of Cause and Effect
mathematically — measuring a trading range's horizontal width in
point-and-figure boxes to project a specific numerical price target for
the subsequent move.

## Historical Example
Wyckoff's Three Laws were formalized as the core theoretical foundation
of his correspondence course material in the 1930s (`[17.01]`), building
directly on his decades of direct ticker-tape observation — these three
laws remain, essentially unchanged, the theoretical bedrock taught in
every subsequent generation of Wyckoff-based market education, including
modern courses and this very lesson.

## Institutional Perspective
Institutional analysts specifically use the Law of Effort vs. Result as
an ongoing diagnostic tool for evaluating whether reported volume
genuinely reflects institutional conviction — a large volume spike (high
effort) producing minimal price progress (weak result) during an
apparent breakout attempt is treated as a significant warning sign,
directly connecting to `[14.05]`'s climactic volume/exhaustion concept.

## Retail Perspective
Retail traders should specifically practice applying all three laws
together on historical charts — identifying a clear trading range,
estimating a rough proportional target using the range's width, and then
checking whether the eventual breakout's volume (effort) aligned with its
price progress (result) — building the integrated diagnostic habit
Wyckoff's method requires.

## Professional Insights
Professional Wyckoff analysts emphasize that the Law of Cause and Effect
provides only a *rough, proportional* estimate, not a precise, guaranteed
target — professional practice always combines this estimate with other
confirmation tools (structure, volume, and the schematic patterns
developed in `[17.03]`-`[17.04]`) rather than treating any single law's
output as a standalone, sufficient trading signal.

## Common Mistakes
- Treating the Law of Cause and Effect's proportional estimate as a
  precise, guaranteed price target.
- Ignoring Effort vs. Result divergence, missing an important warning
  sign of a potentially suspect move.
- Applying only one of the Three Laws in isolation, rather than using
  all three together as an integrated diagnostic framework.

## Misconceptions
- **"The Law of Cause and Effect provides an exact, guaranteed price
  target."** It provides only a rough, proportional estimate, requiring
  combination with other confirmation tools for practical use.
- **"The Law of Supply and Demand is just Wyckoff restating something
  already obvious."** While the underlying concept connects to `[02.01]`'s
  structure discussion, Wyckoff's specific formalization as one of Three
  interconnected Laws provides a uniquely actionable analytical
  framework.
- **"Effort vs. Result divergence always signals an imminent reversal."**
  It signals a *suspect* move worth closer investigation, not an
  automatic, guaranteed reversal signal on its own.

## Key Takeaways
- Law of Supply and Demand: price rises when demand exceeds supply, and
  vice versa — explains why price moves.
- Law of Cause and Effect: a trading range's width/duration (cause)
  roughly predicts the subsequent move's extent (effect).
- Law of Effort vs. Result: comparing volume to price change reveals
  whether a move is genuine or suspect.
- All three laws work together as an integrated diagnostic framework,
  not as independent, standalone tools.

## Summary
Wyckoff's Three Laws — Supply and Demand, Cause and Effect, and Effort
vs. Result — together provide the complete theoretical foundation
underlying every subsequent Wyckoff concept this module develops,
explaining respectively why price moves, how far it's likely to travel,
and whether a given move should be trusted. This sets up the next

## Quiz
1. What is Wyckoff's Law of Supply and Demand?
   a) Price is always random  b) Price rises when demand exceeds supply, and falls when supply exceeds demand  c) Volume never matters  d) Only news moves price

2. What is Wyckoff's Law of Cause and Effect?
   a) Effects never have causes  b) A trading range's width/duration roughly predicts the subsequent move's extent  c) It predicts exact prices with certainty  d) It only applies to bonds

3. What is Wyckoff's Law of Effort vs. Result used for?
   a) Calculating dividends  b) Revealing whether a move is genuine or suspect by comparing volume to price change  c) Determining company valuation  d) Setting interest rates

4. Does the Law of Cause and Effect provide a precise, guaranteed price target?
   a) Yes, always exact  b) No, only a rough, proportional estimate  c) It provides no estimate at all  d) Only for currencies

5. True or False: The Three Laws should be applied independently, never together.
   a) True
   b) False

## Answers
1. **b)** — Price rises when demand exceeds supply, and vice versa.
2. **b)** — A trading range's width/duration roughly predicts the subsequent move's extent.
3. **b)** — It reveals whether a move is genuine or suspect by comparing volume to price change.
4. **b)** — Only a rough, proportional estimate, not a precise guarantee.
5. **b) False** — The Three Laws work together as an integrated diagnostic framework.
