## Core Concepts

### Definition
Within Volume Profile, **Point of Control (POC)** is the single price
level with the highest traded volume. **Value Area High (VAH)** and
**Value Area Low (VAL)** bound the price range containing approximately
**70%** of total session volume, calculated by expanding outward from the
POC — adding the next-highest-volume price levels above and below
alternately (or by row-pair, depending on methodology) until 70% of total
volume is captured. These three levels function as Volume Profile's
direct equivalent to `[15.02]`'s Market Profile POC/VA, with identical
practical trading applications but built from the volume, rather than
time, dataset.

### Intuition
The construction methodology directly parallels `[15.02]`'s Market
Profile approach — starting from the single most significant point
(highest volume, rather than most time) and expanding outward
symmetrically until the 70% statistical threshold is reached, producing
levels that function identically as support/resistance-like reference
points (`[02.04]`), just measured through volume concentration rather
than time concentration.

### Visual Explanation
Like `[15.02]`'s Market Profile levels, these three reference points
inform next-session bias assessment, but calculated from volume
concentration — a price opening within yesterday's Volume Profile Value
Area suggests likely continued rotation, while opening beyond VAH/VAL
suggests potential trend continuation, applying `[15.05]`'s open-type
framework using Volume Profile's specific levels instead.

### Where TPO-POC and Volume-POC Genuinely Disagree
It's easy to read "same construction method, different input variable"
and assume the two POCs are basically interchangeable. They can produce
genuinely different levels for the *identical* session — here is a
concrete case showing exactly how and why.

```
HYPOTHETICAL SESSION -- TWO CANDIDATE PRICE LEVELS

  Level A: Rs 24,050
    - Price returned to this level during 6 separate 30-minute
      TPO periods across the session (drifted back repeatedly,
      without much conviction each time)
    - Total volume traded AT this level across all 6 visits:
      ~3,00,000 shares (modest per visit)

  Level B: Rs 24,070
    - Price only visited this level during 2 TPO periods
    - But one of those visits included a large block trade /
      stop-loss cascade: ~8,00,000 shares traded at this single
      level in that short window

  MARKET PROFILE POC ([15.02], counts TIME):
    Level A wins -- 6 TPO periods beats 2.  TPO-POC = Rs 24,050

  VOLUME PROFILE POC (this lesson, counts VOLUME):
    Level B wins -- 8,00,000 shares beats 3,00,000.
    Volume-POC = Rs 24,070

TWO DIFFERENT, EQUALLY "CORRECT" ANSWERS TO "WHERE IS THE
MARKET'S CENTER OF GRAVITY?" -- DEPENDING ON WHICH QUESTION
YOU'RE ACTUALLY ASKING: "WHERE DID PRICE SPEND THE MOST TIME?"
(TPO) VS. "WHERE DID THE MOST ACTUAL TRADING HAPPEN?" (VOLUME)
```

This is the genuinely different thing about Volume Profile, not a
relabeling of `[15.02]`'s framework: a price level that the market
revisited often but traded thinly (Level A) reflects *time spent
undecided*; a level the market barely revisited but where a large
transaction actually cleared (Level B) reflects *where real capital
committed*. Which one matters more depends on what you're trying to
answer — Volume Profile exists specifically because "most time" and
"most volume" are different questions with different answers.

## Real World Analogy
Building the Volume Profile Value Area is like determining a product's
"core customer price range" by starting from its single best-selling
price point (POC) and progressively adding the next most popular price
points on either side until you've accounted for 70% of all units sold —
revealing the genuine "sweet spot" range where the bulk of real
commercial activity concentrated, distinct from the occasional outlier
sale at an unusual price.

## Indian Market Example
Indian intraday Bank Nifty traders using Volume Profile specifically
calculate POC, VAH, and VAL each session, applying the identical bias
framework from `[15.05]` — a session opening within yesterday's Volume
Profile Value Area suggests range-bound rotation likely, while opening
beyond VAH/VAL suggests potential trend continuation, now using volume-
based rather than time-based reference levels.

## US Market Example
US futures traders using Volume Profile on platforms like TradingView
routinely reference the automatically-calculated POC, VAH, and VAL for
recent sessions as immediate, visually-clear reference levels — Volume
Profile's now-standard availability on most modern charting platforms
has made this specific calculation considerably more accessible than it
was during Market Profile's earlier, more specialized-software era.

## Historical Example
The specific 70% Value Area convention carried over directly from
Steidlmayer's original Market Profile methodology (`[15.02]`) when Volume
Profile emerged as its volume-based sibling framework (`[16.01]`) — a
direct methodological inheritance illustrating Volume Profile's close
conceptual lineage rather than an independently-derived convention.

## Institutional Perspective
Institutional traders using Volume Profile specifically watch for POC
levels that persist across multiple sessions at a similar price — a
"sticky" POC recurring at nearly the same level across several
consecutive sessions is treated as an especially significant, well-
established value reference, similar to `[15.06]`'s composite profile
concept but observed through repeated single-session POC consistency
rather than formal multi-session aggregation.

## Retail Perspective
Retail traders already comfortable with `[15.02]`'s Market Profile
POC/VA concepts will find this lesson's Volume Profile equivalents
immediately familiar — the underlying logic and practical application
transfer directly, with the only meaningful difference being the
underlying measured variable (volume instead of time).

## Professional Insights
Professional Volume Profile analysts specifically note that because
volume data is available at much finer granularity than TPO time periods
(individual trades versus 30-minute blocks), Volume Profile VAH/VAL/POC
calculations can be considerably more precise and less subject to the
somewhat coarser "bucketing" inherent in Market Profile's traditional
30-minute TPO period convention.

## Common Mistakes
- Confusing Volume Profile's POC (highest volume) with Market Profile's
  POC (most time) when switching between the two frameworks.
- Not recognizing when a Volume Profile POC persists across multiple
  sessions, missing a particularly significant, well-established
  reference level.
- Applying the identical bias framework from `[15.05]` without adjusting
  for the fact that Volume Profile levels are calculated differently.

## Misconceptions
- **"VAH/VAL/POC mean exactly the same thing in Volume Profile and
  Market Profile."** They serve the same functional role but are
  calculated from different underlying variables (volume vs. time),
  producing potentially different specific price levels even for the
  identical session.
- **"Volume Profile calculations are always more precise than Market
  Profile."** While often more granular given finer available data,
  precision advantages depend on the specific data quality and
  methodology used, not an absolute, universal rule.
- **"A single session's POC is always highly significant."** As the
  Institutional Perspective notes, POC significance is generally
  strengthened when it persists across multiple sessions, not necessarily
  from any single session's calculation alone.

## Key Takeaways
- Volume Profile's POC, VAH, and VAL directly parallel Market Profile's
  equivalents (`[15.02]`), but are calculated from traded volume rather
  than time spent.
- Construction methodology: expand outward from POC, adding next-highest-
  volume levels alternately, until ~70% of total volume is captured.
- These levels inform next-session bias assessment, directly applying
  `[15.05]`'s open-type framework using volume-based reference levels.
- POC persistence across multiple sessions strengthens its significance
  as a well-established value reference.

## Summary
Volume Profile's POC, VAH, and VAL — calculated by expanding outward
from the highest-volume price level until 70% of total session volume is
captured — directly parallel Market Profile's equivalent concepts
(`[15.02]`) while measuring genuine traded volume rather than time,
informing the identical next-session bias framework covered in `[15.05]`.

Nodes — a Volume Profile-specific vocabulary for describing the profile's
