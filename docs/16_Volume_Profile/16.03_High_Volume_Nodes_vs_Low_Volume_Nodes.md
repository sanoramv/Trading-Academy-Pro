# [16.03] High Volume Nodes vs Low Volume Nodes

> Module: Volume Profile · Lesson 3 of 6 · Estimated time: 30 minutes

## Learning Objectives
- Define High Volume Nodes (HVN) and Low Volume Nodes (LVN) precisely.
- Understand why price tends to move quickly through LVNs and slowly through HVNs.
- Learn to use LVNs as potential support/resistance and target levels.
- Connect this concept to `[13.03]`'s supply/demand zone framework.

## Prerequisites
- `[16.02]` Value Area High, Value Area Low, POC
- `[13.03]` Supply and Demand Zones

## Core Concepts

### Definition
Beyond the single POC, a Volume Profile contains multiple **High Volume
Nodes (HVN)** — local peaks within the profile representing price levels
with significant volume concentration (secondary "mini-POCs" beyond the
main one) — and **Low Volume Nodes (LVN)** — local troughs representing
price levels with comparatively little traded volume, often forming
between two HVNs. This internal profile structure, beyond just the
headline POC/VAH/VAL, provides a richer, more granular map of the
session's (or period's) genuine areas of acceptance and rejection.

### Intuition
HVNs represent genuine areas of sustained two-way acceptance (similar to
POC, but secondary) — price tends to move *slowly* through these zones,
since substantial trading interest exists there. LVNs represent price
levels the market moved through *quickly*, without much genuine two-way
interest — because so little volume traded there, price tends to move
*rapidly* through these zones when revisited, since there's comparatively
little resting interest to slow it down, directly connecting to `[13.03]`'s
supply/demand zone concept (thin consolidation before a sharp departure).

When price approaches an LVN from either direction, it often either
accelerates through it quickly (confirming the "thin," low-interest
nature of that zone) or, less commonly, stalls unexpectedly — the latter
being a notable, information-rich anomaly worth investigating further.

## Real World Analogy
An HVN is like a popular, crowded rest stop on a highway where many
travelers genuinely pause for a meaningful amount of time — traffic
naturally slows through this area given the concentration of activity. An
LVN is like a long, empty stretch of highway between rest stops where
almost no one stops — traffic moves through quickly and smoothly,
precisely because there's little reason for anyone to linger there. A
driver planning a route would naturally expect to move quickly through
the empty stretches and slow down near the popular rest stops.

## Indian Market Example
Indian Bank Nifty traders using Volume Profile specifically identify LVNs
within a developing profile as potential intraday price targets — when
price breaks decisively above an HVN with confirming volume (`[13.04]`),
traders often target the next LVN as a reasonable initial objective,
anticipating relatively quick traversal through that lower-interest zone
before the next HVN potentially provides resistance.

## US Market Example
US futures traders using Volume Profile specifically watch for price
unexpectedly *stalling* within an LVN — since this contradicts the
zone's typical "quick traversal" behavior, an unexpected stall within an
LVN is treated as a notable, information-rich anomaly potentially
signaling genuine new interest developing at an otherwise historically
thin level, worth closer investigation.

## Historical Example
HVN/LVN terminology emerged specifically within the Volume Profile
tradition (`[16.01]`) as practitioners sought vocabulary for describing a
profile's internal structure beyond the single POC — a natural
extension of Steidlmayer's original Market Profile concepts (`[15.01]`)
into more granular, actionable sub-structure as Volume Profile's finer
data granularity (`[16.02]`) made this additional detail practically
useful.

## Institutional Perspective
Institutional traders specifically use LVN identification to help
calibrate execution algorithm parameters (Module 29) — recognizing that
attempting to execute a large order within a thin LVN zone carries higher
market impact risk (less resting liquidity to absorb the order) than
executing within a well-established HVN, directly connecting to `[02.06]`'s
liquidity concept.

## Retail Perspective
Retail traders should specifically practice identifying HVN/LVN
structure within a developing or completed Volume Profile, building the
habit of using LVNs as reasonable price targets and HVNs as potential
support/resistance references — a practical, actionable skill extending
beyond just the headline POC/VAH/VAL levels covered in `[16.02]`.

## Professional Insights
Professional Volume Profile analysts specifically note that HVN/LVN
structure often persists across multiple sessions when a composite
profile (`[15.06]`'s concept, applied to Volume Profile) is built — a
multi-session composite profile's HVN/LVN structure can reveal
particularly durable, longer-term support/resistance and target zones,
more robust than any single session's internal structure alone.

## Common Mistakes
- Ignoring the profile's internal HVN/LVN structure, focusing only on the
  headline POC/VAH/VAL levels.
- Assuming price will always traverse an LVN quickly without checking for
  the notable exception (an unexpected stall signaling new developing
  interest).
- Not connecting LVN identification to `[13.03]`'s supply/demand zone
  concept, missing a valuable cross-framework link.

## Misconceptions
- **"Only the POC matters within a Volume Profile; internal HVN/LVN
  structure is unimportant."** HVN/LVN structure provides genuinely
  valuable, actionable additional detail beyond the headline POC/VAH/VAL
  levels covered in `[16.02]`.
- **"Price always moves quickly through every LVN without exception."**
  An unexpected stall within an LVN is a notable, information-rich
  anomaly worth investigating, not simply an occasional random deviation
  to ignore.
- **"HVN/LVN structure is unrelated to Module 13's supply/demand zone
  concept."** They describe closely related underlying dynamics (areas of
  genuine acceptance versus thin, quickly-traversed zones), viewed through
  Volume Profile's specific vocabulary.

## Key Takeaways
- High Volume Nodes (HVN) represent secondary volume concentration peaks,
  where price tends to slow and potentially reverse; Low Volume Nodes
  (LVN) represent volume troughs, where price tends to move quickly.
- LVNs often function as reasonable price targets; HVNs often function as
  potential support/resistance, similar to POC.
- An unexpected stall within an LVN is a notable anomaly worth
  investigating, not typical behavior.
- This internal profile structure connects directly to `[13.03]`'s
  supply/demand zone concept and `[02.06]`'s liquidity framework.

## Quiz
1. What is a High Volume Node (HVN)?
   a) A volume trough  b) A secondary volume concentration peak where price tends to slow  c) The session's closing price  d) A dividend policy

2. What is a Low Volume Node (LVN)?
   a) A volume peak  b) A volume trough where price tends to move quickly  c) The main POC  d) A type of moving average

3. How is an LVN typically used in trading?
   a) As a guaranteed reversal point  b) As a reasonable initial price target  c) It has no practical use  d) Only for dividend calculations

4. What does an unexpected stall within an LVN suggest?
   a) Nothing significant  b) A notable anomaly, potentially signaling new developing interest  c) A guaranteed reversal  d) A currency devaluation

5. True or False: Price always moves quickly through every LVN without
   exception.
   a) True
   b) False

## Answers
1. **b)** — An HVN is a secondary volume peak where price tends to slow.
2. **b)** — An LVN is a volume trough where price tends to move quickly.
3. **b)** — LVNs are typically used as reasonable initial price targets.
4. **b)** — An unexpected stall within an LVN suggests a notable anomaly worth investigating.
5. **b) False** — An unexpected stall is a notable exception worth investigating, not typical.
