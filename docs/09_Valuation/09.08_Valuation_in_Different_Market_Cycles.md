## Core Concepts

### Definition
**Multiple expansion** occurs when a stock's or market's valuation
multiple (P/E, EV/EBITDA, `[09.02]`) rises independent of underlying
earnings growth — investors are willing to pay more per unit of current
earnings, typically reflecting improving sentiment, falling interest rates
(`[05.04]`), or rising growth expectations. **Multiple contraction** is
the reverse — investors pay less per unit of earnings, typically
reflecting deteriorating sentiment, rising interest rates, or falling
growth expectations. This module's final lesson synthesizes the
discounting mechanism (`[05.04]`), business cycle framework (`[05.08]`),
and growth/value sensitivity (`[09.05]`) into a complete understanding of
why the *same* company's earnings can be valued very differently at
different points in the market cycle.

### Intuition
A stock's price change over any period can be decomposed into two
components: **earnings growth** (the business genuinely growing) and
**multiple change** (the market paying more or less per unit of those
earnings). Confusing these two drivers — attributing a price rise entirely
to genuine business improvement when it was substantially multiple
expansion, or vice versa — leads to systematically mistimed and
mis-analyzed investment decisions, making this decomposition one of the
most practically important skills this entire module builds toward.

### Visual Explanation
The same company's earnings, growing at a steady rate, can produce very
different stock returns depending on whether the market's willingness to
pay for those earnings (the multiple) is simultaneously expanding or
contracting.

### ASCII Diagram
```
   DECOMPOSING STOCK RETURNS: EARNINGS GROWTH vs. MULTIPLE CHANGE

   SCENARIO A (multiple expansion tailwind):
   Earnings grew:        +10%
   P/E multiple:          15x --> 20x  (+33% multiple expansion)
   -----------------------------------------------------------
   Approximate total stock return: +10% + 33% ≈ +43%
   (MOST of the return came from multiple expansion, NOT
    earnings growth -- important to recognize when assessing
    whether this return pattern is repeatable)

   SCENARIO B (multiple contraction headwind):
   Earnings grew:        +10%
   P/E multiple:          20x --> 15x  (-25% multiple contraction)
   -----------------------------------------------------------
   Approximate total stock return: +10% - 25% ≈ -15%
   (Despite GENUINELY GOOD earnings growth, the stock FELL,
    because multiple contraction outweighed earnings growth --
    a pattern that confuses investors focused only on
    "the company is doing well, why is the stock falling?")
```

## Real World Analogy
Multiple expansion and contraction are like the changing "mood" of buyers
at an art auction, independent of the artwork's inherent quality — the
same painting (a company's earnings) might sell for a much higher price
during an exuberant, optimistic auction season (multiple expansion) than
during a subdued, pessimistic one (multiple contraction), even though the
painting itself hasn't changed at all. Understanding whether a price
change reflects the painting's changing quality or the auction's changing
mood is essential for judging whether that price level is likely to
persist.

## Indian Market Example
Indian equity markets have historically shown meaningful **P/E multiple
expansion** during periods of falling interest rates and improving global
risk sentiment (`[06.06]`'s risk-on regime) — such as portions of the
2020-2021 post-COVID recovery — and corresponding **multiple contraction**
during periods of rising rates and risk-off sentiment (portions of 2022),
even when underlying corporate earnings continued growing throughout both
periods, illustrating this lesson's decomposition framework directly
applied to real, recent Indian market history.

## US Market Example
US growth stock multiples (`[09.05]`) expanded dramatically during the
near-zero interest rate environment of 2020-2021, then contracted sharply
during the 2022 Fed rate-hiking cycle (`[05.04]`, `[06.02]`) — with many
individual companies' *earnings* continuing to grow reasonably well
throughout 2022 even as their *stock prices* fell substantially, a
striking real-world illustration of multiple contraction overwhelming
genuine earnings growth, exactly the Scenario B pattern in this lesson's
mental model.

## Historical Example
The **dot-com bubble's** extreme multiple expansion (`[08.06]`, `[09.01]`,
`[09.02]`) followed by its 2000-2002 collapse represents perhaps the most
extreme historical illustration of this lesson's framework — the
subsequent crash was substantially, though not entirely, a multiple
contraction phenomenon (previously-extreme multiples returning toward more
historically normal levels), compounded by genuine earnings disappointment
at many companies that had never achieved the growth their extreme
multiples had priced in.

## Institutional Perspective
Institutional portfolio managers explicitly track **market-wide P/E
multiple levels relative to historical norms** (sometimes using
cyclically-adjusted measures like the Shiller CAPE ratio, which smooths
earnings over a 10-year period to reduce cyclical distortion) as one input
to assessing whether the broader market is trading at an elevated,
depressed, or roughly average multiple relative to history — directly
informing broader asset allocation decisions (Module 27).

## Retail Perspective
Retail investors frequently attribute stock price changes entirely to
"the company doing well" or "the company doing poorly" without
recognizing the substantial role multiple expansion/contraction can
play — leading to confusion when a company with genuinely strong earnings
growth still sees its stock price decline (multiple contraction, as in
2022), or when a company with only modest earnings growth sees a large
stock price gain (multiple expansion).

## Professional Insights
Professional analysts caution that while cycle-driven multiple changes
often mean-revert over sufficiently long horizons, this mean reversion
can take considerably longer than most investors' patience allows, and in
some cases (a genuine structural change in a company or industry, per
`[08.07]`-`[08.09]`) the "old" multiple level may simply no longer be an
appropriate reference point at all — professional practice requires
distinguishing temporary cyclical effects from genuine structural
repricing, not assuming automatic reversion in either direction.

## Common Mistakes
- Attributing stock price changes entirely to earnings growth without
  considering the substantial role of multiple expansion/contraction.
- Assuming historical average multiples remain a valid reference point
  even after genuine structural changes to a company or industry.
- Ignoring the interest rate and business cycle context (Modules 05-06)
  when assessing whether current multiples appear elevated or depressed.

## Misconceptions
- **"Stock price changes always directly and proportionally reflect
  earnings changes."** As the 2022 example illustrates, multiple
  contraction can overwhelm genuine earnings growth, producing falling
  stock prices even for companies with improving fundamentals.
- **"Multiple expansion/contraction is unpredictable noise with no
  systematic drivers."** As this lesson's synthesis demonstrates,
  multiple changes are substantially, if imperfectly, connected to
  identifiable macro factors (interest rates, business cycle phase, risk
  sentiment) developed throughout Modules 05-06.
- **"Once a multiple contracts, it will always eventually mean-revert
  back to its historical average."** Genuine structural changes (moat
  erosion, industry disruption, `[09.06]`'s value trap theme) can mean the
  old multiple level is simply no longer an appropriate reference point.

## Key Takeaways
- Stock returns decompose into earnings growth and multiple expansion/
  contraction — confusing the two leads to systematic analytical errors.
- Multiple expansion/contraction is substantially connected to interest
  rates, business cycle phase, and risk sentiment (Modules 05-06).
- The 2022 rate-hiking cycle offers a clear, recent illustration of
  multiple contraction overwhelming genuine earnings growth.
- Cycle-driven multiple changes often mean-revert over time, but
  structural changes may mean the "old" multiple is no longer valid.

## Quiz
1. How do stock returns approximately decompose?
   a) Purely random noise with no components
   b) Earnings growth plus multiple expansion/contraction
   c) Only dividend payments
   d) Only currency effects

2. What typically drives multiple expansion?
   a) Rising interest rates and deteriorating sentiment
   b) Falling interest rates and improving sentiment (risk-on conditions)
   c) A company going bankrupt
   d) Nothing — multiples never change

3. What did the 2022 Fed rate-hiking cycle illustrate?
   a) Stock prices always rise with earnings growth
   b) Multiple contraction can overwhelm genuine earnings growth, causing prices to fall
   c) Interest rates have no effect on valuations
   d) Only Indian markets were affected

4. Do cycle-driven multiple changes always eventually mean-revert to
   historical averages?
   a) Yes, always guaranteed
   b) Often, but structural changes can invalidate the old reference point
   c) Never, multiples are permanently fixed
   d) Only during recessions

5. True or False: Stock price changes always directly and proportionally
   reflect changes in a company's earnings.
   a) True
   b) False

## Answers
1. **b)** — Stock returns decompose approximately into earnings growth plus multiple expansion/contraction.
2. **b)** — Multiple expansion is typically driven by falling rates and improving sentiment.
3. **b)** — 2022 showed multiple contraction overwhelming genuine earnings growth for many stocks.
4. **b)** — Cycle-driven changes often mean-revert, but structural changes can invalidate the old reference point.
5. **b) False** — Multiple expansion/contraction can cause stock prices to diverge significantly from earnings changes.
