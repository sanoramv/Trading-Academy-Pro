## Core Concepts

### Definition
**Fixed fractional position sizing** risks a constant *percentage* of
current account equity on every trade — this is the exact method
`[25.02]`'s 1% rule already introduced, now formalized as this module's
baseline sizing method. Because it uses *current* equity (not a fixed
starting balance), position size automatically grows as the account
grows and automatically shrinks as the account draws down — a built-in,
self-adjusting property distinguishing it from **fixed unit sizing**
(risking a constant currency amount regardless of account changes).

### Intuition
Recalculating risk as a percentage of *current* equity, rather than a
fixed currency figure set once, means the method compounds gains
(larger account → larger position sizes → larger absolute gains on
continued success) while automatically becoming more conservative
during a drawdown (smaller account → smaller position sizes → smaller
absolute losses if the drawdown continues) — a self-correcting property
directly reinforcing `[25.01]`'s survival-first principle.

### Mental Model
```
FIXED FRACTIONAL FORMULA (recap of [25.02], applied consistently):

  Risk Per Trade (currency) = Current Account Equity x Fixed % (e.g., 1%)
  Position Size = Risk Per Trade (currency) / Stop Distance (currency)

  ILLUSTRATIVE SEQUENCE (1% fixed fractional, starting Rs 10,00,000):

  Trade 1: Equity = Rs 10,00,000. Risk = Rs 10,000. Result: WIN +Rs 20,000
  Trade 2: Equity = Rs 10,20,000. Risk = Rs 10,200 (LARGER, equity grew)
           Result: LOSS -Rs 10,200
  Trade 3: Equity = Rs 10,09,800. Risk = Rs 10,098 (SMALLER, equity fell)

  NOTICE: risk amount AUTOMATICALLY adjusts with equity --
  no manual recalculation decision required each time
```

### Visual Explanation
Charting position size alongside an equity curve using fixed fractional
sizing shows the two lines moving in lockstep — position size expands
during equity growth and contracts during equity decline — a visibly
self-correcting relationship that fixed unit sizing (a flat, unchanging
line regardless of equity) does not produce.

### Flowchart
```
[Before EVERY trade: check CURRENT account equity (not starting
 balance, not yesterday's equity)]
        |
        v
[Calculate Risk Per Trade = Current Equity x Fixed % (e.g., 1%)]
        |
        v
[Calculate Position Size = Risk Per Trade / Stop Distance, [25.03]]
        |
        v
[Execute at this calculated size -- REGARDLESS of recent win/loss
 streak or conviction level, [26.01]]
        |
        v
[After the trade closes, equity has changed -- REPEAT from the top
 using the NEW current equity for the next trade]
```

## Real World Analogy
Fixed fractional sizing is like a farmer who plants a consistent
percentage of this season's harvest as next season's seed — after a
strong harvest, more seed is available and more gets planted; after a
weak harvest, less seed is available and less gets planted
automatically, without the farmer needing to make a fresh, deliberate
decision each season about how much to hold back.

## Indian Market Example
A trader starts with ₹10,00,000 and applies a strict 1% fixed
fractional rule across 20 trend-following trades (`[24.02]`) over a
quarter. After a strong run of wins brings the account to ₹12,00,000,
the same 1% rule now risks ₹12,000 per trade — proportionally larger in
currency terms without any manual adjustment, automatically compounding
the account's growth trajectory. **Hypothetical Example** for
illustration only.

## US Market Example
A trader applies fixed fractional sizing through a drawdown period,
watching account equity fall from $50,000 to $42,000 — the same 1% rule
now risks $420 per trade instead of $500, automatically reducing
absolute risk exposure exactly when the account can least afford
continued losses, without requiring a separate, discretionary decision
to "size down" during the rough patch. **Hypothetical Example** for
illustration only.

## Historical Example
Fixed fractional position sizing is the specific method most directly
associated with Ralph Vince's and later Van Tharp's position sizing
research and writing from the late 1980s and 1990s onward, establishing
it as the standard baseline method taught across most subsequent retail
and professional trading education, including this academy's Module
25/26 sequence.

## Institutional Perspective
Institutional risk limits often function on essentially the same fixed
fractional principle at the portfolio level — maximum risk per position
expressed as a percentage of current assets under management (AUM)
rather than a fixed currency figure, automatically scaling as the fund's
total capital changes.

## Retail Perspective
Retail traders sometimes default to fixed unit sizing (e.g., "I always
buy 100 shares") out of simplicity, missing that this method fails to
compound gains or automatically reduce risk during drawdowns the way
fixed fractional sizing does — a meaningful, often overlooked structural
difference.

## Professional Insights
Professional traders note that fixed fractional sizing's automatic
drawdown-reduction property directly complements `[25.05]`'s maximum
drawdown discussion — the method inherently slows the rate of further
loss during a losing streak, without requiring a separate, manually
triggered "reduce size" rule, though `[25.08]`'s rulebook may still add
additional manual drawdown-response triggers on top of this automatic effect.

## Common Mistakes
- Using fixed unit sizing (constant currency amount) while believing
  it's equivalent to fixed fractional sizing.
- Forgetting to recalculate position size based on *current* equity
  before each new trade, instead using a stale, earlier equity figure.
- Assuming fixed fractional sizing alone is sufficient without also
  applying `[25.03]`'s structural stop placement and `[25.06]`'s
  correlation awareness.

## Misconceptions
- **"Fixed fractional and fixed unit sizing are the same thing."** Fixed
  fractional recalculates from current equity each trade; fixed unit
  uses a constant currency figure regardless of equity changes —
  a meaningfully different long-run behavior.
- **"Fixed fractional sizing guarantees compounding gains."** It
  compounds gains *if* the strategy has positive expectancy — it does
  not create profitability on its own, only scales outcomes proportionally.
- **"Fixed fractional sizing is too simple to be a serious professional
  method."** It remains one of the most widely used baseline methods
  across both retail and institutional risk frameworks, precisely for
  its self-correcting simplicity.

## Key Takeaways
- Fixed fractional sizing risks a constant percentage of *current*
  equity on every trade, automatically compounding gains and reducing
  size during drawdowns.
- Distinct from fixed unit sizing, which uses a constant currency
  amount regardless of equity changes.
- Position size must be recalculated from current equity before every
  single trade, not set once and left static.
- Rooted in Ralph Vince's and Van Tharp's position sizing research from
  the late 1980s–1990s onward.

## Summary
Fixed fractional position sizing — risking a constant percentage of
current equity on every trade — is this module's baseline method,
automatically compounding gains during winning periods and reducing
exposure during drawdowns without requiring separate manual
adjustments, distinguishing it meaningfully from simpler fixed unit
sizing. The next lesson builds on this foundation with volatility-based
(ATR) position sizing, which adjusts size further based on an
instrument's specific, current volatility characteristics.

## Answers
1. **b)** — A constant percentage of current account equity.
2. **b)** — Fixed fractional recalculates from current equity; fixed unit uses a constant currency amount.
3. **b)** — It shrinks automatically, since current equity has fallen.
4. **b)** — Ralph Vince and Van Tharp.
5. **b) False** — It scales outcomes proportionally but doesn't create profitability on its own.
