## Core Concepts

### Definition
**Position sizing** is the specific discipline of determining exactly
how many shares, lots, or contracts to trade on any given position —
distinct from *strategy* (which decides entry/exit signals, Module 24)
and distinct from *risk-per-trade principles* (Module 25's "how much to
risk," expressed as a percentage). Position sizing is the mathematical
bridge connecting the two: it converts a risk-per-trade percentage and
a stop-loss distance into an actual, executable number of units to trade.

### Intuition
Two traders using the *identical* entry signal, exit signal, and stop-
loss placement can produce dramatically different long-run outcomes
purely from differing position sizing rules — one risking a consistent,
disciplined 1% per trade, the other risking 10% or varying their size
based on gut feeling. Van Tharp's research and trading education,
referenced throughout this academy, has long argued that position
sizing — not entry timing or exit precision — is the single largest
lever determining whether a strategy's tested expectancy (`[24.01]`)
actually translates into long-run account growth or ruin.

### Mental Model
```
THE THREE SEPARATE DECISIONS EVERY TRADE REQUIRES:
  1. STRATEGY (Module 24): WHEN to enter, WHERE to exit (signal-based)
  2. RISK PRINCIPLE (Module 25): HOW MUCH to risk, as a % of capital
  3. POSITION SIZING (this module): the EXACT FORMULA converting
     #2 into an actual number of units/contracts for THIS trade

SAME STRATEGY, DIFFERENT SIZING -> VERY DIFFERENT OUTCOMES:
  Trader A: consistent 1% risk per trade, every trade
  Trader B: SAME entries/exits, but risks 1-15% inconsistently
            based on "conviction"

  Over 100 identical SIGNAL-based trades, Trader A's outcome is
  far more likely to reflect the underlying strategy's TRUE
  expectancy, [24.01] -- Trader B's outcome is dominated by
  the VARIANCE their inconsistent sizing introduces
```

## Real World Analogy
Position sizing is like a factory's quality control process running the
exact same inspection criteria on every single unit, regardless of how
the inspector "feels" about a particular batch — consistency in the
process, not variability based on gut instinct, is precisely what makes
the factory's overall defect rate match its tested, expected rate over
time, rather than swinging unpredictably based on inspector mood.

## Indian Market Example
Two traders both use the identical Nifty trend-following signal
(`[24.02]`) across the same year of trades. Trader A applies a
consistent 1% risk-per-trade formula every time; Trader B uses the same
signals but sizes trades based on how confident each setup "feels,"
occasionally risking 8-10% on high-conviction trades — by year-end,
Trader A's results closely track the strategy's backtested expectancy,
while Trader B's results are dominated by a few oversized trades that
happened to lose. **Hypothetical Example** for illustration only.

## US Market Example
A quantitative researcher backtests an SPY strategy and finds strong
positive expectancy, then separately simulates the same trade sequence
under both consistent 1% sizing and inconsistent, conviction-based
sizing — demonstrating in the simulation that the consistent-sizing
version's terminal equity closely matches the theoretical expectancy,
while the inconsistent version's terminal equity varies wildly across
different simulation runs. **Hypothetical Example** for illustration only.

## Historical Example
Van Tharp's research, published extensively from the 1990s onward and
referenced repeatedly throughout this academy (`[24.01]`, `[25.01]`,
`[25.02]`), used simulation studies specifically demonstrating that
position sizing — more than entry technique — determined the range of
possible outcomes for traders using identical entry signals, a finding
that shifted significant emphasis in retail trading education toward
sizing discipline from the 1990s forward.

## Institutional Perspective
Institutional trading desks formalize position sizing into automated,
rules-based systems specifically to remove the conviction-based
inconsistency this lesson warns against — position size for any given
trade is calculated by a formula, not a trader's individual, in-the-
moment judgment about how good a particular setup feels.

## Retail Perspective
Retail traders disproportionately focus on finding better entry
signals or indicators, while underinvesting attention in position
sizing consistency — yet the historical and simulation evidence
referenced throughout this academy points to sizing discipline as at
least as important, and arguably more important, than entry precision alone.

## Professional Insights
Professional traders often describe position sizing as "the only thing
you can fully control on every single trade" — echoing `[25.01]`'s
framing — since market direction, volatility, and even a signal's
success are all partially outside the trader's control, while the sizing
decision is entirely within it, every time.

## Common Mistakes
- Sizing trades based on subjective conviction rather than a consistent, formula.
- Believing entry/exit signal quality alone determines trading success,
  underweighting the sizing decision.
- Applying a sizing formula inconsistently — using it for some trades
  but overriding it for "special" setups.

## Misconceptions
- **"Position sizing is a minor implementation detail after the real
  decisions (entry/exit) are made."** Research and simulation evidence
  referenced throughout this academy suggests sizing consistency is at
  least as consequential as signal quality for long-run outcomes.
- **"A trader with high conviction should size larger on that specific
  trade."** This reintroduces the inconsistency that undermines
  expectancy realization — conviction-based sizing is a documented
  source of poor long-run outcomes, not an edge.
- **"Position sizing and risk-per-trade (Module 25) are the same
  thing."** Risk-per-trade is the *principle* (how much, as a %);
  position sizing is the *formula* that converts that principle into an
  actual, executable trade size.

## Key Takeaways
- Position sizing is the formula converting a risk-per-trade principle
  (Module 25) into an actual number of units/contracts to trade.
- Two traders using identical entries/exits can produce very different
  outcomes purely from position sizing consistency (or lack thereof).
- Van Tharp's research (1990s onward) provides the historical foundation
  for treating sizing as at least as consequential as entry/exit signal quality.
- Consistent, formula-based sizing — applied without exception for
  "high-conviction" trades — is essential to letting a strategy's tested
  expectancy actually materialize.

## Summary
Position sizing — the specific formula converting Module 25's risk-
per-trade principle into an actual, executable trade size — is what
Van Tharp's research and decades of subsequent trading education have
argued is the real determinant of long-run trading outcomes, more so
than entry/exit signal quality alone, precisely because inconsistent,
conviction-based sizing introduces variance that can overwhelm even a
genuinely positive-expectancy strategy. The next lesson begins covering
specific sizing formulas in detail, starting with the simplest and most
widely used: fixed fractional position sizing.
