## Core Concepts

### Definition
**Positional (momentum) investing** holds positions for months to
years, combining fundamental quality screening (`[08.10]`) with
technical momentum confirmation to identify stocks likely to continue
outperforming over an extended horizon. A complete framework specifies:
a fundamental filter (quality/growth criteria), a momentum filter
(e.g., price above a long-term moving average and outperforming a
benchmark index over the trailing 6–12 months), a position review
schedule (monthly/quarterly rather than daily), and exit rules tied to
either a fundamental deterioration or a sustained momentum breakdown.

### Intuition
Where trend-following (`[24.02]`) reacts purely to price structure over
weeks, positional/momentum investing operates on a longer horizon and
adds a fundamental quality filter — betting not just that "this has
been going up" but that "this is a genuinely strong business whose
stock has also been outperforming," reducing exposure to momentum in
weak or deteriorating businesses.

### Mental Model
```
POSITIONAL/MOMENTUM FRAMEWORK:
  1. FUNDAMENTAL FILTER: Quality/growth screen, [08.10]
     (e.g., consistent earnings growth, reasonable debt levels)
  2. MOMENTUM FILTER: Price above rising 200-day MA, [10.06], AND
     outperforming the benchmark index over trailing 6-12 months
  3. ENTRY: On a pullback within the established momentum trend,
     or on initial qualification if already trending strongly
  4. REVIEW SCHEDULE: Monthly/quarterly re-screening -- NOT daily
  5. EXIT: Fundamental deterioration (screen fails) OR sustained
     momentum breakdown (price closes below the 200-day MA for an
     extended period) -- whichever comes first
```

### Visual Explanation
A positional/momentum portfolio's chart typically shows a small number
of large, extended-duration holdings rather than frequent trades — each
position's chart resembling an extended version of `[24.02]`'s trend-
following pattern, but re-evaluated against fundamental criteria at each
periodic review rather than purely technical trailing-stop logic alone.

## Real World Analogy
Positional/momentum investing is like scouting and backing a promising
long-distance runner mid-race: rather than betting on every runner who
briefly surges ahead (pure momentum without fundamentals), the investor
first confirms the runner has genuine underlying conditioning
(fundamentals) *and* is currently pulling ahead of the pack
(momentum) — then stays with that runner for the length of the race,
checking in periodically rather than watching every stride.

## Indian Market Example
An investor screens NSE-listed companies for consistent 3-year earnings
growth and manageable debt (`[08.10]`), then filters further for stocks
trading above their rising 200-day moving average and outperforming
Nifty over the trailing year — building a concentrated positional
portfolio reviewed quarterly, holding individual positions for 12–24
months on average. **Hypothetical Example** for illustration only.

## US Market Example
An investor applies the same combined fundamental-plus-momentum
screening process to S&P 500 constituents, holding qualifying positions
through normal volatility as long as both the fundamental and momentum
criteria continue to hold at each quarterly review, exiting only on
clear deterioration in either dimension. **Hypothetical Example** for
illustration only.

## Historical Example
Momentum as a documented market anomaly was formally established in
academic finance literature by Jegadeesh and Titman's influential 1993
study, showing that stocks with strong trailing 3–12 month performance
tended to continue outperforming over the subsequent 3–12 months — a
finding that has since been replicated across many markets and time
periods and remains one of the most robust documented anomalies in
empirical finance research.

## Institutional Perspective
Institutional momentum strategies are a well-established, formally
recognized "factor" (Module 30 previewed) alongside value, quality, and
low-volatility factors — many institutional funds run systematic,
rules-based momentum strategies at scale, directly informed by the
Jegadeesh-Titman research tradition.

## Retail Perspective
Retail positional investors benefit from this framework's lower time
commitment (monthly/quarterly review versus daily monitoring) compared
to swing trading or scalping, making it well-suited to investors
building wealth alongside a full-time career, though it requires
patience through normal interim volatility that shorter-holding-period
traders would react to more quickly.

## Professional Insights
Professional momentum investors emphasize combining momentum with a
fundamental quality filter specifically to avoid "momentum crashes" —
periods, well-documented in academic literature, where pure price
momentum (without fundamental support) can reverse sharply and
severely, particularly following market stress events.

## Common Mistakes
- Chasing pure price momentum without any fundamental quality filter,
  increasing exposure to momentum crash risk.
- Reviewing positions too infrequently, missing genuine fundamental
  deterioration that should trigger an exit.
- Reviewing positions too frequently (daily), undermining the
  strategy's intended longer holding period with premature, noise-
  driven exits.

## Misconceptions
- **"Momentum investing means chasing whatever is going up regardless
  of fundamentals."** This lesson's framework explicitly combines
  momentum with a fundamental quality filter to reduce that specific risk.
- **"Momentum is just a shorter version of trend-following."** They
  share conceptual roots, but momentum investing typically operates on
  a longer horizon and adds fundamental screening trend-following
  (`[24.02]`) does not require.
- **"Academic momentum research is purely theoretical, with no
  practical trading relevance."** Jegadeesh and Titman's findings
  directly underlie widely used institutional momentum factor strategies.

## Key Takeaways
- Positional/momentum investing holds for months to years, combining
  fundamental screening (`[08.10]`) with technical momentum confirmation.
- Reviewed monthly/quarterly, not daily — the module's longest holding
  period and lowest monitoring frequency.
- Momentum is a well-documented academic anomaly (Jegadeesh & Titman,
  1993), now a recognized institutional factor strategy.
- Combining momentum with fundamental quality filters specifically
  reduces exposure to well-documented "momentum crash" risk.
