## Core Concepts

### Definition
**Scalping** is the fastest-paced trading style covered in this module,
holding positions for seconds to a few minutes, aiming to capture very
small price movements repeated many times per session. A complete
scalping framework specifies: a highly liquid instrument (tight bid-ask
spread, `[01.10]`), a fast execution method (limit or market orders,
`[01.07]`, chosen for speed and cost), a very tight, mechanical stop-
loss, a small, fixed profit target, and strict daily loss limits given
the high trade frequency.

### Intuition
Where swing trading (`[24.06]`) tolerates noise over days, scalping
operates at a timescale where transaction costs (spread, brokerage) and
execution speed become primary determinants of profitability — a
strategy that would be profitable at swing-trading position sizes can
become unprofitable at scalping frequency purely from costs compounding
across dozens of daily trades.

### Mental Model
```
SCALPING FRAMEWORK:
  1. INSTRUMENT SELECTION: Highest liquidity only (tight spread,
     high volume, [01.10]) -- illiquid instruments make scalping
     costs prohibitive
  2. ENTRY: Very short-timeframe signal (1-min/5-min chart), often
     using order flow/tape reading beyond this lesson's scope
  3. STOP-LOSS: Very TIGHT, mechanical, no discretion -- often a few
     ticks/points only
  4. TARGET: Small, FIXED -- often similar magnitude to the stop
     (near 1:1 risk-reward, relying on HIGH WIN RATE and HIGH
     TRADE FREQUENCY for positive expectancy, [24.01])
  5. DAILY LIMITS: Strict maximum loss per day given how many trades
     compound risk exposure within a single session
```

### Visual Explanation
A scalper's chart shows dozens of very short trades clustered within a
single session, each capturing a small move — unlike swing trading's
chart, where a handful of trades unfold over days, scalping's edge
depends on volume of repetition, tight execution, and minimal cost
leakage per trade rather than any single trade's magnitude.

## Real World Analogy
Scalping is like a market vendor making many small-margin sales
throughout a busy day rather than a few large-margin sales — profitable
only if the cost of doing business (rent, spoilage — the equivalent of
spread and brokerage) stays low relative to each individual sale's
margin, since those costs compound quickly across a high volume of
transactions.

## Indian Market Example
A scalper trades Nifty futures (`[20.01]`) exclusively, given their deep
liquidity and tight spreads relative to individual stock futures,
taking numerous small trades throughout the morning session using a
fixed 5-point stop and 6-point target, with a strict daily loss limit of
a predefined number of points before stopping entirely. **Hypothetical
Example** for illustration only.

## US Market Example
A scalper trades SPY or highly liquid mega-cap stocks exclusively for
the same reason — deep liquidity keeps spread costs minimal relative to
each trade's small target, an essential requirement given how many
trades are taken per session. **Hypothetical Example** for illustration
only.

## Historical Example
Scalping predates electronic markets, historically practiced by floor
traders and market makers on physical exchanges who profited from the
bid-ask spread directly; the strategy's modern retail form was enabled
specifically by the rise of low-latency electronic trading platforms
and direct market access from the late 1990s and 2000s onward, which
made the execution speed scalping requires accessible beyond
professional floor traders.

## Institutional Perspective
Institutional scalping today is dominated by high-frequency trading
(HFT) firms (`[29.08]` previewed) using automated systems operating at
microsecond speeds — a scale and speed retail discretionary scalpers
cannot realistically compete with directly, meaning retail scalping
success depends on different, complementary edges (discretion, reading
specific order flow patterns) rather than pure speed.

## Retail Perspective
Retail scalping is widely regarded, including by many experienced
traders and educators, as one of the hardest trading styles to execute
profitably given brokerage costs, competition from institutional HFT,
and the intense focus/speed required — most retail education resources
suggest scalping is unsuitable for beginners and requires substantial
prior screen-time experience before attempting live.

## Professional Insights
Professional scalpers emphasize that transaction cost awareness (spread,
brokerage, any applicable transaction taxes such as India's Securities
Transaction Tax) must be built directly into the expectancy calculation
(`[24.01]`) — a strategy that looks profitable ignoring costs can turn
unprofitable once realistic costs are included at scalping frequency.

## Common Mistakes
- Scalping illiquid instruments where spread costs alone erode most or
  all of the target profit margin.
- Ignoring cumulative transaction costs across many daily trades when
  evaluating whether the strategy is actually profitable.
- Abandoning the strict daily loss limit after a string of losses,
  attempting to "make it back" within the same session (revenge
  trading, Module 28 previewed).

## Misconceptions
- **"Scalping is an easy way to make quick, small, safe profits."** It
  is widely regarded as one of the hardest styles to execute
  profitably, given costs, competition, and required speed/discipline.
- **"Any liquid-looking stock is suitable for scalping."** Scalping
  demands the *deepest* liquidity available, not merely reasonable
  liquidity — spread costs compound quickly at this frequency.
- **"Scalping and day trading are the same thing."** Scalping is one
  specific, very short-holding-period style *within* the broader
  category of day (intraday) trading, not a synonym for it.

## Key Takeaways
- Scalping holds positions for seconds to minutes, requiring the
  deepest liquidity and tightest execution of any style in this module.
- Small, fixed targets near 1:1 risk-reward rely on high win rate and
  high trade frequency for positive expectancy (`[24.01]`).
- Transaction costs must be explicitly built into expectancy — they can
  turn an apparently profitable strategy unprofitable at this frequency.
- Widely regarded as unsuitable for beginners given required speed,
  discipline, and competition from institutional HFT.

## Summary
Scalping captures very small, frequent price movements using the
tightest execution, stops, and targets of any style in this module,
demanding the deepest liquidity available and rigorous cost awareness
given how quickly transaction costs compound across dozens of daily
trades — a style widely regarded as among the hardest to execute
profitably, particularly for beginners. The next lesson turns to
positional/momentum investing — the module's longest holding-period
framework, sitting at the opposite end of the spectrum from scalping.

## Quiz
1. What is the typical holding period for a scalping trade?
   a) Days to weeks  b) Seconds to a few minutes  c) Months to years  d) One full trading session

2. What instrument characteristic is mandatory for scalping?
   a) Low liquidity  b) Deep liquidity, tight bid-ask spread  c) High dividend yield  d) Long expiry options only

3. Why must transaction costs be explicitly factored into scalping's expectancy?
   a) They don't matter at this frequency  b) They compound quickly across many trades and can erase profitability  c) Only applies to futures  d) Brokers waive fees for scalpers

4. Who dominates scalping-speed trading at the institutional level today?
   a) Retail day traders  b) High-frequency trading (HFT) firms  c) Long-term mutual funds  d) Pension funds

5. True or False: Scalping is widely considered an easy strategy well-suited to beginners.
   a) True
   b) False

## Answers
1. **b)** — Seconds to a few minutes.
2. **b)** — Deep liquidity with a tight bid-ask spread is mandatory.
3. **b)** — Costs compound quickly across many trades and can erase profitability.
4. **b)** — High-frequency trading (HFT) firms dominate at institutional scale.
5. **b) False** — It is widely regarded as one of the hardest styles, unsuitable for beginners.
