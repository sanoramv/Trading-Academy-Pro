## Core Concepts

### Definition
**Event-driven trading** builds strategies specifically around known,
scheduled catalysts — corporate earnings releases, the Indian Union
Budget, RBI Monetary Policy Committee meetings, and US Federal Reserve
(FOMC) meetings — rather than trading continuously regardless of
calendar context. A complete framework specifies: the event calendar
being tracked, a pre-event positioning rule (directional bet, volatility
bet via straddle/strangle `[21.09]`, or explicitly standing aside), an
IV-awareness check (`[23.01]`) before entry, and a defined post-event
exit rule given how quickly conditions (especially IV) shift once the
event resolves.

### Intuition
Scheduled events concentrate uncertainty into a specific date, which in
turn concentrates elevated implied volatility (`[23.01]`) into the
period leading up to that date — traders can choose to bet on the
event's *direction*, bet on the event's *magnitude* (regardless of
direction, via straddles/strangles), or deliberately avoid the
elevated-IV, elevated-risk window entirely, but all three are
conscious choices this framework requires making explicitly rather than
trading through the event unprepared.

### Mental Model
```
EVENT-DRIVEN FRAMEWORK:
  1. TRACK the calendar: earnings dates, RBI MPC/Union Budget, [03.10];
     FOMC meetings, [06.02]
  2. CHECK current IV relative to recent range BEFORE the event,
     [23.01]/[23.05] -- is uncertainty already priced in?
  3. CHOOSE an approach:
     a) DIRECTIONAL: enter a position betting on the event's outcome
        direction (higher risk if wrong)
     b) VOLATILITY: straddle/strangle betting on MAGNITUDE regardless
        of direction, [21.09] -- must overcome IV crush risk, [22.05]
     c) STAND ASIDE: explicitly avoid the event window, especially if
        IV is already extremely elevated (uncertainty largely priced in)
  4. POST-EVENT: exit/reassess QUICKLY -- IV crush and the resolved
     catalyst both change conditions rapidly once the event has passed
```

### Visual Explanation
Implied volatility on an event-linked option typically rises steadily
in the days leading up to the scheduled date, peaks just before the
announcement, and drops sharply (IV crush, `[22.05]`) immediately after
— regardless of which direction the underlying actually moves — a
pattern this lesson's framework must explicitly account for in both
directional and volatility-based approaches.

## Real World Analogy
Event-driven trading is like preparing for a scheduled referendum whose
outcome is genuinely uncertain: some observers place a directional bet
on the outcome, others bet purely on how dramatically markets will react
regardless of which way the vote goes, and cautious observers simply
wait until the result and its immediate aftermath are known before
acting at all — all three are legitimate, deliberate choices, unlike
being caught unprepared when the result is announced.

## Indian Market Example
Ahead of a Union Budget announcement (`[03.10]`), a trader checks Nifty
option IV and finds it already elevated well above its trailing 20-day
average, concluding that much of the expected uncertainty is already
priced in — and instead of buying a straddle, considers a defined-risk
premium-selling structure (`[21.10]`, `[24.09]`) to benefit from the
IV crush expected once the Budget is delivered. **Hypothetical Example**
for illustration only.

## US Market Example
Ahead of a company's quarterly earnings release, a trader confirms the
stock has historically moved by an average of 6% on earnings day, then
checks whether the current straddle's combined premium implies a
smaller expected move than that historical average — entering the
straddle only if the options market appears to be underpricing the
event's likely magnitude. **Hypothetical Example** for illustration only.

## Historical Example
The Reserve Bank of India's May 2022 surprise rate hike (referenced in
`[03.10]`) illustrates event-driven risk concretely: a policy decision
outside the regularly scheduled MPC calendar produced a sharp, largely
unanticipated market reaction — a reminder that even well-tracked event
calendars can be supplemented by genuinely unscheduled catalysts that
this framework's calendar-tracking step cannot fully anticipate.

## Institutional Perspective
Institutional event-driven desks often specialize specifically in
volatility-based (straddle/strangle-style) approaches around major
macro catalysts, employing dedicated analysts to forecast whether
current implied volatility over- or under-prices the likely realized
move — a more resource-intensive version of this lesson's IV-awareness check.

## Retail Perspective
Retail traders should treat event-driven windows as periods requiring
explicit, deliberate strategy choice rather than trading through them
with a standard, calendar-agnostic approach — `[21.12]`'s IV crush
warning applies with particular force to unprepared retail option
buyers entering just before scheduled events.

## Professional Insights
Professional event-driven traders track not just the event date itself
but the *historical pattern* of IV behavior around similar past events
for the same underlying — some stocks/indices consistently see larger
or smaller-than-average IV run-ups ahead of their specific recurring
events, information that refines the IV-awareness check beyond a single
snapshot.

## Common Mistakes
- Buying straddles/strangles right before an event without checking
  whether IV is already elevated relative to the event's typical
  historical pattern.
- Holding a position unmanaged through the event without a predefined
  post-event exit plan.
- Ignoring genuinely unscheduled catalysts (like the May 2022 RBI
  surprise hike) that fall outside the standard tracked calendar.

## Misconceptions
- **"Trading around known events guarantees a profitable opportunity."**
  The market often prices in known events efficiently — an elevated,
  already-priced-in IV can make even a correct directional or magnitude
  bet unprofitable after costs.
- **"IV crush only matters for straddle/strangle buyers."** Directional
  option buyers around events face the same IV crush risk on their
  single-leg positions, per `[22.05]`.
- **"Every major market catalyst is on a known, trackable calendar."**
  Some catalysts (like a surprise policy move) are genuinely
  unscheduled and cannot be fully anticipated by calendar-tracking alone.

## Key Takeaways
- Event-driven trading requires a deliberate, explicit choice: directional
  bet, volatility (magnitude) bet, or standing aside — not defaulting
  into the event unprepared.
- Checking current IV relative to its recent range and historical
  event-specific pattern is essential before any event-driven entry.
- IV crush affects both directional and volatility-based option
  positions around events, per `[22.05]`.
- Not every catalyst is scheduled — genuinely surprise events (like May
  2022's RBI hike) remain a residual risk even with careful calendar tracking.

## Summary
Event-driven trading concentrates strategy decisions around known,
scheduled catalysts — earnings, the Union Budget, RBI/Fed meetings —
requiring an explicit choice between directional, volatility-based, or
stand-aside approaches, always with careful attention to IV crush risk
given how sharply implied volatility typically resolves once the event
passes. The next lesson turns from individual strategy frameworks to
combining multiple strategies together into a diversified portfolio of
systems.

## Quiz
1. What three deliberate choices does event-driven trading require?
   a) Only directional bets  b) Directional, volatility, or standing aside  c) Only standing aside  d) Ignoring the event entirely

2. What risk affects both directional and volatility-based option positions around scheduled events?
   a) Interest rate risk only  b) IV crush  c) Dividend risk  d) Currency risk only

3. What should be checked before an event-driven options entry?
   a) Nothing, just enter  b) Current IV relative to its recent range and historical pattern  c) Only the strike price  d) Only the expiry date

4. What is an example of a genuinely unscheduled market catalyst mentioned in this lesson?
   a) A routine quarterly earnings release  b) The RBI's May 2022 surprise rate hike  c) A regularly scheduled Union Budget  d) A weekly options expiry

5. True or False: Known, scheduled events always present a profitable trading opportunity.
   a) True
   b) False

## Answers
1. **b)** — Directional bet, volatility bet, or standing aside.
2. **b)** — IV crush affects both approaches.
3. **b)** — Current IV relative to its recent range and historical event-specific pattern.
4. **b)** — The RBI's May 2022 surprise rate hike, outside the regular MPC calendar.
5. **b) False** — Markets often price in known events efficiently, and already-elevated IV can make even correct bets unprofitable after costs.
