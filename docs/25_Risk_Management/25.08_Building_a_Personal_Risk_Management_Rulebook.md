## Core Concepts

### Definition
A **personal risk management rulebook** is a single, written document
specifying every risk rule a trader commits to following, covering
position sizing, stop-loss methodology, risk-reward minimums, maximum
drawdown response, correlation/cluster limits, and tail-risk safeguards
— synthesizing this entire module into one reference a trader consults
*before* entering any trade, not improvised in the moment under
pressure. Writing rules down in advance, before emotional pressure or
market noise can distort judgment, is the discipline that turns this
module's individual lessons into consistently applied practice.

### Intuition
Every rule in this module (`[25.01]`–`[25.07]`) is straightforward to
understand intellectually but genuinely difficult to follow consistently
in the heat of a live, moving market — a written rulebook, consulted and
committed to in advance, removes the need to make sound risk decisions
under the exact conditions (fear, greed, uncertainty) most likely to
produce poor ones, directly complementing Module 28's psychology discussion.

### Mental Model
```
PERSONAL RISK MANAGEMENT RULEBOOK -- TEMPLATE SECTIONS:

  1. PER-TRADE RISK LIMIT: [25.02]
     "I risk no more than __% of capital on any single trade."

  2. STOP-LOSS METHODOLOGY: [25.03]
     "Stops are placed at [structural level type], cross-checked
      against __x ATR. I do NOT move a stop further from entry
      once placed."

  3. MINIMUM RISK-REWARD RATIO: [25.04]
     "I only take trades with at least a __:__ risk-reward ratio,
      using REALISTIC structural targets."

  4. MAXIMUM DRAWDOWN RESPONSE: [25.05]
     "If my account drawdown reaches __%, I will [reduce position
      size by __% / stop trading and review / other predefined action]."

  5. CORRELATION/CLUSTER LIMIT: [25.06]
     "Total risk within any single correlation cluster (sector/macro
      driver) will not exceed __% of capital."

  6. TAIL-RISK SAFEGUARDS: [25.07]
     "I will not use leverage exceeding __x. I maintain a minimum
      cash/liquidity reserve of __%."

  7. REVIEW SCHEDULE: [24.12]
     "This rulebook is reviewed every [monthly/quarterly] based on
      journaled, statistically meaningful data -- not emotional
      reaction to recent trades."
```

every single time, regardless of experience level.

## Real World Analogy
A personal risk rulebook is like a ship captain's standing orders,
written and posted before any storm arrives: when conditions turn
chaotic and fast decisions are needed, the crew doesn't debate
philosophy in the moment — they execute the predetermined orders,
precisely because those orders were reasoned through calmly, in
advance, when clear thinking was still possible.

## Indian Market Example
A trader completes this module and drafts a rulebook specifying: 1%
risk per trade, structural stops cross-checked against 2x ATR, minimum
1:2 risk-reward, a rule to cut position sizes in half if account
drawdown exceeds 15%, a maximum 3% combined risk across any single
sector cluster (e.g., banking stocks), and no leverage exceeding 3x on
F&O positions — reviewing and refining these specific numbers quarterly
based on journaled results (`[24.12]`). **Hypothetical Example** for
illustration only.

## US Market Example
A trader builds a similar rulebook for a US equity/options portfolio,
adding a specific tail-risk safeguard following `[25.07]`'s discussion:
maintaining a minimum 20% cash reserve at all times, specifically
intended to provide both psychological resilience and tactical
flexibility during a genuine black-swan-scale event, rather than being
fully invested at all times. **Hypothetical Example** for illustration only.

## Historical Example
Professional trading firms and disciplined individual traders alike
have long emphasized written trading plans and risk rules as a
distinguishing feature between durable, long-term market participants
and those who exit the markets after a single severe drawdown — a
theme running consistently through the trading psychology and money
management literature referenced throughout this module (Elder, Tharp,
Taleb).

## Institutional Perspective
Institutional risk management is, in effect, an elaborate, formalized,
committee-governed version of exactly this lesson's personal rulebook —
written risk policies, position limits, and drawdown-triggered review
processes that remove individual trader discretion at the exact moments
discretion is least reliable, scaled up with legal and compliance
enforcement mechanisms individual traders must instead self-impose
through discipline alone.

## Retail Perspective
Retail traders who skip this step — understanding risk management
principles intellectually without ever writing a specific, personal,
numbered rulebook — are the most likely to abandon sound principles
under live market pressure, precisely because no predetermined,
written commitment exists to fall back on in the moment.

## Professional Insights
Professional traders often revisit and refine their written rulebook
after every significant drawdown or major market event specifically
included as a scheduled part of their process (per `[25.08]`'s review
schedule and `[24.12]`'s iteration discipline), treating the rulebook
as a living document that improves with experience rather than a
one-time exercise completed and forgotten.

## Common Mistakes
- Understanding this module's principles intellectually without ever
  writing a specific, numbered personal rulebook.
- Writing vague rules ("manage risk carefully") instead of specific,
  checkable numbers and thresholds.
- Abandoning the rulebook's rules during emotionally charged moments,
  precisely when it exists to provide the most value.

## Misconceptions
- **"Understanding risk management concepts is sufficient without
  writing them down."** Written, specific, pre-committed rules
  meaningfully improve real-world adherence compared to general
  intellectual understanding alone.
- **"A rulebook, once written, should never change."** It should be
  reviewed and refined periodically based on statistically meaningful
  journaled evidence (`[24.12]`), not treated as permanently fixed.
- **"Risk rules can be relaxed during a winning streak."** This module's
  entire framework exists specifically to apply consistently regardless
  of recent results — a winning streak is not evidence that rules can
  be safely loosened.

## Key Takeaways
- A personal risk management rulebook synthesizes `[25.01]`–`[25.07]`
  into one specific, written, numbered reference consulted before every trade.
- Writing rules down in advance meaningfully improves adherence during
  live trading pressure compared to relying on memory or in-the-moment judgment.
- The rulebook should be reviewed and refined periodically based on
  journaled evidence (`[24.12]`), not treated as fixed forever.
- Institutional risk management is effectively a scaled, formalized
  version of the same underlying principle this lesson applies personally.

## Summary
A personal risk management rulebook — covering per-trade risk limits,
stop-loss methodology, minimum risk-reward, drawdown response,
correlation/cluster limits, and tail-risk safeguards, all specified with
concrete numbers and reviewed periodically — is what transforms this
entire module's individual principles into consistently applied
practice, especially during exactly the high-pressure moments when
sound judgment is hardest to access. This completes Module 25's
treatment of risk management; Module 26 now builds the specific
mathematical position-sizing methods — fixed fractional, ATR-based, and
the Kelly Criterion — that operationalize this rulebook's per-trade risk
limit with precision.
