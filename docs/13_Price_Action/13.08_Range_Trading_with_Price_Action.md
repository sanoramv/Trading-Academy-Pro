## Core Concepts

### Definition
**Range trading** applies this module's price action toolkit specifically
to confirmed range-bound conditions (`[02.02]`) — buying near the range's
lower boundary (support) and selling/shorting near its upper boundary
(resistance), on the premise that price will continue oscillating between
these established boundaries until a genuine breakout occurs. This
represents a fundamentally different trading psychology and approach from
trend-following (developed in the next lesson), requiring traders to
consciously fade (bet against) short-term extremes rather than following
directional momentum.

### Intuition
Range trading directly applies `[02.04]`'s support/resistance concept,
`[13.02]`'s candle-by-candle reading, and `[13.04]`'s breakout/fakeout
distinction to a specific market condition — success depends heavily on
correctly confirming the range is genuinely intact (not secretly
transitioning toward a breakout) before repeatedly fading its extremes, a
strategy that performs well within a genuine range but performs
particularly poorly if applied unknowingly during an actual breakout.

```
  -- range traders must be READY to abandon the fade approach
  the instant genuine breakout evidence appears
```

## Real World Analogy
Range trading is like a lifeguard repeatedly pulling swimmers back from
drifting too far in either direction within clearly marked swimming
boundaries — a sustainable, repeatable job as long as the boundaries
remain genuinely respected by the tide and current conditions. But the
lifeguard must remain alert to genuinely changing conditions (a rip
current forming, or the tide shifting) that could mean the previously
reliable boundaries no longer hold — continuing to apply the same
"pull back to the marked area" approach without recognizing genuinely
changed conditions would eventually fail.

## Indian Market Example
Bank Nifty frequently exhibits multi-week range-bound behavior between
major catalysts (RBI policy decisions, major bank earnings) — Indian
options sellers (`[21.09]`, previewed) specifically favor these confirmed
range conditions for premium-selling strategies, since range-bound
markets favor time decay capture over directional betting, directly
connecting this lesson's price action range-reading to a specific,
widely-used Indian derivatives strategy category.

## US Market Example
US traders analyzing individual stocks in extended consolidation phases
(post-earnings, pre-next-catalyst periods) apply exactly this fade-the-
extremes approach on shorter timeframes, while remaining alert to volume
and candle-signature warning signs (per `[13.04]`) that might indicate an
impending breakout from the established range, particularly as a
significant scheduled event (like the next earnings report) approaches.

## Historical Example
The Sensex's multi-year 2010-2013 range-bound period (`[02.02]`'s
historical example, revisited here) offered an extended, real-world
range-trading environment for Indian market participants — traders who
correctly identified and traded this genuine, multi-year range using
appropriately confirmed fade approaches captured meaningfully different
outcomes than those who repeatedly, prematurely anticipated a breakout
that took years longer to materialize than initially expected.

## Institutional Perspective
Institutional market-making and range-trading strategies (a recognized
strategy category, previewed for Module 24) specifically profit from
range-bound conditions through repeated, disciplined boundary-fading,
often combined with options-based premium collection strategies —
treating confirmed ranges as a distinct, valuable trading regime rather
than simply "boring, low-opportunity" periods to wait out.

## Retail Perspective
Retail traders sometimes find range-bound markets frustrating and
attempt to force trend-following approaches (`[10.06]`'s whipsaw problem)
during genuine range conditions — building the discipline to recognize
range conditions and switch to this lesson's fade-based approach, rather
than fighting the prevailing market character, improves results
significantly.

## Professional Insights
Professional range traders specifically emphasize using **tighter,
more conservative position sizing** than trend-following approaches
might use, precisely because range trades inherently fade short-term
momentum (a higher-frequency, lower-conviction-per-trade approach) rather
than riding sustained directional moves — the risk/reward profile and
appropriate psychology genuinely differ from trend-following, a theme the
next lesson develops in full contrast.

## Common Mistakes
- Continuing to fade range extremes after genuine breakout evidence has
  appeared, rather than abandoning the approach promptly.
- Applying range-trading psychology and position sizing to genuinely
  trending conditions, or vice versa.
- Not confirming the range is genuinely intact (per `[02.02]`) before
  beginning to trade it.

## Misconceptions
- **"Range trading is a lower-skill, easier approach than trend
  trading."** It requires genuine skill in confirming range conditions,
  reading boundary reactions accurately, and promptly recognizing when
  conditions are changing — a distinct, not inherently easier, skill set.
- **"The same trading approach works equally well in ranges and
  trends."** As `[10.06]`'s moving average whipsaw discussion and this
  lesson emphasize, range-appropriate and trend-appropriate approaches are
  genuinely different, requiring market-state recognition before
  selection.
- **"A range, once established, will never break."** Every range
  eventually ends — range trading requires readiness to abandon the fade
  approach the moment genuine breakout evidence appears, not an assumption
  of permanent range persistence.

## Key Takeaways
- Range trading applies this module's price action toolkit specifically
  to confirmed range-bound conditions, fading extremes rather than
  following directional momentum.
- Success depends on correctly confirming genuine range conditions and
  promptly recognizing breakout evidence to abandon the fade approach.
- Range trading requires a distinct psychology and typically tighter
  position sizing compared to trend-following.
- Institutions and disciplined retail traders treat confirmed ranges as a
  distinct, valuable trading regime, not simply periods to wait out.
