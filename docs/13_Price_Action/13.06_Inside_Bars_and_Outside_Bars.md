# [13.06] Inside Bars and Outside Bars

> Module: Price Action · Lesson 6 of 10 · Estimated time: 30 minutes

## Core Concepts

### Definition
An **Inside Bar** is a candle whose entire range (high to low) is
contained within the prior candle's range — a single-bar volatility
contraction, conceptually the smallest possible "triangle" (`[12.04]`) or
consolidation pattern. An **Outside Bar** (also called an "engulfing
bar" in some price action terminology, related to but distinct from
`[11.04]`'s specifically body-based Engulfing pattern) is a candle whose
entire range exceeds the prior candle's range on both ends — a single-bar
volatility expansion.

### Intuition
These are the simplest possible expressions of the contraction/expansion
dynamic this module and Module 12 have explored at larger scales —
Triangles (`[12.04]`) and Flags (`[12.05]`) show volatility contraction
across many candles; an Inside Bar shows the identical dynamic
compressed into just two candles. Outside Bars show the opposite:
sudden, single-bar volatility expansion, often marking a decisive moment
where one side seizes control after a period of indecision.

### Mental Model
```

   |   |     range)                       |     |     range EXCEEDS
   | +-+ |  <- current bar,               | +-+ |     the prior bar
   | |x| |     range CONTAINED             | |y| |     on BOTH ends
   | +-+ |     within prior bar's          +-----+
   +---+     range                        +---+  <- prior bar
                                            |   |     (smaller range,
  VOLATILITY CONTRACTION                    +---+     now "engulfed")
  (single-bar version of a
  Triangle/Flag, [12.04]-[12.05])       VOLATILITY EXPANSION
                                        (decisive moment, one side
                                        seizing control)
```

### Visual Explanation
Inside Bars are typically traded via a breakout approach (entering in
whichever direction price breaks the Inside Bar's range), while Outside
Bars are typically read as an immediate, decisive momentum signal in the
direction of the outside bar's close.

## Real World Analogy
An Inside Bar is like a brief, quiet pause in a loud conversation — a
momentary contraction in intensity before the conversation either
resumes its prior tone or shifts to something new. An Outside Bar is like
someone suddenly, decisively raising their voice well beyond the
conversation's prior volume range in both directions — an unmistakable,
attention-grabbing shift that immediately signals something significant
has changed in the exchange's dynamic.

## Indian Market Example
Indian intraday traders on Bank Nifty 15-minute charts routinely watch
for Inside Bars forming during consolidation phases, treating a
subsequent breakout above or below the Inside Bar's range as a specific,
precise entry trigger — a granular, single-bar application of the same
breakout logic covered for larger Triangle patterns in `[12.04]`.

## US Market Example
US day traders reading Outside Bars on 5-minute S&P 500 futures charts
treat a decisively bullish or bearish Outside Bar, particularly one
occurring at a significant structural level (`[02.04]`), as a strong,
immediate momentum signal warranting close attention, especially when
confirmed by expanding volume (`[10.08]`).

## Historical Example
Inside Bar and Outside Bar patterns represent a more recent, granular
formalization within price action trading education (associated with the
broader discretionary price action tradition covered in `[13.01]`,
including practitioners like Al Brooks) of dynamics that classical
technical analysis (Modules 10, 12) had already identified at larger
scales (Triangles, `[12.04]`; Engulfing patterns, `[11.04]`) — this
lesson's patterns represent the same underlying contraction/expansion
logic compressed to its simplest, two-bar minimum expression.

## Institutional Perspective
Institutional systematic strategies sometimes incorporate Inside Bar
breakout logic as a specific, rules-based entry trigger within broader
trend-following systems (previewing Module 24.02), given the pattern's
precise, objectively-definable, easily-programmable criteria (range fully
contained within the prior bar) compared to some more subjectively-
defined price action concepts.

## Retail Perspective
Retail traders find Inside Bars and Outside Bars particularly accessible
entry points precisely because their criteria are so objectively,
precisely definable (unlike some more subjective price action judgment
calls covered elsewhere in this module) — a useful, concrete starting
point for traders building confidence in applying price action concepts
systematically.

## Professional Insights
Professional traders specifically note that an Inside Bar occurring at a
significant structural level (`[02.04]`, `[13.03]`) carries considerably
more significance than one occurring in the middle of "no man's land" —
directly applying `[11.08]`'s contextual checklist to this specific
pattern, consistent with this module's and Module 11's recurring
"context matters more than shape" theme.

## Common Mistakes
- Trading every Inside Bar breakout regardless of structural context,
  missing `[11.08]`'s contextual discipline.
- Confusing an Outside Bar with `[11.04]`'s specifically body-based
  Engulfing pattern — Outside Bars are defined by full RANGE (high-low)
  containment/exceedance, while Engulfing patterns are defined by BODY
  (open-close) containment specifically.
- Not checking volume on Inside Bar breakouts, missing a valuable
  confirmation signal.

## Misconceptions
- **"Inside Bar and Outside Bar patterns are the same as Harami and
  Engulfing patterns from Module 11."** They are related but distinct —
  Inside/Outside Bars are defined by full candle RANGE (high-low), while
  Harami/Engulfing are defined specifically by candle BODY (open-close)
  relationships.
- **"Every Inside Bar should be traded as a breakout setup regardless of
  location."** As with every pattern in this academy, structural context
  significantly affects reliability — an Inside Bar at a key level carries
  more significance than one in an insignificant location.
- **"An Outside Bar guarantees continued momentum in its closing
  direction."** Like all patterns covered throughout this academy, it
  provides probabilistic information, not certainty — confirmation and
  context remain important.

## Key Takeaways
- Inside Bars (range fully contained within the prior bar) represent
  single-bar volatility contraction; Outside Bars (range exceeding the
  prior bar on both ends) represent single-bar volatility expansion.
- These are the simplest, most granular expressions of the contraction/
  expansion dynamic explored at larger scales in `[12.04]`-`[12.05]`.
- Inside Bars are typically traded via breakout direction; Outside Bars
  are typically read as immediate momentum signals.
- Structural context and volume confirmation remain essential, consistent
  with this academy's recurring themes.

## Summary
Inside Bars (single-bar volatility contraction) and Outside Bars
(single-bar volatility expansion) — distinguished from `[11.04]`'s body-
based Harami/Engulfing patterns by their full-range (high-low) definition

## Quiz
1. What is an Inside Bar?
   a) A candle whose range exceeds the prior candle's range
   b) A candle whose entire range is contained within the prior candle's range
   c) A single candlestick with no wicks
   d) A dividend announcement

2. What is an Outside Bar?
   a) Identical to an Inside Bar
   b) A candle whose entire range exceeds the prior candle's range on both ends
   c) A candle with zero volume
   d) A type of moving average

3. How do Inside/Outside Bars differ from Harami/Engulfing patterns?
   a) They are identical concepts
   b) Inside/Outside Bars use full range (high-low); Harami/Engulfing use body (open-close)
   c) Inside Bars only occur in currency markets
   d) Outside Bars require three candles

4. How is an Inside Bar typically traded?
   a) It cannot be traded  b) Via a breakout-direction approach, similar to a Triangle  c) Only as a reversal signal  d) Only on weekly charts

5. True or False: An Outside Bar guarantees continued momentum in its
   closing direction with complete certainty.
   a) True
   b) False

## Answers
1. **b)** — An Inside Bar's entire range is contained within the prior candle's range.
2. **b)** — An Outside Bar's range exceeds the prior candle's range on both ends.
3. **b)** — Inside/Outside Bars use full range; Harami/Engulfing use body specifically.
4. **b)** — Inside Bars are typically traded via a breakout-direction approach.
5. **b) False** — Like all patterns, it provides probabilistic information, not certainty.
