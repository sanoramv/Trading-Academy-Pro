## Core Concepts

### Definition
A **discretionary price action playbook** is a structured, personal,
written decision process combining this module's full toolkit — candle-
by-candle reading (`[13.02]`), supply/demand zones (`[13.03]`), breakout/
fakeout distinction (`[13.04]`), pullback-versus-reversal assessment
(`[13.05]`), Inside/Outside Bars (`[13.06]`), key levels (`[13.07]`), and
market-state-appropriate approach selection (range trading `[13.08]`
versus trend-following `[13.09]`) — into one coherent, repeatable process,
directly extending `[10.12]`'s technical analysis checklist with this
module's more fluid, discretionary reading skills.

### Intuition
This module deliberately moved from `[13.01]`'s foundational philosophy
through increasingly specific tools, building toward this final synthesis
— just as `[10.12]` completed Module 10 by combining its indicator-based
toolkit into one checklist, this lesson completes Module 13 by combining
its more fluid, discretionary reading skills into an equally structured,
repeatable personal process, preventing the two failure modes this
academy has repeatedly warned against: inconsistent, bias-driven
application, and impossibly demanding analysis paralysis (`[10.11]`).

### Mental Model
```
COMPLETE PRICE ACTION PLAYBOOK (synthesizing Module 13)

STEP 1: MARKET STATE (foundational, from Module 02)
  - Trending or range-bound? ([02.01]-[02.02])

STEP 2: KEY LEVELS (13.07)
  - Nearby structural levels (Module 02, [13.03])
  - Nearby round numbers, PDH/PDL, opening range

STEP 3: APPROACH SELECTION
  - Trending -> Trend-following approach (13.09): buy
    pullbacks, assessed via [13.05]'s framework
  - Range-bound -> Range trading approach (13.08): fade
    extremes, assessed via [13.04]'s breakout/fakeout framework

STEP 4: ENTRY TRIGGER
  - Candle-by-candle trajectory ([13.02]) confirming the
    approach's thesis
  - Inside/Outside Bar signals ([13.06]), if present
  - Breakout/fakeout signature check ([13.04])

STEP 5: RISK MANAGEMENT (bridging to Modules 25-26)
  - Stop-loss placement based on the SPECIFIC price action
    invalidation point (e.g., below the pullback low, below
    the range support, per each approach's logic)
```

## Real World Analogy
A discretionary price action playbook is like a skilled improvisational
musician's deep, internalized knowledge of music theory and scales — they
don't consult a fixed songbook for every note, but their fluid,
in-the-moment playing is still grounded in a structured, deeply
internalized framework that ensures coherence and quality, rather than
being random, unstructured noise. This playbook provides exactly that
kind of structured foundation for price action's more fluid, discretionary
application.

## Indian Market Example
An experienced Indian price action trader analyzing Bank Nifty in real
time applies this exact playbook flow — first assessing trend/range
state, noting nearby structural and round-number levels, selecting the
matched approach, waiting for a confirmed candle-by-candle or Inside/
Outside Bar entry trigger, and only then executing with predetermined
risk management — a fluid but genuinely structured process, not random
improvisation.

## US Market Example
A US day trader applying this playbook to S&P 500 futures would follow
the identical structural flow, substituting appropriate US-market-
specific levels and instruments — demonstrating, consistent with
`[10.12]`'s parallel conclusion, that this playbook's underlying process
transfers directly across markets, since it's grounded in universal price
behavior principles, not market-specific quirks.

Institutional discretionary trading desks maintain formal playbooks

## Retail Perspective
Retail traders who complete this module benefit enormously from
formalizing their price action reading into an explicit, written
playbook rather than relying purely on intuition — even though price
action is inherently more fluid and discretionary than Module 10's more
mechanical indicators, structuring its application into a repeatable
process (as this lesson provides) prevents the inconsistent, bias-driven
application that undermines purely intuitive approaches.

## Professional Insights
Professional discretionary traders emphasize that this playbook's value,
like `[10.12]`'s technical checklist, comes substantially from
**consistent application and iterative refinement over time**, tracked
via a trading journal (`market_journal/JOURNAL_TEMPLATE.md`) — a
discretionary price action playbook is inherently more subjective than a
purely mechanical indicator checklist, making disciplined, honest journal
tracking even more valuable for identifying which specific personal
judgment patterns genuinely add value versus which don't.

## Common Mistakes
- Applying price action reading without any structured playbook,
  relying purely on unstructured intuition.
- Not matching the selected approach (trend-following vs. range trading)
  to the correctly-assessed market state.
- Skipping the risk management step, treating a well-read price action
  setup as sufficient without predetermined stop-loss placement.

## Misconceptions
- **"Price action trading is too fluid and discretionary to be
  structured into a playbook."** As this lesson demonstrates, even
  inherently discretionary skills benefit from a structured, repeatable
  decision process — structure and discretion are complementary, not
  contradictory.
- **"This playbook replaces Module 10's technical analysis checklist."**
  They are complementary, not competing — many traders combine both
  indicator-based confirmation (`[10.12]`) and price action reading
  (this lesson) within a single, integrated overall process.
- **"Once built, a price action playbook should never be revised."**
  Professional practice explicitly involves iterative refinement based on
  tracked outcomes — a living process document, not a permanently fixed
  formula, consistent with `[10.12]`'s identical conclusion.

## Key Takeaways
- A discretionary price action playbook combines this module's full
  toolkit into one structured, repeatable process: market state, key
  levels, approach selection, entry trigger, and risk management.
- This playbook complements, rather than replaces, Module 10's technical
  analysis checklist — many traders integrate both.
- Structure and discretion are complementary — even inherently fluid
  skills benefit from a repeatable decision process.
- Consistent application and trading-journal-tracked iterative refinement,
  not a single use, provides this playbook's genuine value.
