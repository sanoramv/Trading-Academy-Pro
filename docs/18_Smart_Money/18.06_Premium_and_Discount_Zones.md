## Core Concepts

### Definition
Within any identified price range (typically a recent significant swing
from low to high, or high to low), SMC divides the range at its **50%
midpoint**, called the **equilibrium** level. The upper half of the
range (above equilibrium) is the **premium zone** — considered a
relatively expensive area, where SMC traders favor looking for selling
opportunities. The lower half of the range (below equilibrium) is the
**discount zone** — considered a relatively cheap area, where SMC
traders favor looking for buying opportunities. This provides a simple,
rule-based framework for assessing whether current price represents a
relatively favorable or unfavorable entry area within its recent range.

### Intuition
This concept is directly related to `[10.09]`'s Fibonacci retracement
framework — both divide a price range into sub-zones to assess relative
entry favorability, with premium/discount's 50% equilibrium level
corresponding directly to Fibonacci's well-known 50% retracement level,
simply reframed with more intuitive "cheap vs. expensive" terminology
rather than percentage-based Fibonacci ratios.

### Visual Explanation
SMC traders typically combine premium/discount zone analysis with order
block (`[18.03]`) and FVG (`[18.04]`) identification — specifically
favoring bullish order blocks or FVGs that fall within the discount zone
(reinforcing a buying bias) and bearish order blocks or FVGs that fall
within the premium zone (reinforcing a selling bias), treating alignment
across multiple concepts as higher-confidence confluence.

## Real World Analogy
Premium and discount zones are like a simple rule of thumb for evaluating
whether a specific item's current price, relative to its own recent
price history, looks relatively expensive or relatively cheap — a
shopper checking whether an item is currently priced in the upper or
lower half of its typical recent price range before deciding whether
it's a good time to buy or wait, without needing more sophisticated
absolute valuation analysis.

## Indian Market Example
Indian SMC-influenced Nifty 50 swing traders specifically identify a
recent significant range, mark its 50% equilibrium level, and favor long
entries specifically within the discount zone (especially where aligned
with a bullish order block, `[18.03]`) rather than chasing entries in the
premium zone, applying a simple, disciplined "buy low within the range"
framework.

## US Market Example
US SMC day traders analyzing intraday ranges frequently apply premium/
discount zone analysis to the current session's developing range,
specifically favoring long entries in the session's discount zone and
short entries in the premium zone, particularly when this aligns with
higher-timeframe directional bias.

## Historical Example
The specific premium/discount terminology emerged within SMC's
2010s-2020s popularization (`[18.01]`), but the underlying midpoint-based
range-division logic has much older roots in Fibonacci retracement
analysis (`[10.09]`) and even Wyckoff's original range-based thinking
(`[17.02]`'s Cause and Effect discussion), illustrating another instance
of SMC providing updated, more intuitive vocabulary for established
concepts.

## Institutional Perspective
Institutional traders executing within a range-bound market genuinely do
consider relative price position within the range when timing
accumulation or distribution — buying more aggressively in the lower
portion and distributing more aggressively in the upper portion is a
broadly sensible, economically rational practice, lending general
plausibility to this concept's underlying logic even without using SMC's
specific terminology.

## Retail Perspective
Retail traders should specifically practice identifying the relevant
range and equilibrium level for their own trading timeframe, recognizing
that "premium" and "discount" are always relative to a *specific* chosen
range — the same absolute price level might be in the discount zone of a
longer-term range while simultaneously being in the premium zone of a
shorter-term intraday range.

## Professional Insights
Professional analysts note that premium/discount zone analysis, while
conceptually reasonable, should not be treated as a standalone entry
signal — it functions best as a *filter* or *contextual bias* combined
with more specific confirmation tools (order blocks, FVGs, structural
breaks) rather than a sufficient trigger on its own.

## Common Mistakes
- Treating premium/discount zone position as a standalone, sufficient
  entry signal rather than a contextual filter.
- Confusing which range (longer-term vs. shorter-term) is relevant for a
  specific trading decision, since the same price can be simultaneously
  in different zones depending on the chosen range.
- Not recognizing this concept's direct relationship to `[10.09]`'s
  Fibonacci 50% retracement level.

## Misconceptions
- **"Premium and discount zones are a standalone, sufficient trading
  signal."** They function best as a contextual filter or bias, combined
  with more specific confirmation tools like order blocks and FVGs.
- **"A specific price level is always in either the premium or discount
  zone, regardless of which range is being analyzed."** Zone
  classification is always relative to a specific, chosen range — the
  same price can be in different zones depending on timeframe.
- **"Premium/discount analysis is unrelated to Fibonacci retracement."**
  It corresponds directly to Fibonacci's well-established 50% retracement
  level (`[10.09]`), simply using more intuitive terminology.

## Key Takeaways
- Premium zone: upper half of a range (above the 50% equilibrium), favor
  selling. Discount zone: lower half, favor buying.
- This concept corresponds directly to `[10.09]`'s Fibonacci 50%
  retracement level.
- Zone classification is always relative to a specific, chosen range —
  the same price can be in different zones on different timeframes.
- Best used as a contextual filter combined with order blocks/FVGs, not
  as a standalone signal.

## Summary
Premium and discount zones — dividing any identified price range at its
50% equilibrium midpoint to assess relative buy/sell favorability —
directly correspond to `[10.09]`'s Fibonacci retracement framework, best
applied as a contextual filter combined with order block (`[18.03]`) and
FVG (`[18.04]`) confirmation rather than as a standalone signal. This
sets up the next lesson's direct comparison of SMC against classical
