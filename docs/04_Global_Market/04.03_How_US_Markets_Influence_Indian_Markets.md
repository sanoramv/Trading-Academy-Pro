# [04.03] How US Markets Influence Indian Markets (SGX Nifty / GIFT Nifty)

> Module: Global Market · Lesson 3 of 8 · Estimated time: 30 minutes

## Learning Objectives
- Explain the mechanism by which overnight US markets affect Indian opens.
- Understand what GIFT Nifty is and why it replaced SGX Nifty.
- Read GIFT Nifty as a pre-market indicator responsibly, not as a guarantee.
- Recognize the limits of this predictive relationship.

## Core Concepts

### Definition
**GIFT Nifty** (formerly known as **SGX Nifty**, traded on the Singapore
Exchange until the contract migrated to India's GIFT City International
Financial Services Centre in 2023) is a Nifty 50 index futures contract
that trades nearly 21 hours a day, including during US and other overnight
global sessions when NSE itself is closed. Because it tracks the same
underlying Nifty 50 index and trades during hours NSE doesn't, GIFT Nifty
is widely watched by Indian traders as an **overnight/pre-market indicator**
of likely Nifty 50 sentiment at the next NSE open.

### Intuition
Since NSE is closed during the US trading session, Indian traders have no
direct, real-time way to see how overnight global developments (Fed
decisions, big US tech earnings, geopolitical news) might affect Indian
sentiment — until GIFT Nifty offers a continuously-traded proxy. If GIFT
Nifty is trading notably higher or lower than Nifty 50's previous NSE
close, it's a signal (not a guarantee) that Nifty 50 may open in that
direction.

### Mental Model
```
US market closes (~1:30 AM IST) --> GIFT Nifty continues trading
overnight, absorbing global sentiment --> Indian traders check GIFT
Nifty level before 9:15 AM IST --> forms an initial expectation for
Nifty 50's opening direction --> NSE's own 9:00-9:15 AM pre-open
session (per [03.05]) then does the actual, India-specific price
discovery
```

## Real World Analogy
GIFT Nifty is like checking a weather forecast from a neighboring city
that's a few hours ahead of you in the same general weather system — it's
a genuinely useful, informative signal about what conditions might be
heading your way, but it's not a guarantee, because local conditions
(India-specific news, NSE's own pre-open price discovery) can still change
the actual outcome by the time weather (trading) actually reaches you.

## Indian Market Example
On mornings following a sharp overnight US selloff (e.g., driven by a
hotter-than-expected US inflation report), GIFT Nifty typically shows a
notable discount to Nifty 50's previous close well before the Indian
market opens, and Indian traders widely reference this level in pre-market
discussion and planning — though NSE's own 9:00–9:15 AM pre-open session
(`[03.05]`) always has the final say in the actual opening price, which can
differ from the GIFT Nifty indication if India-specific news breaks
overnight too.

## US Market Example
On major US index-moving days — like a significant S&P 500 rally following
a dovish Federal Reserve statement — GIFT Nifty typically reflects that
positive sentiment well ahead of the Indian open, giving traders several
hours of advance signal before NSE's own session begins, a direct,
practical illustration of the overnight linkage this lesson describes.

## Historical Example
The migration from **SGX Nifty to GIFT Nifty**, completed in July 2023,
moved the contract's trading venue from Singapore to India's own GIFT
City (Gujarat International Finance Tec-City) International Financial
Services Centre — a deliberate policy move by Indian authorities to
repatriate trading activity and associated economic value (fees, jobs,
market infrastructure development) that had previously accrued to
Singapore, while preserving the same continuous, near-24-hour trading
function that made SGX Nifty valuable to Indian traders in the first
place.

## Institutional Perspective
Institutions use GIFT Nifty not just as a directional indicator but as an
active **hedging and arbitrage instrument**, given its extended trading
hours allow risk management of India-linked exposure even when NSE itself
is closed — a capability particularly valuable for global funds holding
Indian equity exposure who need to manage risk around overnight events
occurring outside NSE's trading hours.

## Retail Perspective
Retail traders very commonly check GIFT Nifty each morning as a rough
"heads up" before the Indian market opens, but should be cautious about
over-relying on it as a precise predictor — actual Nifty 50 opening prices
can and do diverge from the overnight GIFT Nifty indication, particularly
when India-specific news breaks during NSE's own pre-open window.

## Professional Insights
Professional traders treat GIFT Nifty as one input among several
(alongside broader global index futures, currency moves, and Asian
session cues) rather than a standalone predictor, and specifically note
that GIFT Nifty's *own* liquidity is thinner than NSE's regular session
liquidity — meaning large overnight moves in GIFT Nifty on low volume can
sometimes overstate the eventual NSE opening move once India's own deeper
liquidity pool engages.

## Common Mistakes
- Treating GIFT Nifty's overnight level as a guaranteed, precise prediction
  of the exact NSE opening price.
- Not accounting for India-specific news that might break after GIFT
  Nifty's overnight session but before or during NSE's own pre-open window.
- Overreacting to GIFT Nifty moves during periods of very thin, low-volume
  overnight trading.

## Misconceptions
- **"GIFT Nifty and Nifty 50 always open at exactly the same level."**
  They are closely correlated but not identical — NSE's own pre-open price
  discovery (`[03.05]`) can produce a different opening level, especially
  when fresh India-specific information arrives.
- **"SGX Nifty still exists and trades in Singapore."** The contract fully
  migrated to GIFT City by July 2023; SGX Nifty no longer trades under that
  name in Singapore.
- **"GIFT Nifty is only useful for predicting the open."** It's also a
  genuine hedging and trading instrument in its own right, used by
  institutions to manage India-linked exposure around the clock, not merely
  a forecasting tool for retail traders.

## Key Takeaways
- GIFT Nifty (formerly SGX Nifty) trades nearly 21 hours a day, offering an
  overnight proxy for Nifty 50 sentiment when NSE is closed.
- The SGX-to-GIFT City migration (completed July 2023) repatriated this
  trading activity to India.
- GIFT Nifty is a useful but imperfect indicator — NSE's own pre-open
  session always determines the actual opening price.
- Institutions use GIFT Nifty for genuine hedging, not just retail
  pre-market forecasting.

## Summary
GIFT Nifty (the successor to SGX Nifty, now trading from India's own GIFT
City) provides Indian traders a continuously-traded, overnight proxy for
Nifty 50 sentiment, bridging the gap created by NSE's fixed trading hours
and the reality that major market-moving events (US Fed decisions, global
earnings, geopolitical news) often occur while NSE is closed. Understanding
its use — and its limits — completes the practical toolkit for
understanding how global markets connect to Indian mornings, setting up
the next lesson's dedicated focus on currency markets.

   a) Yes, always exactly  b) No — NSE's own pre-open session determines the actual opening price  c) Only on Fridays  d) Only during monsoon season

   Nifty for?
   a) Nothing else  b) Genuine hedging of India-linked exposure around the clock  c) Setting RBI policy  d) Currency printing

5. True or False: GIFT Nifty's liquidity is always as deep as NSE's regular
   session liquidity.
   a) True
   b) False

## Answers
1. **b)** — GIFT Nifty is a near-24-hour Nifty 50 index futures contract.
2. **c)** — The migration to GIFT City completed in July 2023.
3. **b)** — NSE's own pre-open session determines the actual opening price, which can diverge from GIFT Nifty's indication.
4. **b)** — Institutions use it for genuine hedging and arbitrage of India-linked exposure, not just forecasting.
5. **b) False** — GIFT Nifty's liquidity is typically thinner than NSE's regular session liquidity.
