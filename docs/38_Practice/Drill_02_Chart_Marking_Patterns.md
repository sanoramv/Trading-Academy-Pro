# Drill 2 — Chart Marking: Candlestick & Chart Patterns

> Builds `[11.01]`-`[12.10]`'s pattern recognition skills through hands-on identification.

## Instructions
1. Open a daily chart covering 6-12 months of price history.
2. Scan for and mark every occurrence of: a bullish/bearish engulfing pattern (`[11.04]`), a doji at a key level (`[11.02]`), and any completed chart pattern (Head and Shoulders, Double Top/Bottom, Triangle, Flag — `[12.01]`-`[12.05]`).
3. For each marked pattern, note: (a) did it occur at a structurally significant level (support/resistance)? (b) what did volume do on the confirming candle/breakout?
4. Classify each pattern as HIGH-CONFIDENCE (at a key level, volume-confirmed) or LOW-CONFIDENCE (mid-range, unconfirmed volume) per `[11.08]`'s "location matters more than shape" principle.
5. Track forward: did each HIGH-CONFIDENCE pattern actually play out as expected? What was the base rate in your sample?

## Worked Example (Hypothetical)
```
Pattern found: Bullish engulfing candle
Location: At a well-tested support zone (tested 3x previously)
Volume: 1.6x the 20-day average on the engulfing candle
Classification: HIGH-CONFIDENCE (location + volume both confirm)

Pattern found: Doji
Location: Mid-range, no nearby support/resistance
Volume: Below average
Classification: LOW-CONFIDENCE (neither location nor volume supports it)

FORWARD TRACKING (illustrative): Of 8 HIGH-CONFIDENCE patterns
identified in this sample, 6 played out as expected within 5 sessions
(75% success rate in this SPECIFIC sample) -- a small sample,
insufficient for `[24.01]`'s statistical validity standard, but a
useful STARTING observation for further, larger-sample testing.
```

## Self-Check Questions
- Did you resist confirmation bias by tracking ALL patterns found, not just the ones that worked?
- Is your sample size large enough to draw any statistically meaningful conclusion, per `[24.01]`?
- Did you distinguish pattern SHAPE from pattern CONTEXT consistently?
