## Core Concepts

### Definition
The standard Indian equity trading day (NSE/BSE) runs:
- **Pre-open session:** 9:00 AM – 9:15 AM IST
- **Normal trading session:** 9:15 AM – 3:30 PM IST
- **Closing session / post-close:** brief window after 3:30 PM for
  closing price-based orders

A **circuit filter** is a price band (e.g., ±5%, ±10%, or ±20% from the
previous close) beyond which an individual stock cannot trade on a given
day, designed to curb extreme single-stock volatility. A **market-wide
circuit breaker** is a broader, index-level mechanism (based on Nifty 50 or
Sensex moving a defined percentage) that can halt trading across the
*entire* market for a set period, or for the rest of the day, designed to
prevent panic-driven, market-wide cascading declines.

### Intuition
Both mechanisms exist to slow down extreme, potentially panic- or error-
driven price moves, giving participants time to process information rather
than trading purely on momentum or fear. A single-stock circuit filter
protects against isolated stock-specific chaos (e.g., a rogue algorithm or
extreme rumor); a market-wide circuit breaker protects against systemic
panic spreading across the entire market.

### Mental Model
```
PRE-OPEN (9:00-9:15 AM): price discovery before normal trading —
   orders collected and matched at a single "opening price," reducing
   the chaos of a sudden open based on overnight global news.

NORMAL SESSION (9:15 AM-3:30 PM): continuous order matching, as
   covered in [01.04] and [01.08].

CIRCUIT FILTER (stock-level): individual stock halts if price
   band (5%/10%/20%) is breached.

CIRCUIT BREAKER (market-level): ENTIRE MARKET halts if Nifty/Sensex
   moves a defined percentage (10%/15%/20%) from the previous close,
   with halt DURATION depending on the time of day the breach occurs.
```

### Visual Explanation
The pre-open session compresses overnight global information into a single,
orderly opening price rather than letting the first few seconds of trading
absorb that shock chaotically.

## Real World Analogy
The pre-open session is like a controlled, orderly queue forming before a
popular shop opens, where the shopkeeper collects everyone's stated
intentions ("I want to buy at this price") before deciding a single fair
opening price, rather than just throwing the doors open to a chaotic rush
the instant the clock strikes opening time. Circuit filters and breakers
are like a stadium's emergency protocols — a minor incident in one section
(a stock hitting its circuit) gets contained locally, while a
building-wide emergency (a market-wide circuit breaker) requires halting
everything until the situation stabilizes.

## Indian Market Example
On days with major, market-moving news — such as a surprising **Union
Budget announcement** (`[03.10]`) or an unexpected **RBI policy decision**
— individual stocks in directly affected sectors frequently hit their
upper or lower circuit filters, temporarily halting trading in that
specific stock while the broader market continues trading normally around
it, a pattern experienced Indian traders learn to anticipate around known
high-impact event dates.

## US Market Example
US markets use an analogous but differently structured mechanism called
**"Limit Up-Limit Down" (LULD)** for individual securities and
**market-wide circuit breakers** at the S&P 500 index level (halting the
entire market at -7%, -13%, and -20% thresholds from the prior close),
introduced and refined particularly after the 2010 Flash Crash
(`[01.07]`, `[02.06]`) — a structurally similar concept to India's system,
reflecting a shared global regulatory response to extreme volatility
events, even though the specific percentage thresholds and mechanics
differ between the two markets.

## Historical Example
India's market-wide circuit breaker mechanism has been triggered on
several historically notable occasions — most prominently on **March 13,
2020**, during the acute early phase of the COVID-19 market panic, when
Nifty 50 and Sensex fell sharply enough to trigger a full market-wide
trading halt, giving participants a mandated pause during one of the most
volatile trading days in recent Indian market history, directly
illustrating the circuit breaker mechanism functioning exactly as designed
during genuine systemic stress.

## Institutional Perspective
Institutions build circuit filter and breaker awareness directly into
their execution algorithms (Module 29) — large orders are often
deliberately paced to avoid pushing an illiquid stock into its circuit
band prematurely, since a stock stuck at its circuit limit becomes
extremely difficult to trade in size (the order book effectively empties
out on one side once the circuit is hit).

## Retail Perspective
Retail traders sometimes get caught off guard by a stock hitting its upper
or lower circuit, unable to exit a position at their desired price because
trading in that direction is halted at the band — a real, practical risk
particularly relevant for smaller, more volatile stocks, and a strong
argument for understanding circuit mechanics *before* trading such stocks,
not after being caught in one.

## Professional Insights
Professional traders specifically distinguish between a stock that's
"circuit-locked" because of a genuine, sustainable fundamental catalyst
(like unexpectedly strong earnings) versus one circuit-locked due to
speculative froth or manipulation concerns — the former often continues in
the same direction across subsequent sessions, while the latter can reverse
sharply once circuit-driven forced buying/selling pressure subsides,
requiring careful contextual judgment rather than a mechanical rule.

## Common Mistakes
- Not knowing the exact 9:00–9:15 AM pre-open window exists and being
  confused by price action during it.
- Assuming a stock hitting its circuit filter means trading is halted for
  the entire market — it only affects that specific stock, unless a
  separate, distinct market-wide circuit breaker is also triggered.
- Placing large orders in an illiquid stock without checking if it's near
  its circuit filter band, risking an inability to exit if the position
  moves against the trader right into a locked circuit.

## Misconceptions
- **"Circuit filters and circuit breakers are the same mechanism."**
  Circuit filters apply to individual stocks; circuit breakers apply
  market-wide at the index level — related concepts but operating at
  entirely different scales.
- **"The pre-open session is optional/unimportant."** The pre-open session
  meaningfully establishes the day's opening price through genuine price
  discovery and is not a mere formality — significant overnight global
  news is often reflected substantially during this window.
- **"Circuit filters are unique to India."** While the specific percentage
  bands and mechanics differ, price limit mechanisms exist in various forms
  across many global markets, including the US's Limit Up-Limit Down system.

## Key Takeaways
- The Indian trading day includes a pre-open session (9:00–9:15 AM) before
  normal trading (9:15 AM–3:30 PM).
- Circuit filters apply per-stock; circuit breakers apply market-wide at
  the index level — distinct mechanisms.
- The March 2020 COVID crash triggered India's market-wide circuit breaker,
  a real historical example of the mechanism functioning as designed.
- Understanding circuit mechanics before trading volatile/illiquid stocks
  is an important practical risk consideration.

## Summary
The Indian trading day's specific structure — a pre-open price discovery
session followed by continuous normal trading — along with its dual-layer
circuit filter (per-stock) and circuit breaker (market-wide) volatility
controls, are practical, sometimes overlooked mechanics every Indian trader
needs to understand, both for everyday order planning and for
understanding what happens during genuine crisis events like March 2020.
The next lesson turns to what happens after a trade executes: India's T+1
settlement system and clearing infrastructure.

   a) 8:00–8:15 AM  b) 9:00–9:15 AM  c) 9:15–9:30 AM  d) 3:00–3:15 PM

   a) The entire market  b) An individual stock's price band  c) Only government bonds  d) Only currencies

3. What does a market-wide circuit breaker do?
   a) Halts trading in one specific stock only
   b) Halts trading across the entire market for a defined period
   c) Changes the exchange's ownership
   d) Only affects derivatives

4. When did India's market-wide circuit breaker notably trigger in recent
   history?
   a) 2008 Global Financial Crisis
   b) March 13, 2020, during the COVID-19 panic
   c) The 1992 Harshad Mehta scam
   d) The 2021 Zomato IPO

5. True or False: Circuit filters and circuit breakers are the exact same
   mechanism operating at the same scale.
   a) True
   b) False

## Answers
1. **b)** — The pre-open session runs from 9:00 AM to 9:15 AM IST.
2. **b)** — Circuit filters apply to individual stock price bands.
3. **b)** — Circuit breakers halt trading across the entire market.
4. **b)** — March 13, 2020 saw India's market-wide circuit breaker trigger during the COVID panic.
5. **b) False** — They operate at different scales: per-stock vs. market-wide.
