# [21.03] Strike Price, Premium, and Moneyness (ITM/ATM/OTM)

> Module: Options · Lesson 3 of 12 · Estimated time: 30 minutes

- Learn the three moneyness classifications: ITM, ATM, and OTM.
- Understand how moneyness differs between calls and puts.

## Core Concepts

### Definition
The **strike price** (or exercise price) is the predetermined price at
which an option's buyer can exercise their right to buy (call) or sell
(put) the underlying. The **premium** is the price paid by the buyer to
the seller for the option contract — determined by ongoing exchange
trading, not fixed by the two parties. **Moneyness** describes an
option's strike price relative to the underlying's current market price:
**In-the-Money (ITM)** means exercising the option would currently be
profitable (before considering premium paid); **At-the-Money (ATM)**
means the strike price is at or very near the current underlying price;
**Out-of-the-Money (OTM)** means exercising the option would currently
be unprofitable.

### Intuition
Moneyness classification works in opposite directions for calls versus
puts, since they represent opposite rights: a call is ITM when the
underlying's price is *above* the strike (you could buy low via the
option and the asset is worth more), while a put is ITM when the
underlying's price is *below* the strike (you could sell high via the
option while the asset is worth less) — this directional mirror-
imaging is essential to internalize before moving further into this
module.

### Mental Model
```
MONEYNESS — CALLS vs PUTS (opposite directions)

  CALL OPTION:                        PUT OPTION:

  Underlying ABOVE strike             Underlying BELOW strike
  -> ITM (in-the-money)               -> ITM (in-the-money)

  Underlying AT strike                Underlying AT strike
  -> ATM (at-the-money)               -> ATM (at-the-money)

  Underlying BELOW strike             Underlying ABOVE strike
  -> OTM (out-of-the-money)           -> OTM (out-of-the-money)
```

### Visual Explanation
Premium levels are directly influenced by moneyness — ITM options
command higher premiums (since they already have inherent, exercisable
value, called intrinsic value, `[21.04]`), while deep OTM options trade
at relatively low premiums (since exercising them currently would be
unprofitable, and they rely entirely on time remaining and the
underlying moving favorably before expiry).

## Real World Analogy
Moneyness is like evaluating a discount coupon relative to a product's
current retail price — a coupon offering a price below the current
market price (ITM-like) is immediately valuable if used right now, a
coupon offering a price right at the current market price (ATM-like)
offers no immediate advantage, and a coupon offering a price above the
current market price (OTM-like) would actually cost you more than
simply buying at the regular price today.

## Indian Market Example
Indian Nifty options traders reading the NSE option chain (`[21.11]`,
previewed) specifically scan across strike prices relative to the
current Nifty spot level to identify ITM, ATM, and OTM options —
premium levels visibly decline as strikes move further OTM in either
direction, a pattern immediately visible when comparing prices across
the chain.

## US Market Example
US options traders similarly reference moneyness constantly when
discussing strategy — phrases like "buying a slightly OTM call" or
"selling an ATM put" are standard trading vocabulary describing
precisely this strike-to-underlying relationship.

## Historical Example
Standardized moneyness terminology (ITM/ATM/OTM) developed alongside
the broader standardization of exchange-traded options following the
CBOE's 1973 founding (`[21.01]`), providing a common, universally
understood vocabulary for describing an option's strike relative to the
underlying across any market or exchange globally.

## Institutional Perspective
Institutional options desks specifically use moneyness classification
as a key input for constructing multi-leg strategies (spreads, `[21.08]`;
straddles/strangles, `[21.09]`) — combining options across different
moneyness levels to construct precise, customized risk/reward profiles
beyond what any single option alone provides.

## Retail Perspective
Retail traders should specifically practice reading moneyness directly
from a live option chain before placing any real trade — confusing ITM
and OTM, or misreading which direction is favorable for calls versus
puts, is a foundational error that can lead to selecting an entirely
inappropriate strike for one's intended thesis.

## Professional Insights
Professional traders note that "moneyness" is a continuous spectrum, not
just three discrete categories — options are often described as "deep
ITM," "slightly OTM," or similar graduated descriptions, since the
degree of moneyness (how far the strike is from the current price)
meaningfully affects both premium and the option's behavioral
characteristics (Module 22's Greeks, previewed).

## Common Mistakes
- Confusing which direction is ITM for calls versus puts, given their
  opposite moneyness relationships.
- Assuming premium is determined solely by moneyness, ignoring time
  value's contribution (`[21.04]`).
- Not checking a live option chain before trading, relying on outdated
  or misremembered strike/premium relationships.

## Misconceptions
- **"ITM means the same underlying-to-strike relationship for both
  calls and puts."** They are mirror images — ITM calls have underlying
  above strike; ITM puts have underlying below strike.
- **"OTM options have zero value and are worthless to trade."** OTM
  options retain time value (`[21.04]`) until expiry and can become
  profitable if the underlying moves favorably before then.
- **"Premium is determined only by moneyness."** Time remaining until
  expiry and implied volatility (Module 22-23, previewed) also
  significantly influence premium levels.

## Key Takeaways
- Strike price is the predetermined transaction price; premium is the
  market-determined price paid for the option contract.
- ITM: exercising would currently be profitable; ATM: strike ≈
  underlying price; OTM: exercising would currently be unprofitable.
- Moneyness works in opposite directions for calls (ITM = underlying
  above strike) versus puts (ITM = underlying below strike).
- Premium generally increases with "in-the-moneyness" due to intrinsic
  value, covered fully in `[21.04]`.

## Summary
Strike price, premium, and moneyness — with ITM, ATM, and OTM
classifications working in mirror-image directions for calls versus
puts — provide the essential vocabulary for reading and comparing
options across a chain, directly setting up the next lesson's precise
decomposition of premium into intrinsic value and time value, the two
components determining exactly why an option costs what it does.

## Revision Notes

- Moneyness is a continuous spectrum ("deep ITM," "slightly OTM"), not

## Quiz
1. What is the strike price?
   a) The premium paid  b) The predetermined price at which the option can be exercised  c) The current stock price  d) A margin requirement

2. When is a call option ITM?
   a) Underlying below strike  b) Underlying above strike  c) Underlying at strike only  d) Never

3. When is a put option ITM?
   a) Underlying above strike  b) Underlying below strike  c) Same as calls  d) Never

4. Do ITM or OTM options generally have higher premiums?
   a) OTM  b) ITM  c) They're always identical  d) Neither has any premium

5. True or False: Moneyness classification works identically in the same direction for both calls and puts.
   a) True
   b) False

## Answers
1. **b)** — The predetermined price at which the option can be exercised.
2. **b)** — Underlying above strike.
3. **b)** — Underlying below strike.
4. **b)** — ITM.
5. **b) False** — They work in opposite/mirror-image directions.
