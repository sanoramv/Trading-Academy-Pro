## Core Concepts

### Definition
The **NSE option chain** is a live table listing every available strike
for a given underlying (e.g., Nifty) and expiry, with call data on the
left, put data on the right, and the strike price down the center. Each
side displays: **OI** (Open Interest — total outstanding contracts not
yet closed, `[14.04]`), **Chg in OI** (change from the previous session),
**Volume**, **IV** (Implied Volatility, Module 23 previewed), **LTP**
(Last Traded Price — the premium), **Bid/Ask**, and **Net Chg**. The
**Put-Call Ratio (PCR)** — total put OI divided by total call OI across
the chain — is a widely watched sentiment summary derived from this
same data.

### Intuition
Every number on the chain is a market participant's real, capital-backed
opinion, not a prediction from an indicator. High OI at a particular
strike means many traders have committed capital to positions there —
often interpreted as a level the market is "defending," since option
sellers with large OI at a strike may hedge to keep the underlying away
from it (avoiding assignment) as expiry nears.

### Mental Model
```
                    CALLS                 |  STRIKE  |          PUTS
  OI    Chg OI   IV    LTP   Bid   Ask    |          |  Bid   Ask   LTP   IV   Chg OI   OI
 ...     ...    ...    ...   ...   ...    |  23,800  |  ...   ...   ...  ...   ...     ...
 ...     ...    ...    ...   ...   ...    |  24,000  |  ...   ...   ...  ...   ...     ...  <- ATM row
 ...     ...    ...    ...   ...   ...    |  24,200  |  ...   ...   ...  ...   ...     ...

  High CALL OI at a strike ABOVE current price -> often read as RESISTANCE
  (many call sellers positioned there, may defend the strike)

  High PUT OI at a strike BELOW current price -> often read as SUPPORT
  (many put sellers positioned there, may defend the strike)

  PCR = Total Put OI / Total Call OI
    PCR > 1  -> more put OI than call OI (often read as bearish positioning
                or, contrarily, oversold sentiment)
    PCR < 1  -> more call OI than put OI (often read as bullish positioning
                or, contrarily, overbought sentiment)
```

### Visual Explanation
Scanning down the OI columns on both sides typically reveals one or two
strikes with noticeably taller "bars" of OI relative to neighboring
strikes — these standout strikes are what traders commonly refer to as
the chain's implied support and resistance for the current expiry.

## Real World Analogy
Reading an option chain is like reading a stadium's advance ticket sales
by seating section before a big match: a section selling out fast (high
OI, rising) signals where the crowd expects the action to concentrate,
even before the match starts — it doesn't guarantee the outcome, but it
reveals where capital has already committed.

## Indian Market Example
On the NSE Nifty option chain, suppose the 24,200 call strike shows OI
of 42 lakh contracts (illustrative figure) — sharply higher than
neighboring strikes — while the 23,800 put strike shows similarly
elevated OI. A trader reads this as the market pricing a likely
24,000–24,200 close-of-expiry range, useful context (not a guarantee)
when deciding whether an iron condor (`[21.10]`) with short strikes near
these levels fits the setup. **Hypothetical Example** for illustration
only.

## US Market Example
While the specific "option chain" layout convention (calls/puts split
around a central strike column) is an NSE/Indian-brokerage-standard
presentation, US options data on platforms like the CBOE or major
brokers displays the same underlying fields — OI, volume, IV, bid/ask —
per strike/expiry; US traders similarly use OI concentration to infer
likely "max pain" levels (the strike where option sellers as a group
would owe the least, a concept beyond this lesson's scope but built from
the same OI data).

## Historical Example
Elevated Nifty option OI has repeatedly clustered around round-number
strikes near key events, such as ahead of Union Budget announcements
(`[03.10]`) — a pattern consistent with traders using round strikes as
default reference points for both hedges and speculative positioning,
illustrating how OI concentration often reflects psychological
round-number levels as much as pure technical analysis.

## Institutional Perspective
Institutional and market-maker desks track OI changes far more closely
than absolute OI levels — a strike with high but *stable* OI reflects
old, resting positions, while a strike with rapidly *rising* OI reflects
fresh, current-session conviction, a distinction retail chain-readers
frequently overlook.

## Retail Perspective
Retail traders often treat the single highest-OI strike as a hard
"wall" the underlying cannot cross — but OI reflects *existing*
positioning, not a guarantee of future price behavior; walls can and do
break, especially on strong fundamental or macro catalysts (Module 06).

## Professional Insights
Professional option chain readers combine OI-based support/resistance
with the PCR trend over several sessions (not a single snapshot) and
with the underlying's price action and volume (`[14.01]`) — no single
column on the chain is treated as sufficient on its own.

## Common Mistakes
- Reading OI once, statically, without checking Change in OI to see
  whether positioning is building or unwinding.
- Treating the PCR as a precise timing signal rather than a broad
  sentiment gauge that can stay extreme for extended periods.
- Ignoring that high OI can reflect *either* side initiating positions —
  OI alone doesn't reveal whether it's buyers or sellers driving the
  build-up.

## Misconceptions
- **"High call OI always means the market is bearish."** High call OI
  often reflects call *sellers* (who benefit from the underlying staying
  below the strike, `[21.06]`) rather than a bearish directional bet.
- **"The strike with maximum OI will always cap the underlying's move."**
  It reflects existing positioning, not an immovable ceiling — strong
  catalysts routinely break through heavily-OI'd strikes.
- **"PCR above 1 always means 'buy' (contrarian signal).** Its
  usefulness as a contrarian indicator depends heavily on market regime
  and is far from a standalone, reliable timing tool.

## Key Takeaways
- The option chain displays OI, Change in OI, Volume, IV, LTP, and
  Bid/Ask for every strike, calls on one side and puts on the other.
- High OI concentration near a strike is commonly read as potential
  support (puts) or resistance (calls) — a reflection of existing
  positioning, not a guarantee.
- PCR = Total Put OI / Total Call OI — a broad sentiment gauge, best
  used as a trend over time rather than a single snapshot.
- Change in OI distinguishes fresh positioning (rising OI) from
  unwinding (falling OI) — always check alongside absolute OI levels.

## Summary
The NSE option chain turns every earlier concept in this module —
strikes, moneyness (`[21.03]`), premium (`[21.04]`) — into a live table
of real market positioning, with Open Interest and the Put-Call Ratio
serving as the primary tools for inferring where the market expects
support and resistance to concentrate for a given expiry. Having now
learned to read the chain that every strategy in this module is actually
built from, the final lesson turns to the most common mistakes option
buyers make — many of which stem from misreading exactly the signals
covered here.

## Quiz
1. What does high OI at a call strike above the current price commonly suggest?
   a) Guaranteed price ceiling  b) Potential resistance, reflecting existing positioning  c) Nothing relevant  d) A dividend event

2. How is the Put-Call Ratio (PCR) calculated?
   a) Call OI / Put OI  b) Put OI / Call OI  c) Put Volume / Call Volume  d) LTP of put / LTP of call

3. What does Change in OI help distinguish?
   a) Bid vs ask spread  b) Fresh positioning vs. unwinding  c) Strike price levels  d) Expiry date

4. Why shouldn't a trader rely on OI alone to predict price movement?
   a) OI is never accurate  b) OI reflects existing positioning, not a guarantee of future price behavior  c) OI only applies to stocks  d) OI is not published by NSE

5. True or False: High call OI always indicates bearish sentiment.
   a) True
   b) False

## Answers
1. **b)** — Potential resistance, reflecting existing positioning, not a guarantee.
2. **b)** — Total Put OI divided by Total Call OI.
3. **b)** — Distinguishes fresh positioning (rising OI) from unwinding (falling OI).
4. **b)** — OI reflects existing positioning; strong catalysts can break through heavily-OI'd strikes.
5. **b) False** — High call OI often reflects call sellers, not necessarily bearish directional bets.
