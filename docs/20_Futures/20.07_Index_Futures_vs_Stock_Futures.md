## Core Concepts

### Definition
**Index futures** (e.g., Nifty 50 futures, Bank Nifty futures) derive
their value from a broad market index — a weighted basket of multiple
stocks (`[01.08]`'s index construction discussion) — and always
cash-settle at expiry, since physically delivering a fractional basket
of dozens of underlying stocks isn't practically feasible. **Stock
futures** derive their value from a single individual company's stock,
carrying company-specific risk in addition to broader market risk, and
in India, are typically physically settled (requiring actual delivery
of the underlying shares) rather than cash-settled, unlike index
futures.

### Intuition
The core distinguishing factor is diversification: index futures provide
broad, diversified market exposure in a single instrument (similar to
how an index fund diversifies equity risk, `[08.10]`'s screening
discussion extended), while stock futures provide concentrated exposure
to one specific company, carrying the same company-specific risks
(earnings surprises, management issues) that affect that individual
stock's spot price.

### Visual Explanation
Because stock futures in India are typically physically settled, a
trader holding a stock futures position through expiry without closing
or rolling it will actually receive (or be required to deliver) the
underlying shares — a materially different practical outcome than index
futures, which simply cash-settle based on the final index value with no
physical delivery ever occurring.

## Real World Analogy
Index futures versus stock futures is like choosing between investing in
a diversified mutual fund (broad, diversified exposure across many
companies) versus buying a single company's stock directly (concentrated
exposure to that one company's specific fortunes) — both provide equity
market exposure, but with meaningfully different risk concentration and
diversification characteristics.

## Indian Market Example
Indian traders wanting to express a broad, market-wide directional view
(bullish or bearish on the overall Indian equity market) typically use
Nifty 50 or Bank Nifty index futures, while traders with a specific,
company-focused thesis (based on fundamental analysis, Module 08, or a
specific catalyst) might instead use that individual company's stock
futures for more concentrated exposure.

## US Market Example
US traders similarly distinguish between broad index futures (E-mini
S&P 500, Nasdaq futures) for market-wide exposure and single-stock
futures (a considerably smaller, less liquid market in the US compared
to India's more actively traded stock futures segment) for
company-specific positioning.

## Historical Example
India's stock futures market has historically been considerably more
actively traded and liquid than in many other global markets — a
distinctive feature of the Indian derivatives ecosystem that developed
alongside the broader growth of NSE's F&O segment following its 2000
introduction (`[03.08]`).

## Institutional Perspective
Institutional portfolio managers frequently use index futures
specifically for efficient, low-cost portfolio-level hedging — rather
than selling dozens of individual holdings to reduce market exposure
temporarily, shorting index futures provides a faster, more capital-
efficient way to hedge broad market risk while maintaining underlying
stock positions.

## Retail Perspective
Retail traders should specifically match their instrument choice to
their actual thesis — using stock futures for a genuinely company-
specific view, and index futures for a genuinely broad, market-wide
view, rather than defaulting to whichever instrument happens to be more
familiar regardless of the underlying thesis's actual scope.

## Professional Insights
Professional traders note that stock futures' physical settlement
requirement in India creates a meaningfully different operational
consideration than index futures' cash settlement — traders must
specifically plan to close or roll stock futures positions before expiry
if physical delivery isn't the intended outcome, a consideration less
relevant for cash-settled index futures.

## Common Mistakes
- Using concentrated stock futures for what is actually a broad,
  market-wide thesis, missing the diversification benefit index futures
  would provide.
- Not planning to close or roll a stock futures position before expiry,
  risking unintended physical delivery obligations.
- Assuming stock futures liquidity always matches index futures
  liquidity — many individual stock futures contracts are considerably
  less liquid than benchmark index futures.

## Misconceptions
- **"Index futures and stock futures settle the same way."** Index
  futures cash-settle; Indian stock futures typically physically settle
  — a meaningful practical difference.
- **"Stock futures provide the same diversification as index futures."**
  Stock futures concentrate risk in a single company; index futures
  diversify across many.
- **"All stock futures are equally liquid."** Liquidity varies
  considerably by individual stock; benchmark index futures (Nifty,
  Bank Nifty) are typically far more liquid than most individual stock
  futures contracts.

## Key Takeaways
- Index futures derive value from a broad index and cash-settle at
  expiry; stock futures derive value from a single company and
  typically physically settle in India.
- Index futures provide diversified exposure; stock futures provide
  concentrated, company-specific exposure.
- Match instrument choice to the actual scope of your trading thesis —
  broad market view vs. specific company view.
- Plan stock futures positions carefully around expiry to avoid
  unintended physical delivery.

## Summary
Index futures and stock futures differ meaningfully in diversification,
settlement mechanism, and typical liquidity — index futures providing
broad, cash-settled market exposure suited to portfolio-level views and
hedging, while stock futures provide concentrated, typically physically-
settled exposure suited to specific company theses. This sets up the
next lesson's treatment of hedging with futures — a practical
application drawing directly on both instrument types' distinct
characteristics.

## Quiz
1. How do index futures and stock futures differ in settlement?
   a) Both cash-settle identically  b) Index futures cash-settle; Indian stock futures typically physically settle  c) Both physically settle  d) Neither settles

2. Which provides more diversification?
   a) Stock futures  b) Index futures  c) Both are identical  d) Neither provides diversification

3. What should you consider before a stock futures position reaches expiry?
   a) Nothing  b) Plan to close or roll it to avoid unintended physical delivery  c) Only the tick size  d) Only the margin

4. Which instrument suits a broad, market-wide thesis?
   a) Stock futures  b) Index futures  c) Neither  d) Only options

5. True or False: All individual stock futures contracts are equally liquid as benchmark index futures.
   a) True
   b) False

6. **Scenario:** A trader holds a long Bank Nifty futures position into
   expiry without closing or rolling it. Separately, a different trader
   holds a long Reliance Industries stock futures position into expiry
   without closing or rolling it. What happens to each position at
   expiry?
   a) Both are cash-settled automatically, no further action needed
   b) The Bank Nifty position is cash-settled; the Reliance stock futures position typically triggers physical delivery of the underlying shares
   c) Both trigger physical delivery
   d) Both simply expire worthless regardless of price

## Answers
1. **b)** — Index futures cash-settle; Indian stock futures typically physically settle.
2. **b)** — Index futures.
3. **b)** — Plan to close or roll it to avoid unintended physical delivery.
4. **b)** — Index futures.
5. **b) False** — Liquidity varies considerably; benchmark index futures are typically far more liquid.
6. **b)** — Index futures (like Bank Nifty) cash-settle; Indian stock futures (like Reliance) typically physically settle, which is exactly why the unrolled Reliance position — not the Bank Nifty one — risks unintended physical delivery.
